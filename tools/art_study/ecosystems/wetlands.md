# Wetlands: fen, bog, carr

*Zones: the Drowned Fen, the Sunken Bog and the Drowned Village (Act I); the parasitic fen of Shog-Mire (Act III).*

## The real thing
| Kind | Fed by | What grows |
|---|---|---|
| **Fen** | Ground water | Reeds and sedges, with open water channels. |
| **Bog** | Rain | Acid ground: sphagnum moss in hummocks and hollows, peat building over millennia, few trees. |
| **Carr** | Wet ground | Wet woodland (alder, willow) on root-islands among black water. |

Everything turns on the water table. A few inches decide between reed, moss and tree.

## In Godmarrow
The Drowned Fen and the Sunken Bog hold the drowned and the forgotten: a Famine lies sunk to its jaw in the bog. In
Act III the fen becomes flesh: the heartbeat mud, the blood delta, the brood-banks, the amber grease (see
[the organic deep](organic_deep.md)).

## Species and elements
- **Reed beds and sedge tussocks.** In the lore they bend toward the god whatever the wind.
- **Sphagnum hummocks and black pools.**
- **Alder carr** on root-islands, and drowned trees.
- **Human traces:** peat cuttings, duckboards and causeways.

## Rules
- **The water-table map decides everything.** Each thing grows in its own band, from wettest to driest: open water,
  reed, sedge, moss, carr, then wood.
- **Pools** lie in the hollows.
- **Causeways** run where people crossed.

## Transitions
- **Into wood:** through carr.
- **Into moor:** through peat.
- **Into Act III's flesh fen:** the water reddens.
