# Cities and ruins

*Built places: Maren's camp, the Citadel of the Shattered Femur, Kettlewick Stilts, Bellrest Hearth, the Last Vigil.
Ruins everywhere: the Fallen Monastery, the Drowned Village, the Fallen Watchtower, Reliquary Avenue, barrows, crypts,
the Famines.*

## The real thing
What people build follows:
- **their land:** stone where there is stone, timber where there is wood, stilts over water;
- **their needs:** walls, wells, and roads that follow the ground.

When people leave, the land takes it back, in order:
1. The roofs go first, to decay or fire.
2. Then the floors.
3. Then the walls, from the top down, course by course, with rubble spilling outward.
4. Plants root in the joints: moss, then grass, then shrubs, then trees that split the walls.

Roads keep their line long after their surface has gone. Wells and cellars hold water.

## In Godmarrow
- **Towns** are islands of the living on the god's body.
- **Ruins** are its scars: the chapels where the god was worshipped; the Famines (god-bodies the height of hills, with
  ramps into their mouths); the pilgrim roads.
- **The painted look of stone:** the ruins study (`../painted_scene.py`) is the reference.

## Elements
- **Walls:** coursed masonry, broken course by course.
- **Pillars:** snapped at different heights, with fallen drums.
- **Floors:** flagstones, their joints mossed and lifting.
- **Spill:** rubble.
- **Furnishings:** graves and their carving, braziers.
- **Structure:** doorways, wells, timber frames, stilts, roofs in every state of collapse.

## Rules
A ruin is a building plus time:
1. Place the building whole.
2. Age it: the roof first, then the walls from the top, rubble outward.
3. Grow plants by how wet the joints are and how much light they get.

The surrounding ecosystem flows in by its own rules: moss and fern in wet shade, grass in light, saplings on the
rubble.

## Transitions
Any land into a ruin: the ecosystem's own species climbing the walls by its rules.
