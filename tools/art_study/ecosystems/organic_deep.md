# The organic deep

The world is the dead god's body, and the lore lays it out in depth:
- the Hide is the skin, where the towns are;
- the long white ridges of the far lands are its bones (Ossa);
- what runs in the rivers is what still runs in it;
- Shog-Mire is its flesh and blood;
- An-Vhar is its breath;
- the Descent goes into the body itself.

So every ecosystem carries a hidden variable: **depth into the god**. As it rises, more of the body comes through.

## The gradient, act by act

| Depth | Where | What comes through |
|---|---|---|
| Skin | Act I, the Hide | Almost nothing at the surface: warm ash over the cooling cheek, and black glass. Below, roots find marrow in the Root Deep, and the crypts' flagstones lift as something breathes beneath them. |
| Bone | Act II, Ossa | The bone itself is the ground: dust, plates, ribs, marrow seeping from cracks. |
| Flesh and blood | Act III, Shog-Mire | The fen's mud has a heartbeat; the water runs red (the Blood Delta); amber grease; brood-banks; weeping trees. |
| Breath | Act IV, An-Vhar | The chime-winds are breath, and glass forms where it froze. |
| The body | Act V, the Descent | A calcified highway, siphon vaults, cavities, valves, the cerebrum. No ecosystem is left that isn't the god. |

## How it is built
Depth is one more map the generator reads. As it rises, within any ecosystem:
- **Materials shift.** Litter darkens and turns greasy, water reddens, stone turns to bone, moss turns into a slick
  that breathes.
- **The god's own structures intrude.** Ribs push up through the ground, veins run under the mud, marrow runs along
  the roots.
- **Life thins and changes.** The fungi go first; then nothing grows but the body's own growths.
- **The light changes.** The body glows with its own light, never as a gimmick: purposeful and restrained, as in D2R.

Within a single zone, the deep places (a cave, a sinkhole, the Root Deep) carry more of the god than the surface does:
the same land, further in.

## Restraint
Hint, never explain; that is the lore's law. A root that bleeds once, a stone that is warm, a heartbeat felt in the
mud. The deeper the act, the more open it becomes.
