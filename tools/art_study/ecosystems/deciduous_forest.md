# Deciduous forest

*Zones: Fern Gully, the woods along the Pilgrim Road, and the groves across the Hide that clear into meadows
(Act I).*

## The real thing
A broadleaf wood that is younger or more disturbed than old growth: trees of more even age, a single main canopy, few
giants and far less dead wood.

**Seasons.**
- Light reaches the floor in spring, before the leaves open, so the floor flowers then.
- It's shaded through summer.
- In autumn the whole canopy falls as one bright litter.

**Layers.**
- **Canopy.**
- **Understorey:** shade-bearing small trees (hazel, hawthorn, holly).
- **Shrubs:** at the edges and in gaps.
- **Herbs:** ferns, bramble, spring flowers.
- **Ground:** moss on wood and stone.

Its edges are dense with shrubs and bramble, where light comes in from the side.

## In Godmarrow
These are the Hide's ordinary woods: thinner and younger, with the blight in them too but slighter. The litter is
brighter, because this year's leaves dominate. The groves open into meadows; the zone rules want 52–82% open ground,
with groves that clear into meadows. Fern Gully is a damp ravine wood: ferns everywhere, moss on every stone.

## Species and elements
- **Trees:** broadleaf trees, mostly middle-aged.
- **Understorey:** small trees, and holly-like evergreens (dark points in winter).
- **Edges:** bramble thickets.
- **Floor:**
  - bracken in drifts;
  - spring carpets of pale flowers in the shade (drawn as scatter).
- **Dead wood:** fewer logs, mostly classes 1–3, and stumps, cut or snapped.
- **Meadow:** grass where the grove opens (the meadow shader).

## Rules
- Canopy cover is set by the grove: dense in its heart, opening toward the meadow.
- Understorey grows under the canopy; shrubs and bramble on the lit edge.
- Bracken and ferns grow in the damp and the half-light.
- The meadow begins where the canopy ends.
- Dead wood follows the same fall rules as old growth, but rarer.

## Transitions
- **Into old growth:** giants appear and the dead wood thickens.
- **Into meadow and moor:** the edge runs from shrubs and bramble, to lone trees, to grass.
- **Into the gully:** ferns and moss thicken as the ground drops and wets.
