# Chapter 11: Northwest Coast forms

For the Red Shore's shore folk, whose culture is a fusion of Northwest Coast forms with Thai ones (the area page).
**Forms only** (Derek, 2026-10-08; MASTER_RULES section 8): the cedar plank, the post, the canoe, woven bark, the
bentwood box, the ovoid and the U-form, and carved human faces. Never animal crests, and no real nation's crests,
stories or ceremonies. The shore folk's meanings are their own (`docs/wiki/mythology/areas/the-red-shore.md`). The
chapter ends with recipes.

## 1. Cedar: the material under everything

- **Western red cedar** splits straight and clean along its grain, resists rot, and is light. Planks are split from
  standing trees or logs with wedges, then dressed with the adze.
- **The adze's mark** is the surface: rows of shallow scallops, each a thumb's width to a hand's, laid in lines along
  the plank. In a raking light the whole face is fine ripples; worn, the ridges soften and the grain stands proud.
- **Weathering:** new cedar is red-brown; weathered, it goes silver-grey, the grain raised, checks along it, green and
  black where the wet stays.

## 2. The plank house

- **A post-and-beam frame** independent of its skin: big posts at the corners and down the middle carry beams; the
  roof rafters lie on the beams. Houses ran from 30 to 400 feet long and 15 to 30 feet wide.
- **The skin:** split planks set upright in a trench (walls) and laid over the rafters (roof), tied to the frame with
  cord, so they can be taken down and moved. In the south, roof planks were adzed hollow and laid alternately concave
  and convex like tiles.
- **Often dug in:** the floor a step to six feet below the ground, with a bench of earth or planks round the walls.
- **A smoke hole** in the roof over the hearth; the roof planks there can be pushed aside.

## 3. Posts and faces

- **House posts** carry the beams inside; carved, they are figures in high relief standing under the beam.
- **Stacked faces:** a post carved as faces one above another, each fitting into the one below. For the shore folk:
  human faces only.
- **The face's forms:** a broad brow; the eye set in a large eye-socket ovoid with a smaller inner ovoid for the lid
  and pupil; prominent cheeks; nostrils flared; the mouth wide, lips shown as a raised band, often open. Carved with
  crisp planes that meet at sharp edges (the knife's facets), never soft.
- **Paint on the carving:** black for the primary lines (brows, eyes, lips), red for the secondary, a blue-green for
  the recesses; weathering takes the paint off the high places first.

## 4. Formline (the two-dimensional design)

- **The formline** is the continuous swelling and tapering line that outlines and joins every part; it is never even.
- **The ovoid:** a rounded rectangle, its top flatter and slightly concave, its bottom swelling. It is the main shape
  for heads, eyes and joints. The inner ovoid sits off-centre, toward the top.
- **The U-form:** a thick U whose arms taper to points; used to contour and to fill.
- **The split U:** a U with a V cut down its middle; used as a filler.
- **The rules:** symmetry about a central axis; primary formline in black, secondary in red, tertiary areas carved or
  painted blue-green; negative spaces are crescents and trigons (curved triangles) between the formlines.

## 5. Canoes

- **The dugout:** one cedar log hollowed and then spread by steaming, so its sides flare. A high, upswept bow, a lower
  stern, the gunwales curving up to both.
- Sizes from a 15-foot fishing canoe to 50 to 60-foot ones.
- For the Red Shore: the old canoes no one has touched in a hundred years, grey and split, lie upturned above the
  tide line (and were set on end in the palisade at the last).

## 6. Bentwood boxes

- **One plank** is kerfed (three grooves cut across it), steamed, and bent at the grooves into the four sides; the
  fourth corner is pegged or sewn with root; a bottom is pegged in, a lid fitted.
- **Painted** on the outside in formline: a large ovoid design on each long side, black and red on the cedar; the
  corners crisp, the lid's edge bevelled.
- For the shore folk: they hold the ashes of the dead, in the temple's wall niches (built, pass 11 of the temple).

## 7. Woven cedar bark

- Inner bark pulled in long strips, split and softened; plaited into mats, hats, baskets, capes.
- **The weave:** checker (one over one) or twill (two over two, a diagonal); strips a finger to two fingers wide;
  the edge bound; the colour a warm tan going grey.
- For the shore folk: mats on the hut floors and over the doors, the dead wrapped in them on the pyre.

## 8. The fusion, for the Red Shore

- **Tiered Thai roofs over cedar plank walls** (the area page's huts are hide; the temple is Thai; the fusion shows in
  the posts, the gables and the things people made).
- **Carved face-posts** at the hut doors, the palisade gate and the porch of the temple.
- **Ovoids among the gable's flames**, and the serpent rails carved in ovoids and flames together.
- **The colours together:** the Thai gilt and black lacquer beside the formline's black, red ochre and blue-green.

## 9. Recipes

- **A split cedar plank (3D road):** a long box with a slightly wavy split face; the adze's scallops as real shallow
  grooves across it, a few hundredths of a yard deep, in rows; checks along the grain at the ends; paint the weathered
  silver-grey ramp with the grain lines raised, green-black where the wet stays at the foot.
- **A stacked face-post:** a cylinder of cedar (0.4 to 0.6 yd across) carved into two to four faces, each face built
  of real forms: a brow ridge, two eye-socket ovoids with raised inner ovoids, cheek planes, a nose wedge, a mouth
  band; capped with a small tiered roof. Paint black, red and blue-green, worn off the high places.
- **An ovoid in paint (any flat face):** a superellipse with a flattened, slightly concave top, its formline a band
  that swells at the sides and narrows at the corners, an inner ovoid off-centre toward the top, crescents of the
  ground colour between (`paint3d.box_paint` is the first, simplest version).
- **A canoe:** a hull lofted from cross-sections (flat-bottomed U amidships, narrowing V toward the ends), the bow
  swept up, the gunwale a line rising to both ends; adze marks inside; upturned and grey for the dead canoes.
- **Woven bark:** paint as a checker or twill of strips one or two pixels wide at game scale, warm tan going grey, the
  edge a bound band; as real form only in the folds of a mat.
