# Chapter 1: what detail and nuance are, in the real world (2026-10-07; Derek: "review what it means to detail and nuance things, make it like the real world"; "the gate should have extruded iron bars"; "the stones and tiles just look awful, you really need to spend a lot of time on that")

### What detail is
Detail is history. Each mark in a real surface answers "what made this?": feet, rain, frost, salt, a blow, rot, the
flesh pushing up. A mark without a cause is noise, and noise is what my stones and teeth were full of.
- **Three scales, each readable at its own distance:**
  1. the form, which is the silhouette and the big planes;
  2. the features, 10 to 30% of the object's size: a dished hollow, a spalled scoop, a crack, a proud edge;
  3. the texture: grain and pores.
  At the game's zoom the features carry the read. Texture is only a quiet tooth and a flicker on lit edges.
- **Nuance is correlated variation, not random variation.** Real properties change together for a reason. Where water
  sits, stone is darker, smoother, greener and has a tide mark; where feet go, it is paler, dished and polished; at
  a base, rising damp darkens a band and salt eats pockets. One cause drives several properties at once, so the
  surface reads as true.
- **Edges hold the information and interiors stay calm.** A slab's story is told at its arrises (chips, rounding,
  a proud lip catching light, the shadow line under it). Its middle is broad and quiet.
- **Nothing the same twice:** no repeated sizes, spacing, line widths or line values. Every joint, crack and stain
  has its own width and darkness, set by its cause.
- **Form before texture, always.** Every feature above is a shape in the height (a dish, a lip, a scoop) lit
  through its normal. It is never a painted darker patch.

### Old paving flags (sandstone, limestone)
- **Each slab** comes from its own bed: a narrow colour family (buff, grey, iron-rusted bands), bedding lines
  running across it, and a riven surface on some. Sizes vary: random rectangular paving, some long and some near
  square.
- **Settling:** each slab sinks its own way. Where two meet, one edge stands proud of the next, with a lit lip on
  the proud stone and a shadow line on the low one. That height difference is the strongest read in a real old
  pavement.
- **Wear:** dished centres along the paths feet took, paler, smoother and faintly polished; arrises rounded on the
  path and sharp off it.
- **Cracks:** across the short span, from a point load (here, the flesh heaving up from beneath). The two pieces
  are slightly offset in height, the crack edges spall, and the fresh stone inside is paler.
- **Spalling:** surface layers flake off in shallow scalloped hollows with sharp rims and paler stone inside.
- **Water:** it pools in the dishes and leaves tide marks and darker, smoother stone.
- **Joints:** dirt-filled, widest where stones moved, darkest at the bottom. Here the god's flesh rises through
  them first.
- **Others:** tool marks (parallel diagonal tooling) survive on stones off the path; grime gathers in hollows and
  at low edges; rare crustose lichen rosettes sit on the driest, highest stones (ash-grey on the Moor).

### Weathered column stone
- Each drum came from a different block and is a slightly different tone, and drums are shifted off true by old
  quakes.
- Fluting arrises are worn round and broken in bites.
- Under ledges, rain run-off leaves black crust in the sheltered places and washes the exposed ones pale.
- Salt rises at the base: a dark damp band, and honeycomb pockets eaten into the stone.
- Where drums broke apart, the dowel and cramp holes show, stained with rust.
- A break surface is rough and conchoidal, and paler where fresh.

### Teeth (real anatomy)
- **Enamel is translucent:** grey-blue at the thin edges and tip, whiter in the thick body, and yellow toward the
  neck where the dentin shows through.
- **Surface:**
  - fine horizontal perikymata, seen only in raking light;
  - a central ridge and lobes on the face;
  - attrition facets: flat, polished planes with sharp borders where teeth ground on each other, with the dentin
    cupped below the enamel rim;
  - vertical craze lines that catch light faintly.
- **The neck:** where the gloss stops, the root begins (the cemento-enamel junction). Below it lies matte, yellow,
  rough dentin, exposed where the gum receded. Calculus crusts the gumline: yellow-brown, rough, and dark green-black
  lower down.
- **A snapped tooth shows its inside:**
  - a thin enamel shell;
  - the dentin in growth rings, finely striated toward the centre;
  - the pulp canal as a dark hole in the middle;
  - conchoidal break faces.
- Stains gather at the neck, in pits and in the grooves. The gum is scalloped, stippled and swollen.

### Wrought iron (the gate)
- Bars are forged, square or round, slightly irregular and never ruler-straight. Each bar is a real extruded form
  with its own light and shadow and its own cast shadow on what lies behind it.
- **Joints:** crossings are punched through or collared and riveted. Bars end in spear-point finials, and massive
  pintle hinges are leaded into the stone.
- **Corrosion is layered:**
  - dark brown-black scale;
  - orange-red flaking bloom where water sits (tops of rails, joints, the bottom);
  - wrought iron delaminates along its fibres, so corroded bars look like split wood grain;
  - pitting;
  - rust streaks run down onto the stone beneath every joint.
- Iron is dark and of low albedo, with a dull, broad sheen.

### Ash (the Moor)
- **Wind:**
  - fine, matte powder;
  - small ripples crossing the wind;
  - drift tails in the lee of every obstacle, scour hollows on the windward side.
- **Wet ash:** crusts darker and cracks into polygons with curled, lit rims.
- **Clasts:** black vesicular cinders, sparse and sized in a range.
- **The lore:** black glass where a Husk's blood ran into the ash, and the ash warm to the hand.

### How I will build them now
Height first, per feature, each feature caused. The light comes through the normals of that height, so every dish,
lip, scoop, crack and spall is lit by the moon and the lantern like the real thing. Colour variation is then set by
the same causes (wet, wear, damp, sun), and texture comes last and quiet.
