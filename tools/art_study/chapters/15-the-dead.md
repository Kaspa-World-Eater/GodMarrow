# Chapter 15: the dead (one body for every place) (2026-10-09)

Derek, on the opening cave in the game: "Bodies look bad." On the beach: "Don't forget the bodies in the water."
Earlier: "bodies embedded into the shoreline. Rotting corpses." The brief is `tools/landkit/passes/the_dead.md`: one
shared body, built true size on the 3D road (`tools/landkit3d/body3d.py`, to be written), on the pilgrim's
proportions, in the pilgrim's hooded cloak and rope belt, used by the cave's pool, the cove's strand and swash, and
every later dead.

Researched from forensic pathology, anthropometric tables, clinical joint ranges and art history (sources at the
end); our own reasoning is marked. Factual, not gratuitous: the dead are told by weight and shape, and the faces
mostly stay hidden ("Don't turn them over").

## 0. Why the stand-ins failed

- **A sphere torso and tube limbs read as puppets** (reports 07 and 08). A real trunk is three masses (the ribcage
  barrel, the belly, the pelvis) hung on a curved spine, with a shoulder girdle sitting on top. None of it is round.
- **The limbs floated or sank in evenly,** and the joints were posed, not dropped. A dead limb rests on its highest
  supports and flattens where it presses; every joint goes as far as gravity and the ground allow, and no further.
- **The bones went "accordion"** when laid point by point. A bone is rigid and straight; only the joints bend.
- **One value across the body.** Even a true form reads flat without the five tones (MASTER_RULES 0.5c).

## 1. How a dead body lies

### 1a. No tone: what gravity does

- **Primary flaccidity:** at death every muscle relaxes, the face and eyelids first, then the fingers, the upper body,
  the lower. The jaw falls open. **Rigor** then locks the pose for a time (from some hours to a day or more), and
  **secondary flaccidity** follows: the muscles let go for good.
- **Our dead are past rigor,** days or longer in the pool and on the strand: fully slack, every joint settled.
- **The rule for the builder:** a slack body is a chain of rigid bones joined by free hinges with limits. Each part
  falls until it is held, either by the ground, by another part of the body, or by the end of a joint's range.

### 1b. Joint ranges: the limits the settle must respect

Typical clinical ranges of motion for an adult (passive range in a slack body is a little larger; never go past these):

| Joint | Range |
|---|---|
| Neck | turn 80° each way; bend forward 50°, back 60°; tilt sideways 45° |
| Jaw | slack open 1 to 3 cm at the teeth; never past about 4.5 cm |
| Shoulder | forward 180°, back 60°; out to the side 180°; across the body 30 to 40° |
| Elbow | 0 (straight) to 145° |
| Forearm | turn palm down 80°, palm up 80° |
| Wrist | bend forward 80°, back 70°; side to side 20 to 30° |
| Hip | forward 120° (knee bent), back 20 to 30°; out 45°, in 30°; turn in 40°, out 50° |
| Knee | 0 to 135° (a slack knee held only at the heel can sag a few degrees past straight) |
| Ankle | foot up 20°, foot down 50°; roll in 30°, out 20° |
| Spine, whole | forward 80°, back 25°, sideways 35° each way, twist 45° each way |

### 1c. Contact: where it presses, it flattens

- **Soft parts spread on what holds them.** The calves, the buttocks, the backs of the thighs, the cheek, the belly
  and the chest all flatten on the contact side and bulge out to the sides. The bone inside stays where it is.
- **Our working numbers** (reasoning, not a source): a limb's section loses 20 to 30% of its depth on the contact side
  and widens 10 to 15%; the belly and buttocks lose up to 40% and spread more.
- **Contact pallor.** Livor mortis (section 3, fresh) settles in the low parts, but not where they press: the capillaries
  there are squeezed shut, so the contact patches stay pale and can even print the texture of what they lay on (cloth
  weave, a belt, sand ripples). Colour and form tell gravity together.
- **The darkest line on the body is the contact line,** the occlusion where it meets the ground, never a drawn edge.

### 1d. The hands, the feet and the face

- **The dead hand half curls.** At rest the fingers fall into a cascade of 70 to 90° total flexion, least in the index
  and most in the little finger. Joint by joint, our numbers: knuckle (MCP) 20 to 40°, middle (PIP) 30 to 60°, tip
  (DIP) 10 to 20°, all increasing from index to little. The thumb falls against the side of the index.
- **The wrist decides the hand:** dropped, it curls the fingers more (tenodesis); bent back, it opens them. Hanging off
  an edge, the hand curls tight; palm-down on flat ground, the knuckles rise a little and the fingertips touch.
- **The feet fall.** On the back, the feet roll out 45 to 60° from upright and point down 20 to 30°. Face down, the
  ankle points the foot (40 to 50° down) so the top of the foot lies on the ground, or on soft ground the toes press
  in with the heel up.
- **The face.** The jaw drops 1 to 3 cm; the eyelids sit part open; the head rolls to the low side. Face down, the
  head turns to one side and the cheek takes the weight (neck turned 60 to 80°), unless the ground lets the face press
  straight in on the forehead and nose (soft sand, a hollow, the pool's bottom).

### 1e. The poses, by numbers

- **Face down (prone), the commonest on the strand.** Contacts: one cheek or the forehead, the chest, the front of the
  pelvis, the knees, the tops of the feet; the belly sags between. Arms along the sides, palms up, elbows 5 to 20°; or
  one or both up by the head, shoulder 90 to 150° forward, elbow 70 to 110°. Legs apart 15 to 30°, one knee often bent
  20 to 60° with the hip turned out. The shoulders roll forward; the shoulder blades stand out under the cloth.
- **On the back (supine).** Contacts: the back of the skull, the shoulder blades, the sacrum and buttocks, the calves,
  the heels; the lower back bridges a few cm off the ground. Head rolled 30 to 70° to one side, jaw open. Arms out 10
  to 70°, palms up or turned in, elbows 10 to 30°. Feet rolled out 45 to 60°. The belly flat or hollow below the rib
  margin (fresh), or domed (bloat).
- **On the side, half curled.** Contacts: the lower shoulder, the side of the ribcage, the lower hip's point, the lower
  knee, the lower ankle. The upper arm falls forward across the chest to the ground; the upper knee falls forward past
  the lower (hip 40 to 70°, knee 50 to 90°). Spine curled 20 to 40°; the head tips until the ear or temple touches. A
  side lie tends to roll on to face down unless the upper knee or arm props it.
- **The drowned, in the water (the pool, the swash's deeper edge).** Forensic sources agree: **face down, the head
  hanging lowest.** The chest and belly are the most buoyant; the heavy arms and legs hang down, and **the hands,
  forearms, knees and feet** drag along the bottom in a current. Injuries cluster on the forehead and face, the backs of
  the hands and the knuckles, the knees and the tops of the toes; lividity settles in the face. A drowned body sinks
  first and rises only when decay gas lifts it (days to weeks, faster in warm water). Pose: shoulders 60 to 90° forward,
  the arms hanging down and forward, elbows 20 to 40°, wrists dropped, hands curled, palms facing back; hips 20 to 40°,
  knees 30 to 60°, feet pointing down. The back, the shoulder blades and the back of the hood break the surface.
- **The drowned, grounded (the shallows, the pool's edge, the wet foreshore).** The same pose let down: the forehead,
  the backs of the hands, the knees and the toe tops touch first, and the trunk settles onto them. The back is the
  highest form, the cloak plastered on it.
- **Half sunk (sand or blood).** Any settled pose with its low side buried (section 3, sand and the swash); the high
  forms (a shoulder blade, a buttock, a heel, the back of the head) stand clear.
- **Draped over a stump or rock (the cave).** The trunk folds over the high point at the belly or chest; the head and
  arms hang one side (shoulders past 120°, the hands curled, the fingertips perhaps touching the blood), the legs the
  other with the knees bent to the ground. The spine bends over the support to its forward limit; the rest hangs.

### 2e. The trunk's forms

- **The ribcage is an egg-shaped barrel,** narrow at the top (the first ribs only about 12 cm across), widest at the
  eighth rib, wider than it is deep (about 1.3 : 1). The back is flatter, the front fuller. The ribs slope down and
  forward; the lowest point of the cage is at the sides. At the front-bottom the **costal arch** opens in an inverted
  V of 70 to 90°, and the belly sits in that opening.
- **The spine is an S:** forward curve at the neck, a backward curve (about 30 to 40°) through the chest, a forward
  curve (40 to 60°) in the lower back. The trunk's masses hang on it.
- **The shoulder girdle sits on the cage like a yoke:** the two collarbones (about 15 cm each, gently S-shaped) run
  from the notch at the top of the breastbone out and slightly up to the shoulder points; the shoulder blades
  (triangles about 15 cm wide by 17 tall) lie on the back of the cage and slide on it.
- **The belly** is a soft sac between the rib arch and the pelvis, with no bone in front. It is what changes most by
  state and by pose.
- **The pelvis is a bowl tilted forward** about 10 to 15°, its rim (the iliac crests, about 28 cm across the bone)
  standing at the waist's sides, the hip joints low and forward of the sacrum.

## 3. The stages, told by form as well as colour

Forensic sources describe five stages: fresh, bloat, active decay, advanced decay, dry or skeletal. Timings swing
widely with heat and water (cold water can hold a body for months; warm water can bare it in a week), so the game uses
the stages as a look, never a clock. Chapter 3 section 3 already holds the rot colours; this adds the form.

### Fresh
- **Form:** the slack, settled body of section 1, flattened at every contact. **Colour:** pallor. Livor mortis
  settles in the low parts as a purple-red (#6A3848 to #4A2638), with pale contact patches inside it where the body
  presses. Face down: the face, the front of the chest and thighs, the forearms. Drowned: the face and head most.
- **Immersion (any body in liquid, drowned or not):** within hours the palms, fingertips, soles and toes wrinkle and go
  pale grey-white ("washerwoman's skin", #D8D0C4). Over the first week the skin of the hands and feet loosens; from the
  second week it can come away whole, like a glove (degloving).

### Bloat
- **Form, as sources describe it:** gut bacteria make gas, which swells the belly first, then the face and limbs. The
  belly distended and taut; the face swollen past recognition, the lips, eyelids and tongue inflated, the tongue
  pushing out, the eyes bulging; the skin tight and shiny.
- **Our working numbers** (no source gives volumes; grade by eye): belly depth +60 to 100%, so it rises above the rib
  arch into a dome; chest +15 to 25%; neck and face +30 to 50% in width, the jawline gone into the neck; limbs +10 to
  20% in girth, hands and feet puffed like gloves.
- **The rope belt sinks into the swollen waist** as a deep groove, the cloth bulging either side. One detail that
  tells bloat without gore.
- **Colour:** green-grey starting at the lower right belly (#6E7A4A to #4E5A36); **marbling**, the veins outlined in
  branching green-black and purple (#2E3A2A to #3A2E40); skin slipping in sheets to a glossy pink-red beneath; blisters.
- **Buoyancy:** this is when a sunk body rises; in the pool the bloated dead float, the back riding highest.

### Active decay
- **Form:** the gas escapes and **the body collapses.** The belly sinks below the rib arch into a hollow; the skin,
  stretched by the bloat, hangs and **splits along its stretch lines,** shows the ribs as ridges under it and, at the
  splits, as bone. The face sinks: the eye sockets hollow, the nose's soft parts collapse, the lips draw back from the
  teeth. Slack skin drapes over the bones like wet cloth.
- **Colour:** dark, wet, black-green-brown (#2A2618 to #4A3E2C), glossy where wet. **Keep it to form:** ribs ridged
  under skin, a split showing bone, the sunken face under the hood; no open organs, no pooling for its own sake.

### Advanced decay, adipocere and dry
- **On land:** what is left dries to leather, dark brown to tan (#5A4030), shrunk tight over the bones, cracking.
- **In cold, wet, airless places** (the pool's depths, buried wet sand) the fat can turn to **adipocere**: a grey-white
  to tan waxy crust (#C8C2B0 to #A89A78), soft and greasy at first, hardening and going brittle. It **keeps the body's
  shape** long after the flesh would have gone. A good state for the cove: the shape of the pilgrim kept in grey wax.

### Skeletal
- **The same skeleton,** with nothing on it; the cloth holds the bones together longer than flesh does.
- **The order things come apart:** the jaw and the head go first everywhere. In water the extremities go next, from
  the far end in: finger and toe bones, then hands and feet. The spine, ribs and pelvis hold together longest.
- **So a skeleton on the strand:** the skull rolled loose inside the hood, or out of it; the jaw apart from the skull;
  the hand and foot bones scattered or gone (the sleeves and foot wrappings may hold them); the spine and pelvis
  still in line inside the cloak.
- **The ribcage collapses:** with the cartilage gone the ribs splay and flatten (on the back) or fold under the spine
  (face down), and the breastbone drops in. The barrel is gone.
- **Bone colour on our shore:** stained by blood and sand, never clean white (6j).

### Sand and the swash (our reasoning, from how obstacles scour)
- **The swash scours and buries.** Water running round an obstacle digs a scour hollow on the side it comes from and
  drops sand in the lee. A body in the swash sits in its own shallow scour, sand banked on the up-beach side.
- **Bodies sink a few cm into wet sand** as the swash liquefies it; over tides the low side buries to a third or half
  its depth, then more. Sand packs the cloth's folds, the hands' curls and the hood's mouth.
- **The tide-line stain** (chapter 13): maroon film drying to brown-black crust below the high-tide line; dry and
  grey with sand above it.

## 4. Wet cloth on a body

- **Wet cloth is heavy and limp:** it loses its stiffness, makes fewer, smaller, sharper folds, and surface tension
  sticks it to what it touches.
- **Plastered on the high forms.** Where the body rises (the shoulder blades, the back of the skull under the hood, the
  buttocks, the calves, the knees, the heels, the bloated belly), the cloth lies flat on it like a skin. The form
  reads through. This is the Greek sculptors' "wet drapery": anatomy read right through the cloth, the folds only where
  it leaves the body.
- **Bridging and pooling in the hollows.** Between high forms the cloth either bridges in a taut sheet (across the
  small of the back on a prone body, between the legs) or sags and pools into the hollow (the waist, the crook of an
  elbow, between the shoulder blades), with fine radial creases running from each high point.
- **On the ground** the hem spreads flat, outlined by a wet meniscus, the folds collapsed into low ridges. In liquid a
  cloak floats out from the body in a few broad lobes.
- **The hood:** wet, it collapses onto the skull and takes its shape; its opening sags shut over a face-down head, the
  edge lying in the blood or the sand. On a back, the hood flattens under the head like a pillow, its rim framing the
  face in shadow. Air caught under a fresh-drowned hood or back can swell it into a brief dome (reasoning).
- **The rope belt** gathers the cloth at the waist; wet, the rope darkens and its twist sharpens.
- **Tears:** woven wool tears along the grain in straight runs with a frayed fringe, and wears through where it rubs:
  the elbows, the knees, the hem. The drowned drag on their foreheads, knuckles, knees and toes, so the cloth wears
  through there first.

## 5. How painters made the dead read

### Géricault, the Raft of the Medusa and its studies
- He studied the dead in the morgue and the hospital, with "Head of a Drowned Man" among the studies. The *Study of
  Feet and Hands* (Musée Fabre) lays the parts on brown drapery like a still life, each turned by side light.
- **The finished painting puts the dead at the base.** The composition is two pyramids; the foreground is a long
  horizontal of dead and dying bodies, the base the living rise from. Bodies slide off the raft's edge into the sea.
- **The father and the dead son:** the son's limp, alabaster body lies extended along the raft's edge, held by the
  father's hand, the legs trailing toward the water. Pallor against dark; the weight shown by what holds it and what
  it spills over.
- **For us:** the dead as a low, long mass at the bottom of the frame; a body over an edge (our stumps, the berm) to
  show weight; pale flesh turned by one strong light against dark ground.

### Holbein, The Body of the Dead Christ in the Tomb
- A canvas 30.5 cm tall and 2 m long: **a niche**, the body seen full length from the side, at eye level, pressed
  under a low lid. The format itself is the weight.
- The body is long and emaciated, the eyes and mouth open, the hair hanging over the slab's edge, a hand and its
  middle finger reaching over the edge toward us. The flesh greenish, the hands and feet purpling: the start of decay,
  told plainly.
- **For us:** a long, low, side-on read; the hair and one hand breaking over the support's edge, the one detail that
  says "no tone"; colour by state, quietly.

### Mantegna, the Lamentation over the Dead Christ
- Seen **from the feet**, the body running straight away from us in steep foreshortening, on a stone slab. Mantegna
  **shrank the feet** so they would not hide the body; strict perspective was bent for the read.
- A near-monochrome of dark browns; the shroud's sharp folds cling to the legs and follow the form.
- **For us:** at our 30° camera, a body lying toward or away from the camera foreshortens; keep the near
  end's silhouette from hiding the trunk (turn the body off the camera axis 20 to 60° by default). Drapery that clings
  and follows the form tells the volume beneath.

### What makes weight and lifelessness read (from all three, and from the forensic facts)
1. **Contact:** flattening where it presses, the darkest occlusion where it meets the ground, pale pressure patches.
2. **Everything lower than it would hold in life:** the head rolled, the jaw open, the shoulders fallen, the hands
   below the wrists, the feet rolled out.
3. **Limp extremities:** the hand in its cascade, hanging over an edge; one detail of slackness sells the whole body.
4. **No tension in the silhouette:** no straight-locked joint held off the ground, no symmetry, no limb in the air
   that is not hanging.
5. **The turn of form in low light:** the five tones (6j), the contact darkest of all.
6. **Low and long in the frame,** read side-on or at an angle, never end-on.

## 6. Recipes for `tools/landkit3d/body3d.py`

One builder: `body(name, pose, state, where)`. The same skeleton drives every pose and state. Real geometry, true
size, then the area's painter.

### 6a. The skeleton (the data everything hangs on)
- Joints in metres from sections 2a and 2b: pelvis root, lumbar, chest, neck base, head; collarbone, shoulder, elbow,
  wrist; hip, knee, ankle, ball of the foot; fingers of three joints, the thumb of two plus its metacarpal; the toes one
  hinged block, the big toe its own.
- **Bones are rigid segments,** each a transform (start joint, direction, fixed length); never sampled point by point.
- The spine: five links (pelvis, lumbar, lower chest, upper chest, neck) carrying the S-curve as rest angles, each
  link limited so the whole spine stays within section 1b.
- Proportion jitter per body: +/-3% height, +/-5% widths, +/-8% girths, so no two dead are twins (the pilgrim's face
  is the same; the shapes differ a little).

### 6b. The trunk (never a sphere; the forms of section 2e)
- **Ribcage:** a barrel lofted through 6 to 8 sections, first rib to eleventh, widest at the eighth (bone about 28 x
  20 cm, flesh about 32 x 26), back flattened, the costal arch cut as an inverted V of 75 to 85°. The ribs are raised
  bands under the skin mesh (they show in the thin, rotted and bone states).
- **Girdle, belly, pelvis:** collarbone cylinders, shoulder-blade plates on the barrel, deltoid caps (bideltoid
  52 cm); the belly a sac whose front is a free surface the state sets (hollow, flat, domed); the pelvis a bowl tilted
  forward 12°, rim 28 cm, hip sockets 17 to 18 cm apart, the buttocks two soft masses behind.
- **Join them** with smooth unions (signed-distance smooth-min, a 2 to 4 cm blend, then remesh): one skin, the three
  masses still readable.

### 6c. The limbs: straight between joints, settled at the joints
- Each limb segment is a loft of the elliptical sections in section 2d along its straight bone, with the muscle
  bellies placed (the calf a third down, the forearm's bulk near the elbow), never a constant tube.
- Joints are blends (smooth-min of the two segments plus a small knuckle mass: the elbow's point, the kneecap, the
  ankle bones), so they bend without pinching.
- **Settle on the upper hull:** a straight bone across uneven ground rests on the highest points beneath it. For each
  segment, sample the support height along its length and lay the bone on the upper convex hull of that profile. It
  bridges hollows; it never bends into them.

### 6d. Hands and feet, blocked with their digits
- **Hand** (19.4 cm): palm 10.8 cm, a slightly cupped plate 9 cm wide; fingers with phalanges of roughly 1 : 0.62 :
  0.45 from knuckle to tip (middle finger total about 9 cm); thumb from a metacarpal set 30 to 40° off the palm.
  Pose them with the cascade of section 1d.
- **Foot** (27.4 cm, 10.5 wide): the heel a rounded block behind the ankle (about a quarter of the length), the arch,
  the ball, the toes as one block with the big toe split off.
- **Below about 60 px of body length on screen, fingers merge** into a mitten with one notch for the thumb (our
  reasoning; test it on the proof sheet).

### 6e. Head, neck and hood
- Skull: an egg 20.5 x 15.8 x 23.4 cm with the brow, cheekbones and jaw as forms; the jaw on its own hinge, dropping
  1 to 3 cm (and coming away in `bone`). Neck: 12 x 11 cm, from the chest's top link to the skull's base, angled
  forward.
- The face is rarely seen ("Don't turn them over"): give it true form for the supine and side poses, but let the hood
  and the ground take it in most.

### 6f. The settle (how a pose becomes a lying body)
1. **Seed:** place the pelvis root at the pose's height and orientation (yaw from `where`, plus jitter).
2. **Trunk first:** lower the trunk until its masses touch the support; let the spine links rotate within limits so
   the chest and the pelvis each find contact (the lumbar bridges on a back, the belly sags on a front).
3. **Out along each chain:** shoulder, then elbow, then wrist, then fingers; hip, then knee, then ankle, then toes.
   Rotate each segment down about its parent joint until its far end meets the support (the upper hull, 6c) or the
   joint hits its limit (section 1b). A segment held off the ground by its limit hangs straight down from there.
4. **Body on body:** the support is the ground plus every body already placed plus stumps and rocks, so the dead pile
   and drape on each other.
5. **Flatten the contacts** (section 1c): push the flesh mesh up to the support within 2 cm of it, and spread it
   sideways by about a third of the push, so volume is kept.
6. **Record the contact patches:** they feed the contact pallor, the occlusion and the blood or sand edge.

### 6g. Poses (each a seed plus joint targets; the settle does the rest)
| Pose | Seed |
|---|---|
| `prone` | face down; head turned 60 to 80°; arms by the sides palms up, or one up by the head (shoulder 120°, elbow 90°); one knee bent 30 to 60° |
| `prone_reach` | face down, both arms forward past the head (the drowned, beached): shoulders 150 to 170°, backs of the hands on the ground, forehead down |
| `drowned_float` | face down in liquid: section 1e's hanging pose; the back at the surface line |
| `drowned_grounded` | the same, let down: forehead, backs of the hands, knees, toe tops on the bottom |
| `side_curled` | on one side; spine curled 30°; upper arm and upper knee fallen forward to the ground |
| `supine_arms_out` | on the back; head rolled 40 to 70°; arms out 30 to 70°, palms up; feet rolled out 50° |
| `half_sunk` | any of the above with a burial depth (sand or blood) and a scour on the seaward side |
| `draped` | trunk over a stump or rock at the belly; head and arms hanging one side, knees down the other |
| `heap` | two or three bodies settled in turn onto each other (the cave's pool) |

### 6h. States (shape changes from the same skeleton)
| State | Shape |
|---|---|
| `fresh` | section 2 girths (lean end); contacts flattened |
| `bloat` | belly +60 to 100% depth (a dome over the rib arch), chest +20%, face and neck +40%, limbs +15%, hands and feet puffed; the belt cut in as a groove; skin taut, the fine forms (knuckles, collarbones) lost |
| `rot` | belly sunk below the rib arch; girths -20 to -35%; skin draped over the bones; ribs as ridges; a few splits along the bloat's stretch lines showing bone; eye sockets hollow, lips drawn off the teeth; flattened and spread on the ground |
| `wax` | the fresh or bloat form kept, surface softened and crusted, edges crumbled (the adipocere of the cold pool) |
| `bone` | skeleton only, in the cloak; skull and jaw apart; hands and feet scattered or missing; ribs collapsed and splayed; spine and pelvis in line |

Washerwoman's skin is a modifier on any state in liquid: the hands and feet wrinkled and pale; in `rot`, the hand's
skin slipped off as a glove lying beside it.

### 6i. The cloak and belt
- A cloth sim (or a shrink-wrap offset of 0.5 to 1.5 cm on the body plus pinned loose panels) settled after the body:
  plastered over the high forms, bridged or pooled in the hollows, the hem spread on the ground; the hood shrunk onto
  the skull.
- Wear and tears where the body dragged and at the hem, along the weave, frayed. In `bone`, the cloak holds the
  shape: a collapsed sack following the spine and pelvis.

### 6j. Paint: the five tones on every limb (MASTER_RULES 0.5c)
- **Light side** toward the area's key (the overcast's bright side, a fire); **halftone**; **core shadow**, a band along
  every limb and round the barrel of the ribs where the form turns away; **reflected light** from the wet ground lifting
  the underside of the arms, the belly and the jaw; **cast shadow and contact**, darkest at the contact line.
- **Skin ramps by state**, hue-shifted, 4 to 6 tones each (starting points, before the light):
  - fresh: pallor #C9BCAE, #A89A8E, #7A6E68, #4E4446; livor #6A3848 to #4A2638 in the low parts, contact pallor pale
    inside it;
  - bloat: #8E9070, #6E7A4A, #4E5A36, #2E3A2A; marbling lines and slipped glossy patches (#A85A50);
  - rot: #5A4A36, #4A3E2C, #2A2618, near-black wet darks;
  - wax: #C8C2B0, #A89A78, #6E6450;
  - bone: #B8AD94, #8A7A60, #5A4C3C, #3A2E24.
- **The cloak:** wet black wool, its lights a dull grey sheen (the overcast mirrored on the plastered high forms), its
  darks in the pooled folds; dry and grey-dusted above the tide line.
- **Detail where it counts:** the hand, the hood's edge, the belt, the contact line. The shade kept quiet.

### 6k. Contact with the blood and the sand
- In blood: the surface meets the body in a meniscus, a thin lit lip against the skin or cloth; the body below it
  hidden (blood is opaque past a millimetre, chapter 13 section 6); a dark stain climbing the cloth above the line.
- In sand: the scour hollow seaward, the bank up-beach, sand packed in every hollow of the cloth and hand, the
  tide-line crust below the high water.

### 6l. What to avoid
- **Spheres and tubes,** constant sections, symmetrical poses, limbs held in the air, a body that floats over the
  ground or sinks into it evenly.
- **Bending a bone** to meet the ground. Bones are straight; joints and soft flesh do the fitting.
- **One value across a body.** Every limb turned with the five tones.
- **Gore for its own sake:** no open bellies, no organs, no pooled fluids as spectacle. Form tells the state: the dome,
  the sunken belly, the ridged ribs, the bone.
- **Anything eating the dead.** No scavengers, no gnawing marks, no birds, no crabs, no fish (the no-animals law).
  Decay alone works on them.
- **End-on placement** at the camera, which hides the trunk behind the feet or the head (Mantegna had to cheat).
- **Twins:** every body jittered in proportion, pose and state.
