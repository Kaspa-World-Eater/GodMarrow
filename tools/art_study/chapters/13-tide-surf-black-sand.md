# Chapter 13: tide, surf, foam and black sand (the Red Water shore) (2026-10-09)

Derek, grading the Red Water D: "Doesn't look real enough, need a study in the tide, oceans, water, black sand, the
details are lacking. It's very plain." Earlier: "Those curvy lines look ridiculous too, not realistic at all."

The full research, with every quote, source and what could not be verified, is
`tools/art_study/private/ocean_research.md`. This chapter is what we build from. Our scale: 5 cm a pixel (1 m = 20 px),
the camera 30 degrees down.

## 0. Why the beach failed

- **The surf was a wide band of parallel white lines.** That is the "curvy lines" look, and it is physically wrong for a
  steep black-sand beach.
- **The sea, the wet sand and the dry sand were each one flat colour,** with no state, no gloss and no reflection.
- **Nothing on it had the form of the real thing:** no berm, no cusps, no single heavy break, no swash front.

## 1. The surf: one heavy break, close in

- **What breaks.** Breaker type follows the Iribarren number (slope over the square root of wave steepness). A steep
  1:8 coarse black-sand face under long 12 to 16 s swell gives about 1.3 to 2.2. That means a single plunging or
  collapsing shore-break close in, then a bore straight up the face.
  - Reynisfjara's waves "only break maybe once or twice when they're coming up to the shore", against several times on
    the flatter beach next door.
- **The breaker is geometry, the pilgrim's height:** 1.5 to 2.5 m, which is 30 to 50 px.
  - The face is steeper than the back.
  - The lip is a real overhang, its underside in self-shadow.
  - The trough at its foot is the darkest water.
  - No crest is sharper than 120 degrees (McCowan's limit).
- **Breaks peel along the crest** from a start point (a stack, a headland, a cusp horn). At any instant a crest is part
  unbroken, part curling, part white. This one fact kills the stripe look.
- **Behind the break:** only one or two long swells, varying in height through groups of 5 to 10. They bend round the
  stacks and headlands and converge on them, so the heaviest break is there.
- **The sneaker wave:** two and a half times its peers, every 10 to 30 minutes, running far past the normal swash.
  World-building, no story.

## 2. The swash: ballistic, lobed, sinking in

- **The swash front is a real step,** 1 to 3 px with a lit lip. It moves ballistically: fast, slowing, a brief stop,
  then sliding back and speeding up. Constant speed is the "drawn line" tell.
  - Run-up is about 6 to 10 m (120 to 200 px of ground) in 3 to 4 s.
  - The backwash takes longer.
- **The front is lobed,** faster down the cusp bays, slower over the horns and stones. Every run-up reaches a different
  height, so the limits cross.
- **The backwash thins in place,** sinks into the coarse sand and runs down the bays in rills. Where the next bore meets
  it there is a brief white collision.
- **The sheet itself is centimetres deep, so it is painted:** the body colour thinned, a sheen, drifting foam patches.
- **Swash marks** are seaward-concave arcs of fine grit and foam residue, several generations crossing, the freshest
  lowest and wettest.

## 3. Foam: a life cycle, never lines

- **Stage A:** the bore's whitewater. Solid, bright and lumpy, real geometry 6 to 20 px high casting a short shadow
  forward.
- **Stage B:** the patches left behind thin out exponentially (lifetimes of about 2 to 20 s in the sea) and tear into
  **lace**. Lace is holes in a sheet, not lines on water: a foam mat with round-to-elongated holes punched by upwelling
  boils, growing and stretching over a wave period.
- **Foam collects where the water converges:** the swash top, the cusp bays, the lee of stacks, streaks running out to
  sea along rip currents, crusts blown up the beach.
- **White is scarce and earned.** If more than 10 to 15% of the sea is near-white, it reads as drawn.

## 4. Black sand by its state (measured at Mýrdalssandur, beside Vík)

- **Dry black sand:** albedo 0.03 to 0.05, a charcoal grey lifted by grain tops. Paler where finer (wind-sorted on the
  backshore).
- **Wet sand:** albedo 0.01 to 0.02, near black; "wet dust reduces its albedo by 66%".
- **From the water up, the bands are:**
  1. **the glossy film band** (the swash and the seepage face): a mirror of the grey sky. It is the lightest wet value,
     and it shows the cliffs, the stacks and the pilgrim upside down and blurred;
  2. **the wet matte band:** the darkest value in the scene;
  3. **the drying band:** mottled, dry first on the horns and high spots, dark in the bays;
  4. **the dry charcoal sand.**

  The bands follow the cusps and the last swash limits, so they are lobed, never stripes.
- **The profile is real geometry,** from the sea up:
  - a submerged step at the break;
  - a steep 1:8 face;
  - a near-vertical berm crest at the high water, 0.3 to 1 m tall and casting shadow;
  - a flatter backshore of finer, drier sand;
  - the drift-log jam;
  - the cliff foot.
- **Beach cusps** are the rhythm: horns and bays about 12 to 17 m apart (240 to 340 px along the shore), each varying
  ±20%.
  - The horns are coarse, pebbly, drier and lighter.
  - The bays are finer, wetter, glossier, foam-filled, and channel the backwash.
- **Grain:** pebbles are 1 px and cobbles 2 to 5 px, real objects, rounded with a tiny wet highlight. They gather on
  the horns, the berm and the cliff foot. A rare cold-green glint of glass.
- **Ripples are rare on a steep face.** They belong to the low-tide lower sand, runnels and the lee of rocks, at 7 to 14
  cm (painted).
- **Wrack:** a broken high-tide line plus an older, higher storm line, of kelp, splinters, bones and carcasses. Behind
  it, giant grey drift logs jammed at the cliff foot, Rialto and Ruby style.

## 5. Water's light at our camera (Fresnel)

- **Flat water seen 30 degrees down reflects only about 6% of the sky.** A facet tilted 10 to 20 degrees away from the
  camera reflects 13 to 35%; one tilted toward it, under 3%.
- **So under a grey sky the backs of the waves catch a grey-white sheen and the faces toward us go dark.** The wave
  reads from its own normals, with no line drawn.
- **Overcast:** no sun glints, no sparkle path. The shadows are soft occlusion: under the lip, in the trough, under logs,
  in the bays.

## 6. A sea of blood (reasoned from blood's measured optics; nothing glows)

- **The body:** blood absorbs blue and green within a fraction of a millimetre but red only weakly, and scatters
  strongly. A deep body returns a deep, saturated, very dark red. An old standing sea would be browner and darker:
  oxblood to near black.
- **Opaque past a millimetre:** no light through the crests, no bottom seen. Only the last feathered edge of the swash
  shows the black sand through, as a maroon stain.
- **The sheen:** its refractive index is close to water's, so Fresnel stands. On so dark a body the grey sky sheen reads
  strongly, like oil or lacquer: a dull grey-pink glaze on black-red.
- **The foam:** thin films barely absorb, so the foam is pink. Fresh foam is pale rose, draining foam salmon-maroon,
  dried crust brown. Blood's proteins are what makes sea foam last, so the Red Water's foam is thicker and
  longer-lived: heavy pink lace, crusted swash lines, spume caught on the logs.
- **The stain tells the tide:** maroon film drying to brown-black crust below the high-tide line, clean charcoal above.

## 6b. Derek's ruling: a dying heartbeat, and blood's viscosity (2026-10-09)

"Make sure the tide moves rather slow as it's based on dying heart beats not normal speeds and blood is think and
viscous." This overrules the ocean's speeds above wherever they conflict.

- **The timing is a failing pulse,** never an ocean's period: a surge (the beat), a weaker second surge close behind,
  then a long stillness while everything drains. The beats are slow and uneven. Everything moves on them: the swell
  heaves, the roll folds, the swash runs up, all in the surges, crawling between.
- **Viscosity damps the short waves first.** A thick liquid has no chop and no spray: the surface is smooth, heavy and
  lacquered, the long heave alone carrying the form, with a skin that wrinkles and folds slowly where it is pushed.
- **The breaker sags rather than plunges.** The front steepens, bulges and rolls over on itself like a heavy fold of
  syrup or paint, its nose rounded and thick, closing on a small dark crease; it collapses into a heaving ridge, not a
  burst of white.
- **The swash is a gravity current of a thick liquid:** a slow tongue with a blunt, raised, rounded front (a real step,
  taller than water's), running up less far, slowing early; the backwash slower still. It leaves a thick glossy coat on
  the sand that drains back in slow sheets, a red lacquer mirroring the grey sky.
- **The foam is froth that holds:** slow, thick, clinging, tearing only slowly into lace.

## 7. How the masters did it

- **Homer:** foam by removal and touches (scraped, sanded, "staccato marks"), opaque white only where thickest. Broken
  marks over a dark water mass, never an outline.
- **Courbet's *The Wave*:** the wave as a solid mass of material. It is exactly our form law.
- **Hokusai:** the counter-example. A designed, outlined wave is the curvy-line look; do not use it.
- **Sea of Thieves, Valve:** realism from process. Foam born at crests, carried, blurred and decaying; layers half a
  phase apart with noise, so nothing visibly loops.
- **Diablo IV's coasts:** "believability, not realism"; "bleak and littered with seaweed, kelp and rotting carcasses".

## 8. Animation

One wave at one point, about 12 s:

| Time | What happens |
|---|---|
| 0 to 3 s | The swell rises; the backwash draws down |
| 3 to 4 s | The face goes near vertical; the lip throws forward |
| 4 to 5 s | Impact: falling droplets, gone in a second (no mist) |
| 5 to 7 s | The bore runs in and collides with the backwash |
| 7 to 10 s | The uprush slows, the lobed front stops, a foam line is stranded |
| 10 to 15 s | The backwash thins, sinks and leaves the gloss, which goes matte in seconds |

- **Simulate, do not loop:**
  - several swell trains at prime-ish periods (11, 13, 17 s);
  - a group envelope;
  - each cusp cell its own swash phase.
- **Foam is a living mask:** born, carried and decaying. Never a scrolling texture.
- **Moving geometry snaps to the pixel grid,** its deceleration shown in the spacing of the steps.
