# Chapter 7: faces in trees (2026-10-07)

For the Blind Face. Derek, on its second pass: "The face needs a ton of work. Looks like the chad face." It had been
built as a sculpted human face: symmetric smooth bulges, a hard bar of a brow, a square chin, graphic raking light. A
face in a tree is the opposite.

## 1. The real thing
**Pareidolia.** We see faces in bark because the eye hunts for two dark spots over a third. In trees, the parts that
make them are tree parts:
- knots and branch scars for eyes;
- burls and gnarls for cheeks and brows;
- the rolled woundwood round a hollow for lips;
- the trunk's twist and ridges for the long line of a nose.
The face is **found in the tree's own structure**, never added to it.

**Arthur Rackham's trees** (the root of Treebeard and every storybook tree since):
- he turned gnarled trunks into characters by letting the **natural texture and form of twisted bark suggest the
  features and the expression**;
- the faces are long and drawn down along the grain;
- they are asymmetric, grotesque or sorrowful, never handsome;
- the bark's lines run straight through them;
- they are half-seen: a little further away, they are only a tree.

**The Green Man** (medieval foliate heads, 12th to 15th century, on capitals and roof bosses): the features **grow
into** the plant; leaves and shoots come from the mouth, the nose, the eyes. The face and the growth are one thing.

## 2. The rules for a face grown in a tree
1. **Every feature is a tree part in a face's place:**
   - eyes: healed branch scars (a sunken oval with a raised ring of woundwood, its middle closed to a seam). This is
     exactly what "blind, the bark healed over" means;
   - brows: folds of woundwood above those scars;
   - nose: a vein-ridge running down;
   - mouth: a hollow with rolled lips;
   - cheeks: gaunt hollows, one small burl.
2. **Long, along the grain.** Trees grow up, so the face stretches up and droops: long, gaunt, old. Never wide or
   square.
3. **Asymmetric.** One scar higher and larger, the mouth crooked, a single burl. Symmetry is what made it a sculpted
   man.
4. **The bark runs through it.** Fissures and channels continue through the features. Only what is stretched over a
   swelling goes smooth and pale.
5. **Expression from the forms:** grief, or a long moan. The brow folds rise toward the middle, and the mouth hangs
   open and sags. No stoic jaw, no hero's brow bar.
6. **Read by its darks, not its bumps.** Two dark scar-hollows over one dark mouth make a face at any distance. The
   bumps are quiet, and the light is soft. No graphic raking.
7. **It recedes into the trunk** at every edge: half-seen, so it can be missed and then found.
