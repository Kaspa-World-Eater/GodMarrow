# Chapter 10: The Olympic coast

For the Red Shore (Derek, 2026-10-08: the start "on the edge of the ocean ... the black sands ... then it'll work its
way inward through the environment following the natural landscape ecosystem trends"). The real model is the
outer coast of the Olympic Peninsula (Rialto, Ruby and Second Beaches, Hole-in-the-Wall, the Quillayute and Hoh
mouths), with Iceland's Reynisfjara for the black sand itself. This chapter covers each band from the surf inland,
how each is made by its cause, and their true sizes. It ends with recipes for the 3D road and the height engine.
The Red Water is the one thing that is not of the real world: the area page holds its rules (blood that is red by
its own colour, never by glow).

## 1. What makes this coast

- **The land rises and the sea cuts it.** The peninsula is being lifted as the ocean plate goes under it; the sea,
  risen since the ice melted, cuts back the uplifted land. So the coast is a line of cliffs and bluffs being eaten,
  with the harder rock left standing out in the surf as stacks, arches and benches.
- **The forest comes right down to the sea.** There is no dune field of any size: the temperate rainforest's edge
  stands on the bluff top, its fringe sheared by the wind, and its dead trees come down to the beach as drift logs.
- **Everything on the beach is sorted by the waves' energy.** Fine sand where the swash spends itself; cobbles thrown
  up in a ridge where the big waves break; logs only where the storms reach.

## 2. The bands, from the surf inland

**The surf and the foreshore.**
- Long swells, the breakers folding and running up the beach as a thin sheet (the swash), drawing back (the
  backwash) and leaving a lace line of foam and a dark wet margin.
- The foreshore is a gentle slope (1 in 10 to 1 in 30), wet and glossy: it mirrors the sky in a sheen, darkest just
  behind the retreating sheet, lighter as it drains.
- Patterns the backwash leaves: little rills and diamond marks draining back, beach cusps (scallops a few yards
  apart along the swash line), and dark-and-light streaks where heavy grains sort from light ones.
- At low water: wave-cut rock benches with tide pools, surge channels cut along the joints, kelp and wrack lying in
  heaps.

**The black sand.** (Reynisfjara is the model: basalt broken by the sea into fine black grains.)
- Wet: near black, glossy, with the sky's grey lying in it.
- Damp: matt black-brown.
- Dry, above the reach of the tide: grey-black, a little lighter and warmer, wind-rippled, with the light catching
  the ripple crests; footprints hold their shape.
- The tide's lines: a high-water line of wrack (kelp, twigs, foam scum), and older higher lines from storms, each a
  ragged band.

**The cobble berm.** Where the storm waves break, the beach steepens into a ridge of rounded stones, fist to head size,
the biggest at the crest, the sand between them washed out. Stones polished, darker when wet, each with its own
rounded shape and a lit top.

**The drift logs at the storm line.** Rialto's "giant's graveyard":
- Whole trees, mostly Sitka spruce and cedar, thrown up by winter storms and piled crosswise in a belt 10 to 30 yards
  wide between the beach and the forest, two and three deep, some with their root wads still on (root wads 3 to 5
  yards across).
- Trunks 1 to 3 yards across and 10 to 50 yards long, stripped of bark and branches, bleached silver-grey on top,
  darker and green with algae below, checked with long cracks along the grain, ends broken and rounded.
- Sand drifts against their seaward sides and fills between them; the newest lie lowest and loosest, the oldest are
  half buried and grassed over at the forest edge.
- They lie roughly parallel to the shore where a storm left them, never at random.

**The backshore.** A narrow strip above the logs: dune grass, beach pea, sea rocket, salal coming down from the forest;
small clear streams cutting across the sand in their own shallow channels.

**The sea stacks, arches and headlands.**
- Stacks are the harder rock (basalt, breccia, hard sandstone) left when the softer was cut away. Sheer sides,
  undercut at the foot by a notch the waves cut, tops crowned with wind-shaped spruce and salal.
- Arches form where the sea finds a softer layer through a headland (Hole-in-the-Wall). Stacks fall too: one at
  Rialto fell in 2009, its pieces lying in the surf.
- At their feet: wave-cut benches, boulders fallen from them, tide pools, the surf breaking white round them.
- Sizes: 10 to 60 yards tall, often in rows following the old headland's line, the nearest ones walkable at low tide.

**The spruce fringe and the bluffs.**
- On the exposed headlands the Sitka spruce and shore pine grow as krummholz: the salt spray and the sand-laden wind
  shear off every shoot on the windward side, pruning the canopy into a tight wedge that rises away from the wind.
  Their windward branches are dead and grey; their trunks lean and twist away from the wind.
- Behind the first wedge, taller trees shelter in it, so the forest edge rises like a ramp from the sea.
- The bluffs (glacial outwash and till, 10 to 40 yards) erode by slumps and by small rivulets cutting down: a raw
  face of grey sand and gravel, roots hanging from the turf lip, fallen trees lying head-down the face, a talus fan
  at the foot (Kalaloch's Tree of Life hangs by its roots over a gap a stream has cut beneath it).
- Windthrow is the forest's main death here: most of the trees that die are blown down.

**The river mouths.**
- A river braids across the beach in shifting channels, its bars moving with each storm. Logs jam its mouth.
- At the meeting, fresh water fans over the salt in a lighter tongue.
- Inland the river is lined with alder and big-leaf maple, its banks cut.

**Into the rainforest.** Past the fringe the ground rises onto terraces and the old growth begins (chapter 06, the
rainforest and old-growth ecosystem pages): spruce and cedar giants, big-leaf maples hung with moss, sword fern, nurse
logs, a floor of pit and mound.

## 3. For the Red Shore

- The one wind of the world goes out to sea: so the fringe here leans and is sheared on the LANDWARD side, its wedge
  rising toward the water, as if pulled (the area page: "an uncanny thing to get right").
- The Red Water stains: the wet foreshore is red-black, not grey; the tide lines are dried red film; the foam is dark
  and clings; streams cross the black sand clear and fan into the red in streaks.
- The washed-up dead and their things lie along the high-water line with the wrack; the old canoes lie upturned above
  the logs.
- Sea stacks are dark basalt, the red breaking round their feet.

## 4. Recipes

**The beach's height profile (height engine), from the water up, by distance d from the waterline:**
1. Foreshore: rising 1 in 15, with beach cusps (a sine along the shore, period 8 to 15 yd, amplitude 0.15 yd, only in
   the swash zone) and backwash rills (thin channels down the slope, 0.03 yd deep, every 0.5 to 2 yd).
2. Berm: a step up of 0.3 to 0.6 yd at the old high-water line, its seaward face steeper.
3. Cobble berm where the energy is high: a ridge 0.8 to 1.5 yd tall of stones as tilted planes with chips (chapter 04,
   never domes), the biggest at the crest.
4. Dry sand: wind ripples (crest every 0.1 to 0.15 yd, a few hundredths high, asymmetric, the steep face away from the
   wind), drifts piled against every log on the side the wind comes from.
5. The log belt: logs are objects (below); the sand between them is filled to their lower thirds.

**The sand's paint, by wetness (a map from the tide and the waves' reach):**
- wet: the darkest tone, with the sky's sheen as a lighter band where the slope faces the light;
- damp: matt, one step lighter;
- dry: two steps lighter and warmer, ripple crests lit;
- tide lines as ragged bands of wrack and film, following the beach's contour.

**A drift log (3D road object):** a tapered trunk (diameter 1 to 3 yd), its bark gone; the top bleached (a light,
cool grey ramp), the underside dark and green; long checks along the grain as real grooves; a broken, rounded end; on
some a root wad (a flattened fan of root tubes, broken short, with sand caught in it); set half into the sand, at
the storm line, roughly shore-parallel.

**A sea stack (3D road object):** a rock mass of piled joint blocks (stacked prisms, not a smooth cylinder); a
wave-cut notch at its foot; fallen blocks and a bench round it; its top capped with soil, salal and a sheared spruce.
Paint: basalt near black, lighter on the lit faces and edges, wet and dark below the splash line.

**The fringe (with the tree objects to come):** sheared wedges of spruce whose canopy is a smooth swept surface
rising away from the wind; the dead grey branches sticking out on the windward side; taller trees behind.

**A bluff (height engine):** a raw face of grey sand and gravel stepped by slump scars, a turf lip overhanging (3D
road for the overhang), roots hanging, a fallen tree down the face, a talus fan at the foot, a stream's notch cutting
it.
