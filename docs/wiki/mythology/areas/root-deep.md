# The Root Deep

*Act I · zone `root_deep` · under the Hide, though the game marks it outdoors · creatures of levels 13–16 · borders: the
Drowned Fen, the upper catacombs, the Broken Bridge · in the game: the old procedural zone (164 × 164 yards), to be
remade by the bog's method · the Horned: `../../in-game-texts/06-the-myriad.md` · the Deep in its own words: "Words Up Through the Seam"
in the Codex (`data/codex.json`)*

Under the skin of the world lies a country. The lowlands call it the Root Deep, or only the Deep; the oldest texts give
it another name, Ne-no-kuni, which nobody now says. It is warm and dark, and it breathes: out for a long while, then in.
It is courteous. It answers iron. It keeps a little of everyone who goes down, and sends the rest back up in good order.
Its halls are wider when you enter them than when you leave. The Horned, the Shrine Keepers' purifiers come to their
end, walk down its road with their sacks, and nothing walks up.

Here the god shows as strongly as anywhere in the act: the roof is the underside of the Hide, hung with the Hollow
Wood's roots like hair, and the floor is warm and folded. The feeling is courteous dread. Nothing here is cruel, and
nothing here is safe.

**The one image:** the pilgrim small under a roof hung with pale roots, the lantern's shadows shifting as the hall
breathes, and far down the hall a line of horned figures walking away, sacks on their backs.

## Found in the world

*In-game writing: texts a pilgrim can find here, in the world's own voices.*

### At the head of the warm road

*From the day-book of a purifier of the Shrine Keepers at the Well-Shrine in the Drowned Fen, written in the season her
guardian stones began to turn toward her.*

The elder says I should see the road before I walk it, so that when my morning comes my feet will know the way. So today
I went down behind three of the Horned, at a distance, as far as one may go and still come up.

They went down past the drowned altar, where the floor broke in a ring long ago, and the road begins there, going down.
It is warm from the first step. They did not look back. Their faces have gone long and hard, the way ours go, and the
horns on the oldest were longer than my hand. Each carried a sack on her back, tied at the neck, and each sack was
heavy. I know what is in them, because one day there will be one on mine.

At the head of the road stands a stone, squat, like the numbered stones on the pilgrims' road, so worn that nobody can
read its figure. Its top is hollowed into a dish by the coins laid there. Leave a copper on the stone, the boatmen say,
and something will be kinder to you. I left one. Coming up, I found the dish empty.

The road goes down a long way through halls. The roof is high and pale and hung with roots, thousands of them, some as
fine as hair and some as thick as a wrist, and they all sway the same way, slowly, and then back. It is breathing. Every
fourth beat of my heart the shadows from my lamp moved all at once, and settled. I stood still to listen, and under my
own heartbeat there was another, slower. The longer I stood, the nearer the two came to keeping time. I walked on before
they matched.

The Horned went down and down. In all the time I was on that road, nothing came up past me.

I turned back at the third hall. I had counted my paces going in. Coming out, the third hall was narrower than it had
been, and the second narrower than that, and I reached the stone at the top in fewer paces than I had gone down in. I
have counted it twice since on my cord, and the cord says the same.

It keeps a little of everyone, the boatmen say. I do not know what it kept of me. My breath is the same, and my knots
are the same. When I came up, the boatmen's wives were on the bank with their small ones, and one of them saw where I
had been and turned her baby's face into her shoulder, and laid her hand over the top of its head, on the soft place,
until I had gone by. They do that, I am told, when anyone comes up from the Deep, and they will not say why.

My stones have turned another finger's width toward me. I am in no hurry. The road will wait. It was very polite.

### The echo

*Told by the boatmen of the Fen on the nights they sleep under the bowed arch. A purifier wrote it down in her
day-book.*

Two brothers went down into the Deep to see if it was true that it answers iron. The elder carried a pick, and in the
first hall, where the roof hangs low, he knocked on the wall with it three times, as the diggers on the Moor do.

The wall answered. It said good evening, in a voice like warm air coming out of a crack, and thanked him for knocking,
because it is courteous to answer a knock. Then it thanked him for the last time he had come, and for what he had
brought it then. He had never been down before, and he told it so. It said yes, gently, the way you agree with a child
who has it wrong, and was quiet.

They went on, because they were proud. In the third hall the younger brother heard his own voice ahead of him in the
dark, saying "Shall we go back?" He had not said it yet. He was only about to. He answered it. "Yes," he said, "let us
go back."

They came up in good order, as people do from the Deep. But from then on, the elder says, the younger spoke a breath
late, as if he were always answering something. He lived a long time, and poled a good boat, and was kind, and he
answered things nobody else had heard. Near the end he would stop in the middle of a word, and listen, and nod, and say
"yes," and finish the word.

So the boatmen tell their sons: in the Deep your echo comes back before you speak, and you must never answer it. And if
the wall thanks you for something you never did, thank it back and leave. It means no harm. It never means any harm.

## For building it

The zone is the old procedural map. Remake it by the bog's method, with the depth into the god set high
(`tools/art_study/ecosystems/organic_deep.md`).

**The land.** Under the skin. Real models: Wind Cave in South Dakota, a cave that breathes, its mouth blowing out or
drawing in with the weather outside; the lava tubes of Hawaii, where the forest's roots hang through the roof in
curtains; the cenotes of the Yucatán, roots dropping to still water.
- **At true scale:** halls fifteen to forty yards across with roofs ten to twenty-five yards up; the ways between them
  narrow and turning; root-pillars where the Wood's great roots come all the way down.
- **The roof:** the underside of the Hide, pale and pored, hung with root curtains to head height and in places to the
  floor.
- **The floor:** warm and folded, its ridges and soft hollows real height, tallow and grey under the lantern; level
  pools in the hollows.
- **The floor is never bare:** shed root-hair in drifts, husks of fallen veins, pale caps (blue over the hollow places,
  white on the sound), and the Horned's dropped things: a strip of white paper, a broken fan rib, a knotted cord.
- **Objects, by cause:** the lantern-stones where ways come down from above; three wayside shrines set by the
  shrine-folk at the heads of the ways; the chests as things set down and never taken up.

**Landmarks**, each by the pit method:
- **The coin stone at the head of the road:** a squat numbered stone like the Pilgrim Road's, its figure worn away, its
  top dished smooth by centuries of coins laid and taken. No light of its own: a copper glints in the dish at dusk and
  is gone when the pilgrim comes back.
- **The Horned's road:** a broad way down through the halls, worn smooth by feet all going one way, every print pointing
  down. Here and there a sack set down against the wall, tied at the neck, very old, never opened.
- **The underside of a ring:** in one hall, eight great hooked teeth hang point-down from the roof in a ring, their
  roots sunk in a fold of the roof like gums. One has fallen and lies on the floor beneath, point inward. Between them
  the roof sags in a dark skin that lifts and settles. A thin grey light leaks down round the teeth, the only daylight
  in the zone. Old as the rings above; never explained.
- **The Reversed Spring:** a still pool in a fold of the floor whose water runs inward, turning in a slow spiral down a
  throat at its centre, never emptying and never filling; its rim worn by kneeling. Whoever wets their lips from it
  loses a memory and is not told which. Pale wet stone; blue caps round the rim give its light.
- **The halls that narrow:** a proposal: the generator tapers each way toward its exit, and sets a hall's far mouth a
  pace off from where it lay going in.

**Light and weather.** No sky. The lantern is the key light, and the hall breathes: every fourth heartbeat its flame and
every shadow lean in, then out. The god's own light is restrained: blue caps, a faint pale glow in pustules on the
tendrils, the grey round the teeth. The breath is the weather. The in-breath draws the root curtains and the litter
toward the halls' far ends; the out-breath is warm and damp, and beads on the roots and drips. No haze or fog, no red
light, no glowing flesh.

**The god showing through.** Strongly: the Hide seen from beneath, the Wood's veins hanging through it, the warm folded
floor, the breathing and the second heartbeat, teeth in rings. Whether the Deep is part of the god or something the god
lay on stays a rival telling: the Deep calls itself the floor the god stood on.

**Creatures.**
- **Husks** (93): stand in long rows facing down the road, a great many, like a congregation. Pull a few at a time and
  fight in the narrow ways.
- **Tithe-Hands** (61): come through the root curtains, round behind; put a root-pillar at your back.
- **Weepers** (24): shoot from behind the curtains, which are soft cover: they catch needles and let a body through.
- **Wick-Saints of the Canopy** (17): hang in the root curtains and drop. **Wick-Saints** (14): come to the lantern.
- **Gravebloats** (16): let them burst among the Husks.
- **Elder Vein-Borers** (14): the whole floor is soft, so they rise anywhere but on the Horned's road, the one refuge.
- **The Trunk-Thing** (10): where a great root comes down and stands as a pillar, one in many is awake. It never moves,
  and lashes whatever comes within two yards; fight it from range.
- **Pyre-Saints** (9): dry root-hair burns, and the curtains catch and drop fire.
- **Gasps** (5): drift through the curtains. **Bellwethers** (2): a toll far down the halls, then the charge; stand
  before a root-pillar and step aside.

**Sound.** The breath: a long draw through every hall, the root curtains whispering, then the release. A second
heartbeat, slower than yours. Footsteps that sound a beat early. Far off, the Horned's slow tread and the drag of sacks.
Drips. A voice in the walls only if someone knocks with iron.

**Keep out.** Sky and weather from above; fog or haze; red light and glowing red flesh; plants beyond roots, caps and
the god's growths; the Horned speaking, turning or fighting; any explanation of what the Deep is.

**Open questions.**
- **Under or out:** the game marks the zone outdoors, the lore puts it under the Hide. Light it as under (no sky, the
  lantern and the god's own light) and keep the flag only for the generator?
- **The Horned in the world:** figures walking away far down the halls, never fought, never reached?
- **The Trunk-Thing:** its game note calls it "a face grown into a great trunk". With the Blind Face struck, should it
  have no face?
- **A mouth from the Hollow Wood,** where the lore combs the roots: the game has none.
- **The old name,** Ne-no-kuni: keep it anywhere, or let it go?
