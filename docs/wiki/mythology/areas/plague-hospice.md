# The Plague Hospice

*Act I · zone `plague_hospice` · under the ground · creatures of levels 9–12 · borders: the Bone Catacombs (upper) · in
the game: the old procedural crypt map (120 × 120), to be remade by the bog's method · breath gone wrong, and the
purifiers who take it in: `../../in-game-texts/06-the-myriad.md` · the Pale Order's standing dead: `../../in-game-texts/02-the-pale-order.md`*

Beside the pilgrim ossuary of the upper catacombs, the Shrine Keepers' purifiers cut a house into the rock for the
breath-sick, people whose breath had gone wrong and turned round inside them. The lowlands called the purifiers who kept
it the sisters. They chose the place because the air there leans down, toward the deep country beyond the catacombs, so
that a wrong breath let go in it goes down with the air, away from the next bed. The Pale Order's standing dead kept its
doors, because the dead do not breathe and the sickness could not take them. The sisters kept the sick, and then the
sickness kept the sisters. The dead are still keeping the doors.

The game calls it the Plague Hospice; the lowlands call it the breath-house, and its sickness is breath gone wrong and
nothing else. It should feel still and patient: mercy that was tried and failed, with the air itself the danger.

**The one image:** a long ward under a low vault, cots in rows with the blankets drawn up over patient shapes, shafts
going up into the dark with nothing coming down them, and at the far end a knight of bone at the grille, still keeping
it.

## Found in the world

*In-game writing: texts a pilgrim can find here, in the world's own voices.*

### The last leaf of the day-book

*The last leaf written in the house's day-book, found open on the shelf of the alcove at the end of the long ward. The
hand is a purifier's. Most of the leaves before it are counts of breaths.*

The air has stopped leaning down. I am writing it plainly so that whoever keeps this book after me does not have to find
it out.

We came here because the air leans. Hold a paper figure up by its thread anywhere in these rooms and it turns toward the
deep end of the house, toward the catacombs and what lies past them, and a breath let go here goes that way with it. The
breath-sick breathe in longer than they breathe out, the other way about from a newborn, and the wrong breath turns
round in them and cannot find the door. We take it into ourselves, a knot for each, and breathe it out across the fan
toward the deep, and the air carries it down. That is why the house is here. It is why we cut the shafts up through the
rock to the sky as well, one over every third bed, so that whatever would not go down could go up.

The knights' dead keep the doors. The sick are not to leave and the well are not to come in, and the living could not be
trusted with that, because the living breathe. The dead stand at the grilles and do not breathe, and nothing passes
them. Their price is the frames of everyone who dies here, carried up the mountain, when we would give our dead to the
water. We pay it. We curse them in the lanes and pay them on the step.

This season the figures hang straight. The breath no longer goes down. Somebody has stopped the shafts at the top, I do
not know who: the knights say the lowlanders did it, and the lowlanders say we did. So the wrong breath stays in the
rooms and turns round and finds the next bed. I have taken in more than I have room for. My cord is longer than the
knots I remember tying, and this morning the guardian stone at the ward door was facing my alcove.

The sick all dream the same room now. Every one of them, waking, tells me of a warm room they have never been in, very
large, full of people on their knees. They speak of it gladly, and they are very patient.

When my time comes I will walk down through the catacombs the way the air used to go, with my hands full, so that the
others need not. If the stone is facing whoever reads this, go up, and leave the doors to the dead.

### At the grille

*Told by a woman of the lowlands whose son was taken into the breath-house, to a pilgrim on the catacomb road. She asked
him to write down that she still goes.*

When my boy began to breathe wrong, the purifier who came to our door said he must go to the house by the catacombs.
He walked there, with his belt knotted at the back so that I could hold it to the door. At the door a knight of bone
took him from me. Its face was the front of a skull, its shield was made of other people's skulls, and it put its hand
flat between us, as a man stops a cart. The sisters took him in behind it. Nobody well was to go in, they said, and the
dead at the door did not breathe and could not be talked round.

So I went to the grille. There is a grille in the outer passage where you can see down the long ward, and the families
stood at it, and the sick who could still walk came to the other side. I went every seventh day. He came to the bars and
we talked, and behind him I could see the sisters going up and down with their fans, and the dead standing at the end of
the room with their shields.

Toward the end he told me he had been dreaming of a big warm room with people kneeling in it, and that he liked it
there. I put salt on the step every time I went, and the knight at the door never moved its foot off it.

Then one week nobody came to the grille. The cots were in their rows and the blankets were drawn up over all of them,
and nothing moved under them, and there were no sisters, only the dead at the doors and at the end of the room, where
they always were. I called through the grille for the sisters and for my boy. I did not call his name; I am not a fool.
I asked the knight at the door to let me in. It stood there. I asked it a long time, and then I went home, and the next
week I went back.

I still go. I stand at the grille with the others. There are more of us now than there ever were sick. Sometimes, a long
way down the ward, one of the blankets moves.

## For building it

**The land.**
- **Cut into the rock beside the upper catacombs.** Draw it from the long wards of the old hospitals, built for air: the
  Great Hall of the Poor at the Hospices de Beaune (one long hall, beds in rows down both walls, all facing a holy place
  at the far end); the Long Ward of the Sacra Infermeria in Valletta, a vaulted ward kept by a military order; and the
  luminaria of the Roman catacombs, shafts cut up through the rock to bring air and light down.
- **The rock:** a soft pale limestone, lime-washed white, the wash now grey and streaked; the wards barrel-vaulted.
- **The house by cause:** the outer passage from the catacomb road, with grilles where the families stood; three long
  wards opening off it, a shaft over every third bed and a shrine of the small gods at each far end; the sisters'
  alcoves; the burning bank; the dead-room, where the knights took the frames; the way down, where the air used to go.
- **True scale:** each ward 60 yards long and 8 wide, its vault 5 yards up; the cots 2 yards long, a yard apart in two
  rows; the shafts a yard across, going up out of sight.
- **The floor is never bare:** straw from the cots, matted and scattered; paper figures by the hundred, folded with
  their arms open, a few with them closed; long knotted cords; basins and ewers; grey felt fallen from the covered
  mirrors (the Veiled Crone's families covered them); salt crusted on the outer steps; grit and old ash fanned under
  every shaft; paper-boat ash at the bank.

**Landmarks.**
- **The long ward** (the zone's signature):
  - *Shape:* a barrel-vaulted hall running away into the dark, cots in two rows, the mouths of the shafts in a line
    along the vault.
  - *Story by cause:* on most cots the blankets are drawn up over shapes; under every shaft lies a fan of grit and ash,
    the bed beneath it pushed aside; the walls by the beds are scratched with the sisters' tallies of breaths; one paper
    figure still hangs by its thread from the shrine's lintel, straight down.
  - *Material:* lime-wash over limestone; straw; undyed blankets; paper gone yellow-brown and soft.
  - *Light:* one shaft is still open at the top, and a thin hard thread of grey daylight comes down it onto one bed, its
    source far above. Rain comes down the same way and has soaked that bed's straw dark, a level puddle beside it.
  - *Age:* a lifetime since the last sister.
- **The grilles:** iron grilles in the outer passage wall, looking down the wards, their bars worn bright at hand height
  on both sides, salt crusted on the step beneath, the Ossuary Wardens at the doors beside them. A lantern in the outer
  passage throws the bars' shadows long down the ward.
- **The burning bank:** a raised bank of dry stone in a side room under a flue, where the sisters burned the year's
  wrong breath in paper boats and kept silent until the ash was cold. Ash and half-burnt boats drift against it, and
  grey light comes down the flue onto the ash.
- **The last sister's alcove:** her day-book open on the shelf; names written over the walls from the floor to as high
  as she could reach, until there was no room; her cord coiled on the floor, longer than the alcove; the ward's guardian
  stone, turned, facing in.

**Light and weather.** The lantern is the key light; the game's three lanterns stand in the outer passage, at the
burning bank and at the way down. Draw the breath gone wrong only by what it does: lantern flames lean toward the
nearest stopped shaft and gutter, paper figures stir on the floor in no draught, and the felt over the mirrors moves in
and out a little. No fog, no red light.

**The god showing through: more than a hint.** The air itself, which once leaned down toward the deep country and now
turns round in the rooms; the walls on the catacomb side warm to the hand; and at the way down, the faint pull of
something breathing very slowly.

**Creatures** (the game's mix):
- **Husks (52):** the sick who did not lie down. They lie under the blankets, patient, until three are near, then sit up
  together. A ward is a gauntlet of beds; take them in the doorways to the outer passage.
- **Ossuary Wardens (35):** the Order's standing dead, still keeping the doors as they were told: the grilles, the ward
  doors, the dead-room and the way down. They block from the front behind shields of skulls; flank them in the wards.
- **Weepers (19):** faceless mourners on the families' side of the grilles. Their bone needles go through the bars where
  a body cannot; go round by the doors.
- **Choristers (9):** a dirge at the far ends of the wards.
- **Gravebloats (6) and Gasps (6):** the Gasps, breath gone astray, are at home here and come through the walls.
- **Pyre-Saints (3):** at the burning bank. Straw, paper and blankets burn fast, and fire runs the length of a ward
  along the beds; the basins and the puddle under the open shaft douse it.
- **Kneelers (3):** at the shrines at the wards' ends.
- **Objects:** one waystone, three lanterns, four chests. The grilles stop bodies and let needles, fire and Gasps
  through; the beds give low cover; rock and lime-wash break shots.

**Sound.** Coughing: one far off, three rooms away, and then something nearer coughing back. Otherwise a held stillness:
straw shifting, paper rustling, the felt moving, the Wardens' slow tread, and from the far ends the Choristers' dirge.

**Keep out.**
- Anything of decay: no sores, no corruption, no flies. The sickness shows only in the air and the stillness.
- Our world's plague: no plague doctors or their masks, and no "plague" in the world's voice (the tellers say the
  breath-sickness and the breath-house).
- Fog, haze or coloured clouds for the breath; the old whisper's vinegar; red light.

**Open questions for Derek.**
- The sisters as the Shrine Keepers' purifiers, and the Order's dead at the doors, are proposals: keep?
- Should the way down lead toward the Root Deep, where purifiers walk at their end? The game links the hospice only to
  the upper catacombs.
