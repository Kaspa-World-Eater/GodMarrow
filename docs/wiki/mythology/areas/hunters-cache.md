# The Hunter's Cache

*Act I · zone `hunter_cache` · outdoors, 104 × 104 · creatures of levels 4–5 · borders: Fern Gully · in the game: the
old procedural map (`hunter_cache_s1001` to `s1020`), to be remade by the bog's method*

Up out of the far side of Fern Gully lies heather the fire never reached: old, deep, knee- to waist-high, grey-green and
rolling to the sky. Hunters have worked this ground for generations, and the cache is where one of them kept what he
took: a turf-roofed hut half sunk in the heather, hides stretched on frames, tallow in crocks, bone tied in bundles, a
bow leaning by the door. He meant to come back for it. His boots are on the step, and his footprints lead away barefoot.

Nothing in this world was ever born that a hunter could take. The hunters take from what comes up out of the god: hide
and finger-bone from the Hands, fat for tallow from the swollen ones, the bone needles the Weepers cry, and from the
Husks only the knot of the belt, for the copper the road-wardens pay. The heather here grows over soft, warm ground
where things push up through the skin, and the hunters dig their waiting-pits at its edges. It is the Heath as it was
before the burning, and it should feel lonely, patient and watched, the way a place feels when somebody has only just
left it.

**The one image:** a turf hut half sunk in old heather under a grey sky, hides drying on the outstretched arms of a
stone saint, a bow leaning by the door, boots side by side on the step, and bare footprints going away into the
heather.

## Found in the world

*In-game writing: texts a pilgrim can find here, in the world's own voices.*

### The rules on the door

*Burned with a hot nail into the inside of the cache's door, in one hunter's hand, over some years by the look of it.
The later lines are cut deeper.*

Whoever opens this door, you are welcome to the cache if you keep the rules. They are mine, and my father's, and his.

There is nothing on the Hide to hunt that was ever born. Everything that walks out there came up out of the god, and
the god is all there is to take from. So we take from what comes up. That is the trade, and anyone who tells you it is a
holy work or a filthy one has never done it.

From the Hands we take the hide of the palm, which is the best leather there is, pale and without a hair, and the bones
of the fingers, which the carvers want. Set the snares along the soft ground at dusk, a loop at finger height, staked,
and come back at first grey. A Hand caught by one finger will wait for you. Never go to a snare in the dark, because a
Hand is never alone.

From the swollen ones we take the fat, and you render it to tallow in the pan inside, never in the open, because the
smell brings more of them. Lance them from as far off as you can. The small ones burst quicker than you think.

From the Weepers we take the needles they cry. Pull them out of whatever they struck, a post or a shield or a coat, and
keep them in the roll by the door. The camps on the Moor buy bone needles for awls.

From the Husks we take nothing but the knot. The road-wardens pay a copper for every belt-knot brought in, as proof of
a Husk put down. A belt is knotted at the back so that whoever walks behind has something to hold. I know that. I cut it
anyway. A copper is a copper, and a Husk will not be holding anyone.

Take nothing else from anything that was a person, not a hide, not a bone, not a tooth. Burn them where they fall if you
can, and if you cannot, turn them face down.

The ground here is soft in places, and warm, and the heather grows thicker and greener on it. That is where they come
up. Dig your pit at the edge, roof it with heather, and sit in it. Do not sleep in it.

Where the ground is softest and the heather greenest of all, there is a place my father would not go near, and I have
not gone near it, and I burn this into the door so that whoever has the cache after me does not either.

Lately I have woken in the night with my boots off.

### What was found at the cache

*A letter from the hunter's sister to a copyist, who had asked whether the cache was still kept. She had gone
out to it the spring after her brother stopped coming in.*

You asked whether my brother's cache is still kept. It is not. I went out to it in the spring, when he had not come in
to the Moor all winter, and I will tell you what I found, since you asked, and since nobody else will go.

The door was shut, and his rules were on the inside of it as they always were, with a new line at the bottom. The hides
were on their frames, gone black as tar because nobody had turned them. The tallow was in the crocks. The finger-bones
were tied in their bundles, and the needle-roll was by the door, full. His bow was leaning beside the door unstrung, the
way he left it when he meant to be back within the hour.

His snares were set along the soft ground in a line, as he set them every dusk. Every one had sprung, and every one had
rusted shut around nothing.

His boots were on the step, side by side, the laces undone. His footprints went away from the step into the heather,
and they were bare. I followed them. They went straight, the way you walk when you know where you are going and are in
no hurry. They crossed the soft ground where the heather is greenest and went on to the place he wrote of on the door,
the one he would not go near. There they stopped. There was no mark of anything else there, and no mark of him coming
back, and the heather there was so green and so soft that I went no closer than the last print.

I brought nothing back. It is his cache, and he meant to come back for it. I left his boots on the step, side by side,
in case.

## For building it

**The land.** Old heather moor that has not burned for a very long time, drawn from real heather in its old age. Heather
grows through pioneer, building and mature phases into the degenerate: tall, woody and open in the middle, its old stems
grey with lichen. Bracken grows in drifts, with bilberry and a few juniper bushes, peat beneath, and outcrops. The fire
never crossed the gully, so this is how the Heath looked before.
- **The soft ground, by cause:** patches where the ground is warm and gives underfoot, the heather on them thicker,
  greener and younger than round about, because the warmth feeds it. From the camera they read as green islands in
  grey-brown heather. Things come up through them, and the hunters' pits and snare lines ring them.
- **The floor stays alive:** heather litter and grey woody stems, bilberry, moss and lichen, bracken fronds, peat in the
  gaps, snare stakes, old pits, and near the cache, bone chips and scraps of hide.

**Landmarks, by the pit method.**
- **The cache.**
  - **Shape:** a low hut dug half into a heathery bank, walls of turf and stone, a turf roof thatched with heather, its
    door turned away from the wind; drying frames and a hearth before it.
  - **Cause:** hides gone black on their frames; finger-bones in bundles; tallow crocks; the needle-roll; the bow
    unstrung by the door; the boots side by side on the step; bare footprints leading off in a straight line, which
    neither rain nor wind fills. The rules are burned inside the door. The zone's four chests are his caches, buried
    and stone-lidded round the hut.
  - **Material:** turf, heather thatch, bone pegs, pale hide, tallow, rusted snare wire.
  - **Light:** the zone's one lantern stands on its stone by the door, follows no one, and leans always toward the place
    where the footprints stop.
  - **Age:** a family's generations; left a year or two ago.
- **The drying saint** (the statue): an old wayside saint, arms held out, which the hunters use as a rack. Hides hang
  from both arms, and one is draped over its face.
- **The waiting-pits and snare lines** round the soft ground: shallow pits roofed with heather; wire snares on stakes,
  sprung and rusted shut.
- **The place he would not go near:** the softest and greenest patch, where the footprints end. Nothing is built there.
  It reads only as green, soft, and too even.

**Light and weather.** Overcast and open, the heather in grey-greens and purple-browns. The wind from the Ridge's
quarter comes over lighter here. Rain darkens the heather, beads on the hides and drips from the turf eaves, and the
pits hold water. No fog. The lantern is the key light.

**The god showing through: about as on the Heath.** The soft warm ground where things come up; the god's pieces on the
racks; the place where the footprints end.

**Creatures.** Husk 31, Tithe-Hand 20, Bloatling 10, Weeper 6: the hunters' trade, now that nobody works it.
- **Husks** stand on the soft ground, as if waiting for something to come up beside them.
- **Tithe-Hands** run the snare lines; the old heather hides them to the wrist.
- **Bloatlings** drift toward the smell of tallow round the cache.
- **Weepers** keep to the bank above the hut.
- **In a fight:** the old heather hides low things and burns fast if lit; the hut's turf walls are cover; the frames are
  low cover; the soft ground is where new ones come up.

**Sound.** Wind in old heather, a dry hiss unlike the Ridge's moan; hides knocking on their frames; a snare wire
ticking; insects in the heather; at the soft ground, a slow give underfoot.

**Keep out.** Meat, or anything eaten (the old voice line's "dried meat on the rack" breaks the law: the racks hold
hides); snares or traps sized for any real creature; trophies of real creatures; fog; red light; crows; the old
generator's shared landmarks.

**Open questions for Derek.**
- Whether the hunters' trade is right for the world: hide and finger-bone from the Hands, tallow from the swollen ones,
  Weeper needles for awls, and a copper from the road-wardens for each Husk's belt-knot.
- Who pays the bounties. We say the road-wardens (after the warden's notice in the Codex, "What the Marked Carry").
- Whether a zone this small stays its own map or becomes the far end of Fern Gully.
