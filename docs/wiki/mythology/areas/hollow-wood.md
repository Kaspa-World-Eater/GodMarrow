# The Hollow Wood

*Act I · zone `hollow_wood` · outdoors · creatures of levels 5–8 · borders: the Ashen Moor, the Hollow Crypt, the
Pilgrim Road · in the game: a 50-piece set baked and a seeded zone (`hollow_wood_s9001`), not yet in the zone list, its
floor still bare · pass logs: `tools/landkit/passes/hollow_wood_seeded.md`, `vigil.md`, `hollow_camp.md`*

Past the ordinary old growth, the trees turn pale and warm. These are the god's veins, stood up out of the Hide and
grown into a forest: trunks that go up out of sight, warm a hand's depth in, with a slow beat under the bark. Cut one
and it weeps, and the cut closes by morning. Every tenth trunk has grown round something the god was carrying, and every
root lies combed one way, toward where the Root Deep breathes. The roads were there first; the Wood grew away from them.

It is the act's first forest of the god: towering, open, grim and quiet. Woodcutters, fungus-pickers and one hunter live
at its edges, and two of its hamlets are empty now, emptied in two different ways. The god shows plainly, though far
less than in the Deep.

**The one image:** the pilgrim small at the foot of a pale trunk that goes up out of the frame, one hand on the bark; at
the tenth trunk along the road, the lip of a bell showing in the wood.

## Found in the world

*In-game writing: texts a pilgrim can find here, in the world's own voices.*

### The tenth trunk

*Told by the fungus-pickers in their hollow to their children, as a copyist who wintered with them wrote it down. The
hunter of the Wood added the last lines.*

When the god fell, it was carrying things: a bell in its hand, a candle in its mouth, and other things besides. They
fell from it when it struck the ground and lay scattered where the Wood is now. And because the god was dying, its veins
came up out of the ground the way a drowning man's hands come up out of the water, reaching for what it had dropped.
Each vein that found a thing closed round it, and stood up, and became a tree.

Why one trunk in ten, the children always ask. Because the god let go with both hands, the pickers say, and there are
ten fingers on two hands.

The first woodcutter in the Wood knew none of this. He cut whatever came to hand, and the trunks wept clear and closed
by morning, and he cut again. On the tenth his axe struck metal. He split the wood and lifted out a bell, and it rang
once in his hands, though he had not swung it, and every trunk in the Wood leaned toward him at once, the way a crowded
room turns to look at a man. He put the bell back and ran. By morning the wood had closed over it, all but the lip. It
is there still, by the road, if you count.

So we count, and leave every tenth one be.

*The hunter's lines:* My father told it so, and I believed him. But on my line a tenth has grown round a man's ribs with
a pilgrim's badge still in them, warm, and the god carried no pilgrims. Last winter I counted a tenth grown round a
lantern, its flame turned toward the road. The year before, that trunk was a ninth. The Wood is still taking things in.

### Fourteen hearths

*A leaf from the day-book of a brother of the Pale Order sent to a hamlet at the Wood's edge in the spring after it was
emptied. It was found pressed in a book at his bone-house.*

I was sent with the captain to cut the count of the dead in the hamlet at the Wood's edge.

There were no dead. Every door in the lane stood open. The beds still held the shape of the sleepers, boots stood at the
hearths, shawls hung on the pegs, and there was nothing to count: no bone, no tooth, no nail, not even a smell. I took
the strip of bone into every house, and it came out as clean as it went in. I have never before brought a strip back
clean.

One man is left, a woodcutter. He had dug fourteen graves by the old stone and laid in each a shawl or a pair of boots
from a house, because there was nothing else to lay. He had things from thirteen houses. In the fourteenth grave lies a
small glass, face down. He says the hamlet was fourteen hearths. I counted thirteen houses.

He gave us the names, Tam, and Wenna who owned the glass, and the Hollis boys, and Gethin, and on, twelve names. At the
thirteenth his mouth opened and nothing came out. In his own house a short coat hangs by the door with its sleeves
turned up, and a second pallet lies made up by the hearth, and he does not know whose. He splits his kindling small now,
for small hands, and cannot stop.

The captain said one word to me in the old tongue, a number, and told me to write it and say nothing. It is on the back
of this leaf. We did not stay the night.

When anything of the god dies, it leaves us something to lay. This left nothing. I know of only one thing that leaves
nothing. Every faith on the Hide is afraid of it, and in that hamlet I understood why.

### The pickers' hollow

*Spoken by the hunter of the Wood, under the great ribs over his hollow, to a pilgrim who had lost the road; the pilgrim
wrote it down at the next ring of stones. The pickers called the hunter Tenth, for the trunks he kept to.*

Sit on the flat stone. The moss is soft, and there is nothing under it.

The next hollow over belonged to the pickers, four families of them. The white caps hold a little light, the way a coal
holds it under ash, and grow where the ground is sound. The blue hold a colder light and grow over something hollow;
step round them. The red give no light at all. They grow where the god still bleeds underneath, and look wet in the
driest spell, and nobody picks them.

Once in a long while the ash at the ring of stones lies still, and the whole Wood leans the other way. Nobody hunts
then. The pickers laid white caps on every flat stone and sat in their doorways to wait for the ash to lift. My father
called those days a bad year; the pickers called them holy. In my life there has been one such time, nine days long. On
the tenth the ash lifted and the Wood leaned back, and I went down to their hollow to tell them. They were sitting in
their doorways, all four families, and the caps on the stones had gone grey. They did not get up.

People who come through say the same thing took them that took the hamlet at the Wood's edge. At the hamlet there was
nothing left to bury. At the hollow there was a great deal. I have left them lying, each with a stick at the head, and I
watch them. Fresh, a body lies any way it fell. A day on, its head is toward the ring. In three days it has moved a
hand's width toward it, and nobody drags them. I keep their fire going in the iron bin. It is only manners.

Further in is a glade the pickers never went near, where somebody long ago held rites to bring the god back up out of
the ground. Its candles are burning still, and the wax runs red. Whether the rites failed, or worked and that is all
that came of it, nobody can tell you.

And where the Wood thins into tall grass, keep your face to the grass. Something the woodcutters call the Stalker Crone
keeps there, and comes when you look away.

## For building it

**The land.** Real old growth, turned pale.
- **Its structure,** after Białowieża: every age at once, dead wood half the forest, pit and mound. The start's
  rainforest (`the-red-shore.md`) runs into it through a band where spruce and cedar stand dying among the first pale
  trunks.
- **The vein-trees:** 50 to 70 m, crowns always out of frame, trunks 2 to 4 m across, fluted and buttressed along the
  vein-ridges. Their joined roots are real in Pando, the Utah aspen grove that is one root system under some forty
  thousand trunks: here every root runs downhill, joins the next and lies combed one way.
- **Felt from under it,** in Diablo II's camera: open corridors and clearings, no clumps, small trees mostly dead,
  fallen veins withering by stages of drying (`hollow_wood_seeded.md`).
- **The floor is never bare:** pale litter in many tones, bark strips, twigs, brown and olive mosses at the giants'
  feet, grey lichen, the three caps, and the combed root-threads as real relief. No green carpets.

**Landmarks**, each by the pit method:
- **The tenth trunks** (placed by rule): a bell's lip, a skull with the wood in at the eyes, a candle, a man's ribs with
  a pilgrim's badge, a lantern. Each is rolled in smooth woundwood, as real trees swallow fences and gravestones; the
  flames are hidden lights deep in the bark.
- **The Ribcage Bough over the pickers' hollow** (`hollow_camp.py`, C+): a valley whose slumped banks show the god's
  hide, with ribs a yard thick arching 10 to 14 yards overhead; the pickers' huts; the dead lying heads toward the ring,
  each with a stick. Light: the iron bin's fire on the ribs' undersides.
- **The emptied hamlet at the Wood's edge,** as the brother's leaf has it; one grave of the fourteen stays dry in a lane
  of mud, and its only light is one window's candle, leaning toward a second pallet.
- **The Niche Candle:** a tenth trunk whose bark has drawn back from its candle until the hollow stands man-high.
  Pilgrims' candles on its sill burn down to wax runs and soot; the one inside never has.
- **The Sword-in-Root:** a root-flare grown round a sword to the hilt, woundwood rolled up the blade, an old run of
  dried blood below it; black, pitted iron, lit by one shaft of moon through a gap. The hunters say the hilt sits a
  finger lower each spring.
- **The Vigil, the altar glade** (`vigil.py`, B-): an altar in a hollow under a pointed arch, a spiral of runes, red-wax
  candles that never go out because the sap dripping from the roof sets into wax as it falls, wax pouring into blood in
  the carr's hollows, tendrils creeping up the altar tree. The wax is red; the flames are warm.

**Light and weather.** Overcast by day, the moon through gaps by night, flecks under closed crowns; the lantern is the
key light. White caps glow faintly warm, blue faintly cold, red not at all. The crowns shelter: fine rain only in the
gaps, heavy drips under the giants, stemflow down the flutes, dry ground in the lee of the buttresses. When the Deep
breathes out, every flame in the Wood leans the same way. No fog.

**The god showing through.** Plainly, still restrained: the warm, beating trunks, weeping eyes on some dying trees, the
hide in slumped banks, the ribs.

**Creatures.**
- **Husks** (103): posts among the trunks, pale among pale, until three gather; fight them between two buttresses.
- **Vein-Borers** (30): rings of dark red bubbles in soft ground, never on a road or a flat stone.
- **Tithe-Hands** (24): round behind; put a buttress at your back. **Weepers** (23): their needles stick in the bark, so
  a trunk is full cover.
- **Bloatlings** (18): draw them into the Husks. **Gasps** (16): the lantern and the tenth trunks' flames hold them off.
- **Wick-Saints of the Canopy** (8): drop out of the crowns at dusk.
- **Pyre-Saints** (6): fire runs from their footprints over dry litter and fallen husks; the living trees, warm and wet,
  never take it.

**Sound.** Nothing calling, nothing singing. Trunks ticking in the cold; drips from far above; insects at dusk; a slow
pulse when the pilgrim stands against a trunk; a bell, rarely, far off; in the emptied hamlet at evening, one high
call.

**Keep out.**
- **The Blind Face:** Derek struck it from the lore (2026-10-07: "no face, scratch it from the lore too").
- Any animal or animal word (insects are allowed; the Wood's floating lights are spores and foxfire).
- Fog, including the ground fog the Vigil scene still carries; red light; a cut tenth trunk.

**Open questions.**
- **The white stripes:** keep, soften, or give them a cause (bark stretched paler over each vein-ridge)?
- **Some trees mostly straight:** straight in the crowded heart where they race for light, wandering at gaps and edges?
- **The sap:** the law says dark blood; the lore says the pale wood weeps clear, though a carver's eleventh trunk "ran
  red down the blade". Proposal: clear from a fresh cut, dark blood where the red caps grow and wherever it dries.
- **The Stalker Crone** keeps to the Wood's tall grass in the lore; the game spawns her only on the Pilgrim Road.
- **A door down:** the lore combs the roots toward a mouth of the Root Deep in the Wood's north; the game has none.
