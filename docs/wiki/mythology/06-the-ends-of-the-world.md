# The ends of the world

Every people foretells an end. Each is one people's view of the next turning of the wheel, and all of them have
happened before (`00-the-deep-lore.md`). None of them mentions the pilgrim.

## The Pale Order: the Setting Down

When the Count closes at the Last Number, the Mother will set the world down.

Everything finished will stay with her: the laid bone, the closed counts, the dust. Everything unfinished will be given
to the wind: the flesh still growing, the breath still leaving, every name.

And the sky, which is the Order's dust, will come down at last and lie over her like a cloth over a face. Then she will
rest. The brothers use the word *rest* of nothing else but dust and her.

At the end of every counting of the walls, the brothers stand and name every other end of the world that any faith has
ever told, one by one, and condemn each of them aloud. It takes until dark.

*Behind the myth.* The Count never comes nearer its last number. Every year it comes out higher, by more than the
year's dead.

## The Gilded Peak: the Pause Completed

The held breath will be let go.

What remains will be the Silence the breath was held in, which held the meaning all along. Nothing will be destroyed.
The sentence will be finished, and a finished sentence has been said, and does not need saying again.

The monks who were below the mountain when it fell keep the last office for those who were above. When the end comes,
they will be the empty space outside it. They will hold nothing, so that nothing is kept wrongly.

## The Veiled Crone: the Still Winter

One winter, every thread that ever went through the eye of the loom will come back up its valley at once. Every soul
that ever died will stand at its own door, in the snow, and ask which way.

The Unveiled say that then the dead will stop at their own faces, at last, and be home. The Covered say the loom will
finish its cloth and fold it, as the circles fold the year's cloth at midwinter, one row knitted for every death.

Either way, nothing will move again. The candle will stop burning down.

## The Bleeding Maiden: the Filling

The flesh will fill every hollow: every wound and every mouth, every grave and every room, and the gap between one word
and the next. When nothing is hollow anywhere, there will be no *then*. That will be the Mother made whole.

The processions keep the name of every knight of the Pale Order in their litanies, so that she will know whom to take
first. The lowland children, who know nothing of the litanies, end their counting game with the rhyme's last verse:

> *Who will open the Tall Red Hat?*
> *Mother will.*

*Behind the myth.* The gap between one word and the next is where the Silence is. The Filling is the one end that would
close the Silence too, and the processions say it carefully.

## The Myriad: the Last Breath Out

The out-breath will finish.

Every small god will settle like dust where it lies: on the stones, in the fords, in the mouths of the bells. Nothing
will be riding the breath, because there will be no breath left to ride.

Then a purifier who walked down into the water long before will come back up out of it, horned, and breathe the world
in, once, as the last rite. The world will be folded like a paper figure, its arms left open, and set down on a step.

And nothing will come to breathe on it.

The bells of the wet country have been heard to ring a new way in this age. The purifiers say they ring *out is closer
than it was*.

## The Second Mouth: the Walking

When enough of the faithful carry the Mother inside them, she will walk out of the world in them: many bodies, and one
will.

The processions call that the end of the world. The cult calls it the beginning, finishing.

## The Tithed: Somebody Always Sits Up

The common people have no end of their own. They have heard the faiths' ends on the road, and do not know which is
right, and are not asked.

What they know is smaller. The lanterns go out one at a time. Every lantern keeps a little of everyone it carries back,
and even a kept soul does not last forever, so each year there are a few less lanterns on the roads. One day there will
be one lantern left, on one road, with one person under it.

And somebody will sit up with it, the way the Tithed sit up with the dying, and the newborn, and the dead before the
barrow. Somebody always sits up.

## The shore folk: When the Tide Stops

One day the Red will go back down the black sand and not come up again. It will go down past the rocks and the stacks,
farther than anyone has seen the sand go, and keep going, back up into the Fallen One's side, and the wound will close.

Some keepers say that then the Fallen One will get up. The forest will slide off its back like rain off a roof, all the
great trees at once. The spirits will have nowhere left to live but in people, and everything the Red ever kept will be
standing on the dry sand, looking up.

Other keepers say the Fallen One will not get up: the tide stopping only means it has finished dying at last. Then no
more dead will come up out of the water, because there will be no water. The dead will lie down for good, the living
will have nothing left to burn, the fire will go out, and it will be quiet.

## The older voices

**The Choir** is waiting for the out-breath to finish. Then there will be a rest, and they will come in on the next line.
They are practising it.

**The Keeper of the Spiral Hollow** keeps the law of after: *The Holder will hear again. Then it will answer.*

**The Deep** says, courteously, that soon it will breathe in.

**The builders** cut one last thing over the empty seat at their deepest pillar: *Now one of you.*

**The Slayers** cut on the walls of their temple deep in the body: *This place is a thought, and it is forgetting what is
in it, one thing at a time. When it has forgotten everything, it will be a thought that remembers no one. That is rest.
We are helping.*

## The signs

These are what every people can see, now, in the year 1114:
- The Pale Order's Count rises every year.
- The lanterns on the roads are fewer every year.
- The rings with teeth are coming nearer to each other, and someone is still cutting them.
- The wind goes out and never comes back.
- The Red Water's tide still beats.
- The courtyard of the fallen Peak is still warm.
- The stair in the shore folk's temple has not been swept.
- A pilgrim is walking who does not know where it came from.

*Behind the myth.* Every end in this page has happened before, in some turn of the world, and every one will happen
again. At the end of this turn the pilgrim takes up the mantle of what it came to end, and becomes the next one
(`00-the-deep-lore.md`). Whichever end this turn's people see coming, it will look to them like their own prophecy, come
true.
