# The ages

The world is older than anyone can say, and nobody on the Hide counts its years: the peoples tell time by what
happened, the year of the grey breath, the winter the dead came back asking, before the Peak fell, in my grandmother's
time. Everything here has an order and no numbers, and most of it is known only through myths that disagree. The age
names are working names; Derek chooses the final ones.

**One turn of the wheel.** These ages are one turn of a cycle that has always turned (`00-the-deep-lore.md`). A world
is told into being, grows loud with gods, starves, dies, and lies in its last breath until something ends it. Then the
Silence holds the pause until the next telling begins. The world has ended many times, and nothing remembers the turns
before. This page tells only this turn.

## The Silence

Before there was anything, there was the Silence. It held every meaning there could ever be, the way the pause before a sentence holds what the sentence is for. It had no edge, and nothing in it
could be named, so nothing in it could stand. It is still there. Every god, every age and every word in this bible
is held inside it the way a word is held between two pauses.

## The Lying Down

Something came to lie in the Silence. What it was, nobody knows, and we leave it unknown. It was vast past any seeing:
its skin a country, its bones mountain ranges, its breath a weather. The peoples who came later called it a god because
"god" was the largest word they had. It may never have known that anything lived on it.

On it, in that unnumbered time, the first peoples lived and feared. Where they came from, nobody can say. They feared
the dark, the open, the cold, the hunger of things larger than themselves, and they prayed, and here the oldest law of
the world began to work: **a belief held long enough by enough people grows a body.** Fear and worship are food to a
god; a god that nobody remembers starves. And belief does not pass through a god and away: it settles, in the bones of
anything long feared or long prayed to, and hardens there into **marrow**.

## The Age of the Ythraal

The Ythraal walked. Every great fear had grown a body, and every body ruled the people whose fear had made it.

- **The Thrae,** three fingers of something from beyond the outside, reaching in, which ate the shape of things
  without ever knowing the world was there. The herald, a great dead thing of bone with fire in its ribs, fought them;
  they never noticed. One lay down and is the bleeding mountains above the Red; the other two went. The herald fell
  into a crater, was worshipped there, and ate its worshippers and then itself: the Starved Crown.
- **The Famines:** god-corpses the height of hills that kept walking, eating the land bare, into which whole kingdoms
  climbed to live. One still walks, with Dhum's lamps lit in its side.
- **The Needle,** a spike of iron in a forest, full of a slow digesting liquid, bones and great eyes, which drinks
  dreams.
- **The reaper,** grown from the fear that the dead would never lie down: it reaped them, then the living, carried an
  age's dead down a pit and lay on them to count.
- **The shell** far out under the Red, whose sleeper's breath is the tide.
- **The serpent of the wet country,** dead before anyone counted: its rot is the wet country, its stone spine the road.

The peoples of the age:
- the cities in the sky, whose builders nobody knows: one great city, Qhaloth, burned the world's marrow and then the
  marrow of its lesser cities' people to climb, cut them loose (Ghenn and Sorrath fell, their people hollow: the
  Ghauul), and rose until it stopped, and is the dark moon, dead, rusting, ruled by one lich;
- the Unison, a single mind in many bodies, in the mire;
- the Hallowed, who live as long as a people keeps their name in its prayers;
- the Angles, inside the thing, whose halls have too many corners;
- in the Deep, Vesk, the iron city that sank and was held up by its people, fused into its legs.

Rule by terror was the order of things. A city that trembles is a city that feeds its god. And every power anyone
had was marrow dug out of the ground, which was the god's own life.

## The Wasting

It ended the way it had to. The marrow was gone out of half the world, and the peoples who fed the Ythraal died of the
waste, or stopped believing, or were eaten by what they believed in. The Ythraal wasted away. **A god that wastes to
its end breaks apart into lesser things**, each carrying one of its hungers: these are the Wants, and they bargain,
because a bargain is the nearest thing to a prayer they can get. The peoples fed their gods pain when they had nothing
else: processions of penance, and the iron maidens standing in rows in the wastes. Some penitents were believed into
something more, a saint or a Want or a god for a winter. The cities emptied. Knowledge died with the hands that held it.

Some powers did not die. They slept, or went under, or kept walking because nobody told them to stop. The Starved Crown
ate its worshippers and then itself to stay awake. One Famine still walks, and Vesk walks in the Deep. The pit at the
dead serpent's throat was still being fed in blood when the god died, and long after.

## The Held Space

All that time, inside the thing beneath, there was a space held open, and in it there was no night. Light lay evenly
over everything and came from nowhere. In it:
- the Choir sang the thing what it was, in turns, so that the note was never dropped;
- the Angles cut halls with too many corners and sigils that hurt the eyes;
- high in the dark, a court of white faces judged every thought.

Whether the Held Space was the thing's life, a dream it had, or a story told by things that need one, no voice
agrees.

## The first morning

The thing beneath stopped. Something that cannot be named pressed in and took its names one at a time; or the faithful
divided it; or something from outside came in where it was open; or it grew tired and the Silence came in to let it lie
down. Every people tells it differently (`04-the-held-space.md`). Some say it is not dead.

In the grey of the next morning the people woke on the ash of its hide, cold, scattered, with no names and no memory of
lying down. How they came there, nobody knows, and every hearth tells it its own way (`02-the-creation-myths.md`).
Everyone's memory begins there.

## The Age of the Last Breath: in order

The world now is a corpse that has not finished dying. Its last breath is still going out, its bone still stands, its
flesh still grows without a mind, its threads still pull, and things still come up out of it. The faiths of this age
keep the dark back with a soul in a jar for a lamp.

What happened, in the order the tellings agree on, as near as anyone can set it. No one on the Hide dates any of it.
- **The first morning.** The people wake on the ash. On the first night the things that come up stand round them, and
  take the tenth loaf, and go. The sky is grey and stays grey. (`02`, `04`, `05`)
- **The first lantern.** A soul chooses to be kept, in glass, as a flame. (`05`)
- **The burning heath.** The heath below the moor burns through a whole round of seasons, and nobody lit it. (`05`)
- **The first Laying.** Brothers on the pale ridges begin laying bones, each in its place, and the Count begins. They
  say they stood beside the Mother as she fell. (`05`)
- **The spreading.** The people go out over the Hide. The barrow rings rise, nine to a ring. The old powers that
  outlived the Wasting hunt them. (`05`)
- **The pit fed again.** In the wet country, people find the long-dead thing lying through the bog and feed the pit at
  its throat in blood, as the peoples before them did. (`05`)
- **The first cup.** At a wound in the ground that does not close, the first cup of blood is poured. The processions of
  the Bleeding Maiden reckon everything from it. (`03`, `05`)
- **The numbered stones.** Stones with figures that count down are set along the Pilgrim Road, by people who found the
  road already lying there. (`05`)
- **The refusal.** A keeper refuses to give her daughter to the pit and leads her people up into the trees above the
  Red. Nobody feeds the pit after that, and its red glows on. (`05`)
- **The lantern-makers hunted.** The faiths hunt the makers of lanterns out. Their lanterns stay on every road. (`05`)
- **The dead walk back.** The shore folk's dead, given to the Red in canoes, walk back up the black sand; from then on
  the shore folk burn their dead. (`05`)
- **The Peak begins.** A bell-caster hears what is behind a bell's note and climbs a mountain of bone. (`05`)
- **The circles sit down.** The circles of the Veiled Crone sit down to say her names, and learn that a soul cannot
  pass its own reflection. (`05`)
- **The bridge.** Masons build the bridge over the vein beyond the Root Deep. The far crew falls silent mid-song. (`05`)
- **The Well-Shrine.** The first purifiers keep the Drowned Fen's one clean pool, and the yearly Cutting-Away begins.
  (`05`)
- **The second mouth.** A nameless woman cuts the first second mouth, and the cult begins inside the processions.
  (`05`)
- **The grey year.** The Fen gives back its wrong breath all at once. Six purifiers come up horned. (`05`)
- **The mirror that showed nothing.** A circle of the Veiled Crone meets something that shows nothing in a mirror, and
  is never seen again. (`05`)
- **The hatching.** A chapter of the processions built along a great wound sees the wound crust over and hatch; its
  procession goes out and never comes home. (`05`)
- **The emptied hamlet.** At the Hollow Wood's edge, a hamlet is emptied by something that leaves nothing behind.
  (`05`)
- **The fall of the Peak.** The Gilded Peak falls in a handful of nights. Its courtyard becomes black glass. (`05`)
- **The loose winter.** The dead do not go through, and come back asking "Which way?". The Veiled Crone's circles
  split into the Shroud and the Mirror. (`05`)
- **The siege of the shore.** The dead of other places come up out of the Red at the shore folk's village, winter
  after winter. (`05`, `areas/the-red-shore.md`)
- **Now.** The village's wall falls; the last of the shore folk die on their temple's platform. (`05`)
