# The ages

The world is older than any count. The peoples of the Hide number their years only from the moment the god died, and
it is now the year **1114** of that count. Everything before has an order and no numbers, and most of it is known only
through myths that disagree. The age names here are working names from the Lore Bible; Derek chooses the final ones.

**One turn of the wheel.** These ages are one turn of a cycle that has always turned (`00-the-deep-lore.md`). A world
is told into being, grows loud with gods, starves, dies, and lies in its last breath until something ends it. Then the
Silence holds the pause until the next telling begins. The world has ended many times, and nothing remembers the turns
before. This page tells only this turn.

## The Silence

Before there was anything, there was the Silence. It held every meaning there could ever be, the way the pause before a sentence holds what the sentence is for. It had no edge, and nothing in it
could be named, so nothing in it could stand. It is still there. Every god, every age and every word in this bible
is held inside it the way a word is held between two pauses.

## The Uncounted

Something came to lie in the Silence. What it was, nobody knows, and we leave it unknown. It was vast past any seeing:
its skin a country, its bones mountain ranges, its breath a weather. The peoples who came later called it a god because
"god" was the largest word they had. It may never have known that anything lived on it.

On it, in that unnumbered time, the first peoples lived and feared. Where they came from, nobody can say. They feared
the dark, the open, the cold, the hunger of things larger than themselves, and they prayed, and here the oldest law of
the world began to work: **a belief held long enough by enough people grows a body.** Fear and worship are food to a
god; a god that nobody remembers starves. And belief does not pass through a god and away: it settles, in the bones of
anything long feared or long prayed to, and hardens there into **marrow**.

## The Age of Terrors

The loud gods walked. Every great fear had grown a body, and every body ruled the people whose fear had made it.

- **The dragons,** born of the terror of a world without walls. They were one power among many, perhaps the greatest,
  perhaps only the ones that lasted longest.
- **The Famines:** god-corpses the height of hills that kept walking, into which whole kingdoms climbed to worship.
- **The tower** filled with one thing full of eyes.
- **The cities** that worshipped their walls until the walls stood up.
- **The reaper** asleep in a cavern floored with an age's dead.
- **The shell** under a delta, filtering blood.
- **The serpent of the wet country,** fed in blood.
- **The lords:** kings, abbots and matrons believed into small godhood.

The peoples of the age left ruins and little else:
- the climbers, who built a stair to climb out of the sky;
- the One Swallow, a single mind in many bodies, in the mire;
- the tall hollow ones made of wanting;
- the builders whose halls fit no human body.

Rule by terror was the order of things. A city that trembles is a city that feeds its god.

## The Starving

It ended the way it had to. The peoples who fed the loud gods died, or stopped believing, or were eaten by what they
believed in. The gods starved. **A god that starves to the end breaks apart into lesser things**, as a dead thing breaks
in the heat into flies, and those lesser things are the demons: hungry for what their god was fed and unable to get it,
so they bargain, because a bargain is the nearest thing to a prayer they can get. The cities emptied. Knowledge died
with the hands that held it.

Some powers did not die. They slept, or went under, or kept walking because nobody told them to stop. The last dragon
ate its worshippers and then itself to stay awake. One Famine still walks. The serpent of the wet country was still
being fed in blood when the god died, and long after.

## The Held Space

All that time, inside the thing beneath, there was a space held open, and in it there was no night. Light lay evenly
over everything and came from nowhere. In it:
- the Choir sang the thing what it was, in turns, so that the note was never dropped;
- the builders cut stairs and long niches, in proportions no human body fits;
- high in the dark, a court of white faces judged every thought.

At the very last, a congregation knelt there and sang. They are the ancestors of every person now alive on the Hide.
Whether they had always been inside, or walked in from the starving world through its mouth carrying their salt, is
told both ways. Whether the Held Space was the thing's life, a dream it had, or a story told by things that need one,
no voice agrees.

## The Death, year 0

The thing beneath stopped. Something that cannot be named pressed in and took its names one at a time; or the faithful divided it; or
something from outside came in where it was open; or it grew tired and the Silence came in to let it lie down. Every
people tells it differently (`04-the-held-space.md`). Some say it is not dead.

The congregation took hold of each other's belts in the dark and walked out through its mouth onto the ash of its
cheek, on its last breath. Most of them. The common count begins here.

## The Age of the Last Breath: the chronicle

The world now is a corpse that has not finished dying. Its last breath is still going out, its bone still stands, its
flesh still grows without a mind, its threads still pull, and things still come up out of it. The faiths of this age
keep the dark back with a soul in a jar for a lamp.
