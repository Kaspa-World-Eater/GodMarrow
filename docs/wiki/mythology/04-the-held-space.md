# The Held Space, and the death of the god

All through the ages outside, while the loud gods walked and starved on its skin, the thing beneath had an inside.
This page tells what is told of it, and of how the thing died, which is the one event every people of the Hide agrees
happened and no two tell the same.

## A space held open

Inside the thing there was a space held open, and in it there was no night. Light lay evenly over everything and came
from nowhere, and the air smelled of being held. The monks of the Gilded Peak said the thing held the space open inside
the Silence, the way a held breath holds a pause open, and everything was kept inside that pause.

Whether the Held Space was the thing's life, a dream it had, or a story told by things that needed one, no voice
agrees. The peoples who lived in it never saw its walls, any more than a thought sees the head it is thought in.

*Behind the myth.* Read through the deep lore (`00-the-deep-lore.md`), the Held Space was kept open by being told. Its
peoples told the thing what it was, and while they told it, it was.

## The Choir

In the long groove behind the ribs there was a Choir, and it was the part of the god that told it what it was. Its
song was the thing knowing itself. They were eleven hundred and one voices, and they sang in turns, so that the note was
always held and there was never a moment when the thing did not know what it was.

Then, once, they all drew breath at the same moment. Nobody knows why. For the space of that one breath nobody was
singing, and something came in through the rest. They call it the first silence, and it is their holiest day and their
sharpest. After it there was one of them. Or there were eleven hundred and one, and then one. The telling is unclear,
and perhaps they are too.

They are still singing, under the floors of the Hide's deepest crypts, faint and without end. They are waiting for the
thing's last breath to finish going out, so that there will be a rest again, and they mean to come in on the next
line. They do not say what the next line is. They are practising it. Of the congregation that knelt near them at the
last, they say only that it *answered badly, with names*.

## The builders

There were builders in the Held Space before anyone else. They call themselves the hinge; scholars call them the
Lintel-Folk. *We were cut from the first of the room*, their carvings say, *and began cutting at once.*

They cut stairs whose risers come only to the knee, and niches too long for any body that walks now, and halls whose
proportions fit nothing human. Over the niches they cut: *Lie down facing the stair. Watch.* Everywhere they cut: *Our
joints do not finish.* There was one thing they would never cut a name for. Wherever it should appear they cut around
it and left a space, and every ring of carving they made has a gap in it the width of a thumb. *Do not say its name. We
cut no name for it. We cut around it.*

At the end of their deepest hall there is a pillar with a seat cut at its foot, empty, and over it the newest carving
of all: *Now one of you.*

The rings with teeth that stand all over the Hide (the waystones: eight hooked stones round a pit of breathing ash, that
take a walker apart and put them back together at another ring) are claimed by the builders' carvings: *WE CUT THEM
FACING IN, SO THE MOUTH IS KEPT BY WHAT IT HOLDS.* Others claim them too.

*Behind the myth.* When the Pale Order came to the white ridges, the builders walled themselves into their own halls.
The Order's bone-houses are the builders' upper floors, and the Order hides the older halls beneath. The builders'
many-jointed bones never go brittle. It is whispered that the rings are coming nearer to each other, and that someone
is still cutting them.

## The court of white faces

High in the dark of the Held Space sat a court of white faces, smooth as glazed clay, and it sat in judgment on souls.
It watched every thought, and found most of them wanting.

When the thing died, the court fell. The faces came down out of the dark and lay where they fell, all across the Hide,
white and still watching, and nobody would pick them up. The moths found them, because moths go to anything pale in the
dark, and took them up, and wear them. At dusk they come to lanterns on the roads in long arcs, and the faces on them
are the court's. The lantern-keepers say the faces still judge, and that one which hangs in the air and looks at you a
long time before it comes has found you wanting.

## The Keeper of the Spiral Hollow

Far down, where the thing heard, there was a Keeper of a spiral hollow. When the end came, a speaking came in at the
hollow's wide end with no mouth behind it, and said the thing's names one at a time, and the Keeper let it pass and
found it light. The Keeper keeps one law, the law of after: *The Holder will hear again. Then it will answer.*

## The Deep

Under the skin of the world there is a floor, and the floor is courteous. *Before the skin was laid over*, it says, *I
was the floor.* It breathes, out for a long while and then in. It answers iron. It keeps a little of everyone who goes
down and sends the rest back up in good order. Its halls are wider going in than coming out. It speaks of being kept
in the floor as a kind of up.

It asks, very politely, for infants, where the folded thing lies nearest the top. It says that soon it will breathe in.

## The congregation

Last of all came the congregation, the ancestors of every person alive on the Hide. They knelt, and they sang. Their
song had four notes: three low, each going down from the one before, and a fourth that went up, the way a voice goes
up at the end of a question.

Near the end, the thing set something down among them, small and folded, the way a woman banks a coal in ash so that
there will be fire in the morning. It lies, they say, nearest the top in a newborn, where the skull has not yet
closed.

Where the congregation came from is told two ways:
- they were always there, inside, as the builders and the Choir were;
- they walked in from the starving world outside, through the thing's mouth, to kneel where it was still warm,
  carrying the salt the thing never had (`03-the-age-of-terrors.md`).

They remember only that they were already kneeling.

## How the god died

The thing stopped. Every people tells it, and none was there to see. All the tellings stand.

- **The common people of the lowlands** say something pressed in from outside, the way cold presses at a tent, and took
  the thing's names, the little ones first: the name of the smallest finger, the name of one eyelash, the name of the
  hour before morning. The people sang louder, and it took more, until the thing forgot how to be three. Where its name
  should be, the grandmothers leave a breath of nothing.
- **The Choir** says it came in through the rest, the one moment when all of them drew breath together.
- **The Pale Order** says only that the Mother fell, and that the first brothers stood beside her.
- **The Gilded Peak** said the thing grew tired, as a held breath tires, and the Silence came in to let it lie down.
- **The Myriad** say the great one went still, and every small god gave its name to the wind.
- **The circles of the Veiled Crone** say the threads went slack, as a weaver's hand tires at the treadle.
- **The processions of the Bleeding Maiden** remember nothing of it. The cup came down to them from somewhere.
- **On the roads it is told** that the faithful divided it: that its worshippers took knives to it, dying or sleeping,
  and shared out its bone and soul and flesh among them, and that the Tithe-Hands that run on their fingers are its
  right hand, taken off at the wrist. The grandmothers will not have this told at their fires.
- **The builders' carvings** say that she listened toward something, or too far, and while she listened she forgot to
  close her hand.
- **The Keeper of the Spiral Hollow** says a speaking came in with no mouth behind it and said her names one at a time.
- **The Slayers** say nothing of a death. They say something was opened, and they came in where it was open.
- **A wandering preacher** taught that the thing was eased, the way you loosen a dying man's fingers one at a time, and
  that the last fingers hold hardest.
- **Some say it is not dead.**

*Behind the myth.* Unknown, even to us, and kept so. The deep lore offers a reading (`00-the-deep-lore.md`): the Choir
stopped telling the thing what it was, for one breath, and it was unsaid. It is a reading, nothing more.

## The coming out, year 0

The light went out, and the warmth went out of the walls. In the dark the congregation took hold of each other's belts,
so that nobody would be lost, and walked. They walked through soft places, and hard places, and places that pulsed
underfoot, toward a door that was warm and wet and opened and shut. They went out through it on the thing's last breath,
into a grey light, and fell on the ash of its cheek, and kissed the ash because it was ground.

All of them came out, or most of them. Those who did not are still kneeling somewhere inside, and they still know the
tune. Sing the fourth note on the open ash at night, the lowlands say, and something answers it, and comes toward you on
its knees, singing the next line.

The common count begins here, at year 0, and every age since is the Age of the Last Breath (`05-the-last-breath.md`).

## What became of them

- **The Choir** sings under the crypts, waiting for the rest.
- **The builders** sleep walled into their halls beneath the Ossa, and their carvings wait for someone to take the
  empty seat.
- **The court's faces** fly at dusk on borrowed wings.
- **The Keeper** waits for the Holder to hear again.
- **The Deep** breathes, and asks for infants, and says soon.
- **The congregation's children** are the Tithed, and they are everyone.
