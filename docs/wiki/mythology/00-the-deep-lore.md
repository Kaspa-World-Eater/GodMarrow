# The deep lore

*For us only. Derek, 2026-10-08: "That's like the super deep lore." The game never says any of this. It lets the player
feel it, and make the connections.*

## Everything is told

In this world, what is told becomes. A thought held long enough, a word said often enough, a story repeated by enough
mouths gains weight, and enough weight stands up and walks.

The gods are not real, the way a stone is real, and in the end neither is the stone. They are dreams that manifest
because they think they are real, and because people think they are real. Each one is a story that has started to
believe itself, held up by a people that believes it too. Under the skin of the world runs something like an
existential crisis that nobody is awake enough to have.

> "Everything in this world is simply narrative. Even the gods aren't real. They're simply dreams. Manifesting because
> they think they're real. People think they're real ... Thoughts and words can manifest and gain power in this world
> and that's why they say the god has a thousand faces and 1,000 hands." (Derek)

**The god has a thousand faces and a thousand hands.** Every face any people ever gave it, it wears; every hand
anyone ever feared, it grew. The world shows it everywhere:
- the Tithe-Hands are its hands, running loose;
- the Weepers have lost their faces;
- the white faces of the old court are worn by moths;
- every faith has carved it a face, and every carved face is believed into a little life.

This is the oldest law of the bible ("a belief held long enough grows a body", `03-the-age-of-terrors.md`) seen from
the inside. Belief builds a god out of the telling alone, and there is nothing underneath it.

## Everything is everything

Everything reflects everything else. What anyone sees is themselves: projected outward, recognised, and given an
identity. Named, the reflection answers. Answered, it stands up.

> "Everything is everything, reflecting off one another and people still think what they [are] seeing but it's really
> them projecting themselves and recognizing that and providing identity to those things." (Derek)

So each faith's god is the faith itself, looked at in the bone, the blood or the wind:
- **the Bearing Mother** is the brothers' own need to be carried, seen in bone;
- **the Bleeding Maiden** is the processions' own wound;
- **the Veiled Crone** is the circles' fear of being forgotten;
- **the small gods** are the shrine-keepers' own breath, seen in stones;
- **the Fallen One** is the shore folk lying down at the end of everything;
- **the creatures that come up** out of the god are the people's fears, given back to them with teeth.

The world already rhymes with this, and the player can find the rhymes:
- a soul cannot pass its own reflection;
- the Red Water is a mirror nobody can cover;
- a reading-stand holds a book of your own life, one page ahead;
- the court of white faces judged every thought, and its faces still fly at dusk;
- the dead tell the merchant their names "tomorrow".

## The Silence

If everything is told, the Silence is the untold: the space between words, where every meaning waits, unsaid. It is
the one thing nobody made by speaking, and so the one thing in the world that is not a dream. It holds everything
because it holds nothing.

It has no name that can be said, and the peoples say it many ways:
- **That Which Cannot Be Named;**
- **That Which Cannot Be Held**, as the monks of the Empty Hand say it;
- **the Unmaking;**
- or they say nothing, and leave a pause where its name would go. Written, it is a gap.

Derek: "a fusion of nihilism and daoism." Whatever name can be said of it misses it. It takes no side. It is what
remains when the telling stops. **Never "the Hush"** ("so corny").

**It unmakes by unsaying.** What its servants touch leaves no body, bone, breath or thread, because the telling is
taken out of it, and a thing with no telling never was. A hamlet is emptied and its survivors dig a grave too many,
for someone none of them can remember.

**How the god died, read this way:**
- The Choir told the god what it was, in turns, so that the telling never stopped.
- Once, all of them drew breath at the same moment. For that moment nobody was telling.
- The Silence came in through the rest, and the god was unsaid, one name at a time.

The faiths' other tellings of the death stand beside this one (`04-the-held-space.md`). This is only the reading the
deep lore suggests.

## Mantles

Everything fills a role, and every role is a mantle.

> "Things just fulfill roles. Killing one just leaves another to take [the] mantle. And there are an infinite number of
> mantles that have always existed even if they're merely born that day. They have always existed and they've never
> existed." (Derek)

**Mantles in the world:**
- the Ossuarch, and the Marrow Pontiff kneeling in the well;
- the Ossuary Matron, the Carrion Warden, the Tallow-Mother;
- each power's herald;
- the Starved Crown;
- the merchant at the end of the world;
- the pilgrim.

Kill whoever wears a mantle and another takes it up, though it was born that morning. There is no end to the mantles.

**The faiths half know it:**
- the Pale Order writes its brothers by number and calls the Ossuarch an office of no one man;
- the processions know a penitent only by place in the line, and when one goes into the wall, everyone behind moves up;
- the Gilded Peak kept the last niche of its sanctum empty, always, for whoever would fill it.

**In the game, the bosses and named uniques are mantles.** When one falls, the mantle waits for its next wearer. That
is why they are there again each time the world is walked, and on every harder road.

## The cards

One thing every people has, in every age and every turn of the world, is the tarot.

> "A unifying theme across all people at all times is the use of the tarot, and its myriad of cards. No one even knows
> all the cards or the formations that they can take when drawn." (Derek)

**What the cards are:**
- **Everywhere and always.** The brothers of the bone-houses lay cards, and so do the circles, the processions, the
  shrine-keepers, the shore folk and the grandmothers at the banked fires. Cards are cut into the oldest ruins, by
  peoples who left nothing else anyone can read.
- **Read differently by every people.** Each reads the same cards its own way, and gives them its own names.
- **Never all known.** Nobody knows all the cards. There are always more: a card turns up in a dead man's coat, or in a
  draw, that nobody has seen before. Nobody knows all the formations the cards can take when drawn, either. Every
  spread anyone has learned is one of countless others.
- **The cards are the mantles.** Each card is a role that has always existed and never existed, waiting for whoever
  will wear it: the one who counts, the one who kneels, the one who ends the world. A card drawn is a mantle
  recognised. Read through the deep lore, the tarot is the world's oldest way of telling itself, the faces it gives
  itself laid out face up.

**In the game, the cards are already there:**
- **The Reading** that begins every walk is a tarot reading by the Mysterious Stranger: a nameless pilgrim given a fate
  by the cards.
- **The body board's Major and Minor Arcana** are cards taken into the pilgrim's own body.
- **The Hollow Mystic** works with cards.

Since nobody knows all the cards, the game can always find another.

## The cycle

The world has ended before. It has ended many times. Every age in this bible is one turn of a wheel that has always
turned: a world is told into being, grows loud with gods, starves, dies, and lies in its last breath until something
ends it, and then the Silence holds the pause until the next telling begins. Nothing remembers the turns before, except
perhaps the Silence, which remembers nothing.

The ends of the world that every faith foretells (`06-the-ends-of-the-world.md`) are each one people's view of the
next turning. All of them have happened before.

## The pilgrim

Derek has no story for the game, and none is to be written (2026-10-08: "we're not writing story or quests"). He has a
direction he likes, and story notes toward its end:

> "I like the idea of the hero really being a world killer, slaying the thoughts and dreams, but with no real purpose.
> No meaning." (Derek)

> "Perhaps the hero doesn't have a backstory. Maybe they always were around. Maybe they're just a dream that
> manifested, maybe they just popped out of the digestion warp point thing ... They don't really know ... And at the end
> the cosmic joke is that the world has gone through many world endings, it's a cycle. You're not a hero. You're a world
> ender, brought back into being like Kali, to renew things, to take up the mantle of the evil bad guys at the [end]
> and become merely the next one in the next cycle like it's always been." (Derek)

**Where the pilgrim comes from:** nobody knows, the pilgrim least of all.
- **Always here:** they were always walking.
- **A dream:** they are a dream that manifested, a story that believed itself into a body.
- **Out of a ring:** they rose out of a waystone, one of the rings with teeth that take a walker apart, blood first and
  bone last, and put them back together elsewhere. But this pilgrim never stepped in.

The Reading that begins the game is the Stranger giving a nameless thing a fate. It is identity given from outside,
the way every face in this world is given.

**What the pilgrim does:**
- every creature the pilgrim kills is a dream unmade;
- every mantle the pilgrim empties waits for its next wearer;
- every lantern keeps a little of the pilgrim each time it carries them back.

The pilgrim takes the world apart one telling at a time, as the Silence took apart the god, and nothing in the world
ever says why.

**The joke at the end:** the pilgrim is not a hero but the world-ender, brought back into being to end the turn and
renew it, the way Kali destroys so that things begin again. At the last, the pilgrim takes up the mantle of the thing
they came to kill, and becomes the next one, the ender the next turn of the world will fear. It has always been so.
Perhaps the Slayers, who "came in where it was open", were the last turn's pilgrims.

The game never states any of it. The player may come to suspect it, and the end may show it.

## The Reading

The game begins with a tarot reading: the Mysterious Stranger lays the cards, and the pilgrim builds itself from them
in nine draws (`ui/reading.gd`, `data/reading.json`):
- a god;
- a face;
- a card, upright or reversed;
- a fear;
- a seeking;
- a road;
- a question;
- a price;
- the reading.

**Derek's direction (2026-10-08):** weave the hero myth into it. Make it more archetypal, and let the Stranger be in on
it, mocking the pilgrim a little as it picks each aspect, card and archetype, because he knows what is happening. The
idea, in Derek's words but never to be quoted in the game: the pilgrim does not draw the cards; the deck draws the
pilgrim.

**Read through the deep lore:**
- The pilgrim thinks it is choosing who to be. It is being fitted for a mantle.
- Every card it takes was worn before, and will be worn again.
- The Stranger has laid this reading for every world-ender of every turn of the world, and he finds it a little funny,
  and a little sad, every time.
- He never says so plainly. He speaks in half-lines, calls each god by a different name every time, and lets the
  pilgrim believe its hand is its own.

**Some lines in his voice,** as examples for whoever writes the Reading (none is final, and none quotes Derek's
phrase):
- "Choose, then. Go on. You always choose this one."
- "Your hand thinks it's choosing. Let it."
- "Shuffle if you like. They remember the order."
- "Reversed. ... You never could hold that one the right way up."
- "There. It fits you. It fit the last one, too."
- "What are you seeking? ... It has been looking for you much longer."
- "Every face on this table has been worn. Some of them by you."
- "The price? You'll pay it. You always pay it."
- "Ah. That one. Don't look so surprised."

## How the deep lore sits under the bible

**Every telling is true a little.** The ages, the creation myths and the ends of the world are true in the only way
anything here is true: they are told, and the telling makes a little of what it tells. Where the tellings disagree,
each is a little true, and none is the thing.

**The hidden truths, read through it.** This is a reading for us, not a new ruling:
- **The Bone grows because it is counted.** Every number the brothers cut makes a little more of what they count.
- **The gilded saints cannot finish.** They are looked at and not seen, so they are neither told nor untold, and a
  thing in between cannot end.
- **The congregation answered the Choir "badly, with names".** The names they gave are why there is anything left of the
  god at all: hands, faces, bone, blood, breath, each one a name that kept a piece of it standing.

## Showing it, never saying it

No text in the game explains the deep lore. It shows in the world:
- faces everywhere, and none of them true;
- hands;
- mirrors and reflections;
- offices and mantles;
- the dead coming back to their places;
- names kept behind the teeth;
- the gap where the Silence's name should be.

**For the in-game writing:** let people believe their masks completely. Never let a text explain. Let two texts rhyme
across the world, so the player can lay them side by side and see.
