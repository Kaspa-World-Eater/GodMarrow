# The Gone Peoples

*Other peoples were here before the Tithed, and they are gone, or nearly. What they believed comes down to us in what
they cut in stone, in what the Citadel's readers have made of their marks, and in what they have said, once or
twice, to people who wrote it down afterward.*

## The Choir

*Heard through the floor of the ninth gallery of the Rib Crypts, under the Long Candle. A sweeper of the Pale Order lay
with his ear to the flagstones every night for a winter and wrote down what he could make out. This is his fair
copy.*

They sing under the floor. It is very faint, and it never stops, and when you have listened long enough you begin to
hear words in it. This is what the words say, as near as I could make them out.

They were the part of the god that told it what it was. When it was alive, they sang behind its ribs, in the long
groove there, and the song was the god knowing itself. They sang in turns, so that the note was always held, so that
there was never a moment when the god did not know what it was. There were eleven hundred and one of them.

Then, once, they all drew breath together. They do not know why. Every one of them breathed in at the same moment, and
for that one moment nobody was singing, and That Which Cannot Be Named came in through the rest. They call it the
first silence. It is their holiest day and their worst one, and they sing about it more than anything else.

After that there was one of them. Or there were eleven hundred and one, and then one. I could not make out which.

They speak of us, once. The congregation that knelt in the god, they sing, answered badly, with names.

Now they are waiting. The god's out-breath is still going out, and when it has finished, there will be a rest, and
they mean to come in on the next line. They do not say what the next line is. I think they are practising it.

## The builders

*Copied by a scholar of the Undercount from carvings in the lowest halls under a bone-house of the Pale Order, and
sent in secret to the Citadel. The Order does not know the copy exists.*

Below the lowest stair the brothers use, there are older halls. Their stair risers come only to the knee, and the
niches cut in their walls are too long for any body I know. The Order does not go down there. I have been down there
eleven times.

The carvings are in a script of straight cuts, and it took me four years to read it. The ones who cut it call
themselves the hinge. The Citadel calls them the Lintel-Folk. They say they were cut from the first of the room, and
began cutting at once. They cut the stairs and the niches. They say, over the niches, *Lie down facing the stair.
Watch.* They say, in many places, *Our joints do not finish.*

There is one thing they will not cut. Wherever it should appear, they cut around it, and leave a space. *Do not say its
name*, they say. *We cut no name for it. We cut around it.* Every ring of carving they made has a gap in it the width
of a thumb.

Near the end of the last hall there is a pillar, and a seat cut at its foot, empty. Over it is the newest carving of
all, cleaner than the rest. It says *Now one of you.*

There is one more carving I must report, because I have seen its words before, on the plinth of a waystone on the
salt road. It says: *WE CUT THEM FACING IN, SO THE MOUTH IS KEPT BY WHAT IT HOLDS.*

## The court of white faces

*Told by the lantern-tenders of the lowlands, who see the Wick-Saints come to their lanterns at dusk. A copyist set
it down.*

In the warm place where the Tithed knelt, before the god died, there was a court of white faces high up in the dark,
and it sat in judgment on souls. The faces were smooth and white as glazed clay, and they watched what everyone
thought, and they found most of it wanting.

When the god died, the court fell. The faces came down out of the dark and lay where they fell, all over the Hide,
under the ash and in the crypts, still white, still watching. Nobody wanted to pick them up.

The moths found them. Moths go to anything pale in the dark. They found the white faces and settled on them and took
them up, and now they wear them. At dusk they come to the lanterns on the roads, swooping in their long arcs, and the
faces on them are the faces of the court.

The tenders say the faces still judge. If one of them hangs in the air and looks at you for a long time before it
comes, it has found you wanting, and you should not be there when it decides what to do about it.

## The people of the one swallow

*From a clay tablet dug out of the mire at the foot of the great stepped tower in the red country. A reader of the
Citadel made out its marks, and wrote his reading beside a drawing of the tablet.*

They were one mind in many bodies, and they lived in the mire. They built the stepped tower and the causeway that
runs to it, and nobody has lived in either since. The Citadel calls them the First Brood, which is the Citadel's name
and not theirs. Theirs, as near as the marks can be read, is the One Swallow.

The tablet tells their beginning. At the beginning, it says, the whole of everything was one swallow, a single great
throat going down, and they were inside it, going down with it. It was dark, and close, and they were all together.
It was the only time they were warm all through.

The tablet does not say what came after. It does not tell their end. It ends with the swallow, and a line of marks
the reader could not make out, repeated many times to the edge of the clay.

## The climbers

*A rubbing taken from a stone at the foot of the stair nobody climbs, in the Heights. The stone is cut in a script
nobody now reads; this is the reading the Citadel accepts, though it argues about it.*

The sky is a mouth, open. We are inside it. Everything is inside it.

A people that knows it is inside a mouth does not stay. We have built the stair, and we will climb out by it, all of
us, and carry the children, and leave nothing behind for the mouth to close on.

That is all the stone says. The stair goes up into the cloud, and no one living has seen the top of it. The people who
lived at its foot were climbed, all of them, and their houses were left with the doors open. None of them came down.

## The tall ones at the door

*What one of the Marrow-Eaters said of its own people, through a closed door, to a reader of the Citadel who did not
open it. He wrote it down that night and left the Citadel the next morning.*

It knocked at my door after midnight, three times, softly, and when I asked who was there it answered very politely.
It said it would not keep me long. It asked whether there was marrow in the house. I said there was not, and it
thanked me, and then it did not go away. It stood outside the door, and after a while it began to talk, as if it were
lonely.

It said its people were the wanting of something that wanted nothing. Once, it said, long ago, they had stood a long
while in a warm room where nothing wanted anything at all, and they had been at rest there. It said that was the
only time they had ever been at rest, and that they had been looking for the room ever since.

It said they were tall and hollow, and that it would show me if I opened the door. I did not open the door. In the
morning there were marks on the outside of it, very high up, as if someone tall had leaned their forehead against
it for a long time.

## The Slayers

*From the walls of the temple deep inside the body, as the lamp-bearers of the Last Vigil copied them, a line at a
time, between watches. The lamp-bearers say the walls are warm, and that there is a thought still thinking in the
temple, and that it is better not to copy too much at once.*

Something was opened, and we came in where it was open. There was never a beginning. There was only a way in.

We leave no bodies. We have never had them to leave.

This place is a thought, and it is forgetting what is in it, one thing at a time. It forgets a name, and then the
thing the name was for. It forgets a face, and then the one who wore it. When it has forgotten everything that is in
it, it will be a thought that remembers no one. That is rest. We are helping.

*Under the copy, a lamp-bearer wrote:* The readers ask whether the Slayers mean the Silence. I cannot say. Their walls
never once mention it. They may mean the Silence, or its opposite, or something none of us has a word for.
