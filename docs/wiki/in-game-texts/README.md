# In-game texts

*Texts for the player to find. Derek, 2026-10-08: "The in-game writing is more for finding shit in the game for the
players." The bible behind them, for us, is `../mythology/`.*

These are pieces of writing that a pilgrim can come across in the world:
- letters and day-book leaves;
- words cut into stairs, doors and tombs;
- sermons someone wrote down;
- notices, ledgers, warnings;
- stories a people passes down.

Each is in the world's own voice. Its writer believes they live in the world, knows only what they know, and is often
wrong. Each text has a title and one line in italics saying what it is and where it was found.

**Each zone's own texts are in its page** (`../mythology/areas/`, under "Found in the world"). This folder holds the
ones that belong to a people or a faith rather than to one place:

## Rules for in-game writing

- **The world's own voices only.** Never a knowing narrator. People believe their masks completely.
- **Readable.** Plain running prose, with little jargon and few names.
- **Never explain the deep lore** (`../mythology/00-the-deep-lore.md`). Let two texts rhyme across the world, so the
  player can lay them side by side and connect them.
- **No little towns or villages** that are not in the game (2026-10-08). Name peoples, not their towns.
- **The Silence is never "the Hush".** People call it That Which Cannot Be Named, That Which Cannot Be Held, the
  Unmaking, or say nothing where its name would go.
- **The word laws** of `../01-rules-and-decisions.md`.
- **No quests and no story hooks.** These texts build the world.

The game's own Codex (`data/codex.json`) is older than these rules. Its texts are being brought into line with them.
