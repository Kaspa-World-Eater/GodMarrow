## The Reliquary, and the rule

The world is a corpse too large to be known. Whose it was, how long it has been dead and whether it was ever a god, no one can say, and every faith says something different.

- **What every faith agrees on:** the ground is warm, the dead do not stay down, and things still come up out of the body.
- **What the faiths disagree on:** everything else. The Pale Order calls it the Bearing Mother. The Gilded Peak says it is a mouth. Some say it was the greatest of all gods, some that it was a beast, some that it is not dead at all. Under the Hide, things that live there do not call it anything.
- **The skin is the Hide;** the ridges are its bones; what runs in the rivers is what runs in it still.

**The rule the world runs on.** A god is a belief that grew a body. Fear and worship are what it lives on, and the more who fear it or pray to it, the more real it is and the wider its reach. A god nobody remembers starves. So the old powers ruled by terror: a city that trembles is a city that feeds.

- **Dreams:** sleepers dream, and powers graze there. A nightmare is something feeding.
- **Marrow:** belief does not pass through a god; it settles. In the bones of anything long feared or prayed to, it hardens into marrow. Godmarrow is that stuff, and every faith of this age learns to spend it.
- **Not everything is a god.** Behemoths, lords, the dead, demons and things from outside live by other rules or none. Some of them eat gods.

## The Silence

Ur-Nihl is the space between words, the silence between sounds. Every meaning in the world is held in it, and it is written everywhere for those who can see and hear it.

- **What it holds:** a word means something only because of the quiet on either side of it; a note is music only because of the rest after it. Take the Silence away and every sound runs into one noise.
- **Where it is written:** in the gap between two letters, the pause in a chant, the blank margin of a page, the still air between two breaths, the empty place in a ring of standing stones. A reader who has learned to see it finds it more legible than the words.
- **Why it is feared:** gods are made of being spoken of. The Silence is the one power that is not spoken, and cannot be, and every god is held inside it the way a word is held between two pauses. The old faiths that call it a killer misread it; the faiths that understand it are rarer and stranger.
- **Its names:** the Hush in the lowlands. Its own name, Ur-Nihl, is written as a gap by those who know it best, because the gap is where it is.
- **How it reaches people:** some hear it. A person who has heard it once stops hearing words the same way; they hear the spaces, and what is held there.

**The older lore of the void, kept.** Every reading below stays in the world as what some faith or scholar believes; none is confirmed, and all of them are true to the one who holds it.

- **The Edgeless.** What there was before anything had a name; the Silence is what it is called once there is someone to call it. Taboo to name in most mouths.
- **The Unsaying.** The Pale Order's articles and the old Codex say the Silence took the god's names one at a time, until it forgot how to be three in one and died. Read with your ruling, it is a god that fell into the spaces between its own names.
- **The two empty hands.** The Gilded Peak's teaching: Radiance, the open hand, erases with light, noon with no shadow, a face looked at until nothing is seen; Absence, the closed hand, erases with dark. Neither keeps anything.
- **The Void That Emanates.** The Reading's name for it, the Mouth With No Lips; it hears its name as you hear yours across a crowded room.
- **Its servants leave nothing.** Where they pass there is no corpse, no bone, no blood, no breath, no thread; a little glass, a little dust. Every other faith fears this above anything, and has once been glad of it.
- **The wounds and the Scar.** Where the corpse lies open, the Silence is nearest. The Scar is the place it is said to have come in, and the ground there will not close.
- **The Slayers' reading.** The temple deep in the body holds that the end is a thought that remembers no one, which may be the Silence by another name, or its opposite.

## The ages

Four ages, the last of them ours. You rejected the first names offered; pick one set below, or mix them.

|  | Set A: by what ruled | Set B: by what was heard | Set C: in the old tongue |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Before anything is remembered | The Uncounted | The Unspoken | Ur-Vhaal |
| The heights | The Age of Terrors | The Age of the Loud Gods | Vhaal-Khoroth |
| The fall | The Starving | The Long Hush | Nhaal-Uthrim |
| Now | The Age of the Last Breath | The Age of the Last Breath | The Age of the Last Breath |

**Before anything is remembered.** Whatever came before the corpse lay down. No faith agrees. Nothing alive remembers it.

**The Held Space, kept.** Some voices claim to remember the inside of the corpse while it lived: a space held open in the Silence, warm, lit from nowhere, where a choir sang its name to it, builders cut stairs in its bone, a court of white faces judged its thoughts, and near the end a congregation knelt. The faithful of this age descend, they say, from that congregation. Whether the Held Space was the corpse's life, a dream it had, or a story told by things that need one, no voice agrees.

**The heights.** The gods walked and were loud with worship. Dragons were among them, one power of many, perhaps the greatest. Colossi fed on whole countries. Towers drank, citadels marched, and mortal lords ruled through dread until the dread outlived them. Cities were raised that no one now could raise. Everything that is a ruin today was alive then, and most of what is known of it is wrong.

**The fall.** The gods starved, one by one, as their peoples died, stopped believing or were eaten. A god that starves to the end breaks into demons, as a carcass breaks into flies. The cities emptied. What was known was lost with the hands that knew it. Some powers did not die: they slept, went under, or kept walking because no one told them to stop.

**The Age of the Last Breath.** Ours. The corpse's last breath is still leaving it, and people live in that breath. Knowledge is the rarest thing in the world: the tablets lie in ruins no one can read whole, and those who try do not finish. What is left of the heights is roads that were avenues, a stair no one climbs, and a citadel still walking somewhere far off. The faiths of this age keep the dark back with a soul in a jar for a lamp.

**The count.** The common count, the Pale Order's, runs from the last breath: the gatherer of the Codex writes in the eleven hundred and fourteenth year. Other faiths count differently and say so; the far peoples do not count from the corpse at all.

## The dead, and the crafts of raising them

A corpse carries the belief it died with, and a power's reach raises its dead to serve it. The risen are not a plague: they are the land remembering what it was afraid of.

- **The Risen.** The ordinary dead of a place, up and walking because something is still feared there. Take the fear away and they lie down.
- **The Tithed.** Those who have given all they can: to a power, to a faith, to the toil of keeping the world going, until nothing of them is left to give. Emptied, they do not lie down. They wait in rows, and rise when three gather.
- **Wraiths.** Dreams that lost their sleeper and go on being dreamed with no one to dream them. They want a mind to finish in.
- **Liches.** Mortals who learned to live on fear directly and made themselves small gods that cannot die. Every lich is a faith of one. Forget it, and it is a skeleton.
- **Revenants.** The dead who come back for one thing, and lie down when it is done. The most honest things in the world.
- **The crafts.** Every faith that touches the dead does it differently. The Pale Order anoints bone with marrow and the bone stands. The Bleeding Maiden's people give blood and the blood gets up. The Veiled Crone's circle holds souls in glass. The Myriad's purifiers take a wrong breath into themselves. The Gilded Peak lets the dead finish. Each faith calls its own craft holy and the others' theft.

## The faiths this age

The game follows five faiths among many. Each keeps one power fed, each has an inner circle that knows a name the rest must not, and each has a calling a player may take. Who that player is, the player decides.

| Faith | Its word | What it keeps | The power | The calling |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| The Pale Order | order | Bone, the dead's frames, the Count | Oss-Vharoth, the Bearing Mother | Ossuarch |
| The Gilded Peak | monastery | Nothing, on purpose | Ur-Nihl, the Silence | Empty Hand |
| The Veiled Crone | circle | Souls, held in glass | Yh'Anuul, the Crone, the Soul | Hollow Mystic |
| The Bleeding Maiden | procession | Blood, given with the Words | Nol-Shogthuth, the Flesh | Hemomancer |
| The Myriad | shrine | Breath, and the small gods that ride it | the Myriad itself; it has no true name | Shrine Keeper |

**The Pale Order.** As old as the world, it claims, and keeper of the ossuaries cut into the white ridges, in the shape of a cavern it will not name. It anoints bone with marrow, stands its dead in niches facing the stair, counts every bone, and believes a number outlasts a name. Its halls stand on older halls it did not cut, and it hides this. An **Ossuarch** is a warden of one ossuary, sent out with the standing dead to bring bone home.

**The Gilded Peak.** A monastery on a mountain of bone, built to hear the Silence. Its monks chanted one syllable and held the pause after it as the prayer; they gilded what they honoured until it could no longer be seen, and begged names in the lowlands and gave them away. The Pale Order's dead climbed the stair one night and the courtyard became glass. The monks who were below that night still walk the roads with their bowls. An **Empty Hand** is one of them: a monk who has heard the space between words and carries nothing.

**The Veiled Crone.** Circles that sit in rings saying her names until saying and self are one thread. They read the threads of souls in polished glass and taught that every life is drawn back through the eye of her loom and woven again. In one winter a whole valley's dead came home asking which way, and a circle's eldest looked into the eye and said there is no loom. Since then the faith is split between those who still send the dead on and those who keep them. A **Hollow Mystic** is a reader of glass who keeps souls rather than sending them.

**The Bleeding Maiden.** Hooded processions that give blood in measure, with the Words said over it, so that the Flesh knows whom it is for. Villa Llaga stood on the lips of a wound that never closed, until a year of blood given without enough words hatched as her mindless children and the town was gone. Hearts are hers. A **Hemomancer** is one of her blood-adepts: one who opens their own veins and lets the Mother come through, and leads what she makes.

**The Myriad.** Common folk call them Shrine Keepers. They keep the shrines of the small gods of every stone, ford and bell, and their purifiers breathe wrongness out of the world, hold it in their own bodies and breathe it back at whoever made it. Every purifier who holds too much walks down into the water in the end, horned, carrying what they took. A **Shrine Keeper** is a purifier of the Myriad, running out of room.

## Heresies and other faiths

The five are not alone, and not whole.

**The Second Mouth** (the only cult). A heresy of theophagy hidden inside the Bleeding Maiden's processions. Where the faithful give to the Mother, its initiates take her in: her flesh where it lies close under the Hide, and the bodies she has touched. A second mouth is cut under the breastbone and sewn shut after every rite, and the liturgy is the faithful's own Words said backward, so she cannot tell who is taking her. Grades: Vessel, Shrine, Reliquary. Its founder, **the crone**, is the first Reliquary: an ancient hag who no longer ages, folded in a chair she has not left in a lifetime, covered in mouths, a few of which whisper in the Maiden's voice. Some initiates believe she died long ago and what sits there is the Mother wearing her. The full write-up is in the doc *The Second Mouth*.

**Other faiths of this age**, unconnected to the five:

- **The Herald-cults.** Each power's herald draws its own worshippers. The Marrow Pontiff (cult name Oss'khalath) is an office of the Pale Order that may be one bone never replaced; the Weeping Gash (proposed; cult name Shog'maathe) comes to the bleeding and offers to take it, kindly, and keeps what it nurses; the Long Exhale (Hau'Nuulh) carries the Myriad; a Silent One has no name, only a gap.
- **The Last Vigil.** Lamp-bearers who went down into the body and keep a watch at its gate against whatever was left thinking in the deep.
- **The Count-breakers.** Lowland apostates who believe the Pale Order's numbers are what keeps the dead walking, and unmake tallies at night.
- **Faiths of the Dreth'ael towns.** A town owned by a blood-lord worships it without saying so: the lord must be known to live, and the town keeps it known.
- **Faiths no one on the Hide has met.** The sea people who pray to a drowned god's memory; the mountain that is one mind and its pilgrims; worship in far places that does not count from the corpse at all.

## Creation myths

No two agree, and none is confirmed. Each is what its keepers believe; writers take one and never the others.

- **The Pale Order: the Falling.** Before the Count there was no count, and the Order forbids saying more. Then the Bearing Mother fell, and her frame came to rest as the world, and the first brothers stood beside her and laid her bones where they lay. Everything began at that falling; the Order counts from it. The world was not made. It was set down, and is waiting to be set down again.
- **The Gilded Peak: the Mouth.** In the Silence a mouth opened, and the opening was the god. It drew one breath in and held it, and everything that has been was inside that one held breath. It was small. The Silence it opened in did not notice it. The Peak teaches that creation is a pause held too long.
- **The Veiled Crone: the First Thread.** Before her, everything was like the east wall at Greywell: dry, unmoving, held by nothing. She drew the first thread out of nothing and tied the first knot, and the knot was the world, and every life is a length of that one thread. Some circles add, quietly, that someone tied a knot before she began.
- **The Bleeding Maiden: the Wound.** The world began as a wound that opened and did not close. What came out of it was flesh, and the flesh grew because nothing told it to stop. The Maiden is that first opening; the Mother is the growing; the faithful give blood back into the wound so it knows what it made.
- **The Myriad: the Breath.** The great one breathed in once, long, and every small god was inside that breath, warm, one sound. Then it began to breathe out, and the world is the out-breath, and every stone, ford and bell is a small god riding it on the way out.
- **The Second Mouth: the Door.** The world is not a body but the outside of one. The Mother is inside, and every wound in the world is a door she is trying to come out of. Creation is not finished: it ends when she has walked out wearing her people.
- **The Tithed of the lowlands: the Kneeling.** Grandmothers say the people knelt inside something warm and sang to it, and it died around them, and they took each other's belts in the dark and walked out of its mouth onto the ash. They do not say what it was. They keep three notes of the song and never sing the fourth.
- **The Lintel-Folk** (from their cut stones): they were not made; they stood up out of the bone and began cutting at once, and the first thing they cut was the room.
- **The First Brood** (scratched on its causeway): the whole of the beginning was one swallow, and they were inside it going down, and it was the only time they were warm all through.
- **The Marrow-Eaters** (in a grace cut round a slab): they were the hunger of something that wanted nothing, and they stood a long while in a warm room where nothing wanted, and were at rest.
- **The Slayers** (in grooves no one can read whole): something was opened, and they came in where it was open, and there was never a beginning, only a way in.
- **A salt-boiler, asked what came before:** he put both palms over the asker's mouth and held them there, and nodded.

## Eschaton myths

Every faith has an end, and each condemns the others'.

- **The Pale Order: the Setting Down.** When the Great Count closes at the Last Number, the Mother sets the world down at last. Everything finished stays with her: laid bone, closed counts, dust. Everything unfinished is given to the wind: flesh still growing, breath still leaving, every name. The sky, which is the Order's dust, comes down and lies over her like a cloth over a face. The anathemas said at every Feast condemn every other end by name.
- **The Gilded Peak: the Pause Completed.** The held breath is let go, and what remains is the Silence the breath was held in, which held the meaning all along. Nothing is destroyed; the sentence is finished, and a finished sentence has been said. The monks who were below when the Peak fell keep the last office: to be the empty space outside when it ends, holding nothing, so nothing is kept wrongly.
- **The Veiled Crone: the Still Winter.** One winter every thread that ever went through the eye comes back up its valley at once and stands at its door asking which way. The circles that keep glass uncovered say the dead will stop at their own faces and be home. The circles that send the dead on say the weave will finish its cloth and fold it. Either way, nothing moves again; the candle stops burning down.
- **The Bleeding Maiden: the Filling.** The Flesh fills every hollow: every wound, mouth, grave, room, and the gap between one word and the next. When nothing is hollow there is no then. The faithful say this is the Mother made whole, and say it carefully, because it means the end of the Silence too.
- **The Myriad: the Last Breath Out.** The out-breath finishes. Every small god, having given its name to the wind, settles like dust. A purifier who has already walked down into the water comes back up and breathes the world in, once, as the last rite; the world is folded like a paper figure with its arms open and set on a step, and nothing comes to breathe on it.
- **The Second Mouth: the Walking.** When enough Reliquaries carry her, the Mother walks out of the world in them, many bodies, one will. The faithful call this the end. The cult calls it the beginning finishing.
- **The Slayers: the Thought.** The world is a thought with no one thinking it, forgetting what is in it one thing at a time. When it has forgotten everything it is a thought that remembers no one, and that is rest.
- **The lowland Tithed** have no end of their own. They say the lanterns go out one at a time, and somebody always sits up.
- **The one who sits at every fire** gives a different end each time he is asked, and has not picked one.

## The structure of the orders

How each of the five is built: where it sits, who holds what office, what it does, what it hides, and what its calling can do. This is the source for classes, skills and enemies.

### The Pale Order

- **Seat:** nine great ossuaries and a hundred lesser, cut down into the white ridges in stairs and niches; Nine Stairs is first among them. The Ninth Stair of each holds its running Count.
- **Ranks:** novice (a cord of two hundred and six knots, a dead teacher carried on a yoke) · brother (a number in place of a name) · Warden of an ossuary · the Wardens in Agreement · the Marrow Pontiff, an office kneeling at the bottom of the Fifth Well that may be one bone never replaced.
- **Offices:** talliers (keep open counts in their heads) · sweepers (carry the dust to the sky) · Brothers of the Well (draw marrow, render chrism) · star-watchers (count the Unfallen from the night-roof) · Seekers (follow fallen stars) · knights of the Kostyak (ride the low road with the standing dead, in plate cut with the names of those who wore it before).
- **Rites:** the Laying (washing, chrism joint by joint, niche, name, number) · the Feast of the Laying (every wall counted; the count always rises, called the Mother's gift) · the three funerals (flesh to the wind, bone to the niche, dust to the sky).
- **Hidden:** the halls beneath were cut by others; the dead watch for something; the Count does not close; the gift has a column no one may add down.
- **The Ossuarch, a calling:** a warden sent beyond the line of white stones to bring bone home. Crafts: raising and commanding the standing dead; anointing; the Count used as a weapon (numbers that close or open a body); bone made armour; the Unfallen asked down from the sky.

**Factions within the Order**

- **The Ninefold.** Militant closers. They hold that the Count must be closed, and that waiting for the dead to come in is a failure of devotion. They count the living, close frames before their time, and bring bone home from bodies that were not finished with it. The lowlands fear their knights more than any risen thing. The Wardens disown them in daylight and use them at night.
- **The Undercount.** Scholars who go down past the Order's own halls into the older ones, reading the notches cut by the builders. They believe the Count was never the Order's, that it is still being kept by someone below, and that the Order has only been copying it. They take no frames and carry no dead; they carry chalk and come back with numbers they will not say.

### The Gilded Peak

- **Seat:** a monastery cut into a mountain of bone, now fallen; its courtyard is black glass. The monks who were below that night walk the roads.
- **Ranks:** novice (shaved on the stair, a grey smock) · monk (a bowl, a robe, in time a lantern holding a teacher) · master gilder · the abbot. A teacher walks one step behind and below a novice on the stair.
- **Offices:** the chanters (one syllable, then the pause, which is the prayer) · the gilders (gold laid until a thing cannot be seen) · the bowl-bearers (go down in fours and ask for names) · lay goldbeaters and bell-casters who eat and keep the work coming.
- **Rites:** the long chant · the Noon of No Shadow · the giving of names to the open hand at noon · the saints gilded while still breathing.
- **Hidden:** where the names go; why the bowls grow heavier; what the saints under the gold are still looking at.
- **The Empty Hand, a calling:** a monk who has heard the space between words. Crafts: Radiance (erasing with light) and Absence (erasing with dark); taking names; the pause made a weapon; striking where meaning is held; carrying nothing, so having nothing to lose.

**Factions within the monastery**

- **The Bowl.** The monks who still go down to the lowlands in fours and ask for names. They believe a name given into the bowl is not destroyed but laid in the pause where all meaning is held, and kept there safer than in any mouth. They are gentle, and towns empty of names behind them.
- **The Gilt.** Ascetics of Radiance who gild themselves living, a leaf a day, until no one can see them. They believe to be looked at and not seen is the last holiness, and they walk among people unnoticed in full noon. Where one has stood, people cannot afterward say who was there.

### The Veiled Crone

- **Seat:** circles in lodges where her threads come up through the skin of the world, along a valley of cold lakes and far beyond; many stand empty since the loose winter.
- **Ranks:** novice · reader of threads · circle-keeper (begins and ends the names) · the eldest of a circle, who wears the coat of glass sewn by every eldest before.
- **Offices:** readers (polish a glass until a soul's thread shows) · grinders (lay, make the hollowed glass) · menders of the coat's lining · coverers (cover every glass in a house of death).
- **Rites:** the circle of names, dusk to grey · the covering of glass at a death · the Folding of the year's cloth · the casting-on of a child's thread.
- **Hidden, and split:** the loose winter showed there may be no loom; half the faith still covers the glass and sends the dead on, half uncovers it and keeps them.
- **The Hollow Mystic, a calling:** a reader who keeps souls rather than sending them. Crafts: needles on soul-thread; glass that catches a soul at its own face; threads pulled taut to bind and move; kept dead circling as wisps; an iron coat of glass that stands up full of the kept and does not die for good.

**Factions within the circles**

- **The Covered.** The orthodox circles, which still cover every glass in a house of death and send the dead on through the eye of the loom. Since the loose winter they do it faster, and more fiercely, and burn uncovered glass where they find it. They say the loom is there, and that saying so is the work.
- **The Unveiled.** The circles that took the felt off. They keep the dead at their own faces in glass, and carry them, and believe there is no loom and nowhere to send them. Villages turn their mirrors to the wall when they pass; widows pay them for a little more time.

### The Bleeding Maiden

- **Seat:** processions and chapters along open wounds in the world; Villa Llaga was the greatest and is empty; lesser chapters such as Sallow Lip keep the Rule.
- **Ranks:** hood (known by place in the procession: the twenty-ninth hood) · the Elder at the front · the bloodletter, who opens others and by old rule never opens themself.
- **Offices:** cup-beaters (the tithe-cup, its line raised each year) · carvers (images the saints come down into; the Maiden's face left unfinished) · the keeper of the chained Rule · the counted-down (members ruled dead by a disputed count, who keep quiet offices) · allied leech-wives of the mire.
- **Rites:** the Day of the Measure (blood given in measure, the Words said over it) · the procession (the sway, the stop, the sway) · the night the saints come down the crypt stair · the Raising of a new image.
- **Hidden:** the fourth clause of the Rule, kept in the Elder's mouth; why the wound asks more each year; the Second Mouth among them.
- **The Hemomancer, a calling:** a blood-adept who opens their own veins and lets the Mother come through. Crafts: blood that is flung and falls and pools; life spent to power everything; the procession of small hooded things raised from blood; the sewn giant; penances worn as armour and weapons; healing in blood.

**Factions within the processions**

- **The Measured.** The orthodox chapters: blood in measure, to the line on the cup, with the Words, once a year. They hold that a gift must be whole to be heard and that too much is spilled, not given. They keep the Rule and raise the line a thumbnail each year, and do not ask why she asks for more.
- **The Open Vein.** Zealots who say the measure starves her and the Words are a fence. They give everything, scourging in the streets, and their processions go out and do not come home. Where they pass the ground stays wet. The Measured call them a flood; the Second Mouth recruits from among them.

### The Myriad

- **Seat:** wayside shrines everywhere, and the Well-Shrine at the one clean pool of a drowned fen; its gate faces a stone that turns to the wall when the shrine is forgotten. Common folk call its people Shrine Keepers.
- **Ranks:** novice folder · purifier (renamed at her folding; her birth-name burned in a paper figure) · keeper of the day-book · the elders.
- **Offices:** purifiers (Fan-Bearers) · folders of the paper figures · keepers of the guardian stones · allied boatmen of the fen.
- **Rites:** the folding of a paper figure · the Cutting-Away (once a year's wrong breath given to the water, until the water gave it back) · the turning of the stones · the Small Cutting since.
- **Hidden:** where the breath goes; why the knotted cord at every purifier's wrist grows longer than the knots she tied; that every purifier ends horned, walking down into the water with her hands full.
- **The Shrine Keeper, a calling:** a purifier running out of room. Crafts: breathing wrongness out of the world and holding it; the fan that tells breath where to go; paper figures; turning what was done back on whoever did it; a reversed presence in the haze beside her.

**Factions within the Myriad**

- **The Folded.** The orthodox purifiers: they take a wrong breath in and turn it back, by the rite, on whoever made it, and keep count of their held breaths on the knotted cord. They serve as long as they have room, and fear the day they do not.
- **The Horned.** Purifiers who have begun to turn and have stopped resisting it. They believe the end every purifier comes to is the true office: to carry the world's wrong breath down into the water and keep it there. They gather at the drowned places, horned and patient, and the Folded will not speak their names.

## Names, forms and words

- **The register.** Old names follow the game's true names (Yh'Anuul, Oss-Vharoth, Nol-Shogthuth, Ur-Nihl): guttural, apostrophed, doubled vowels, endings in -oth, -uul, -aath, -ith, -qhan. Names that cost something to say. Taboo names are written as a gap.
- **Common names** are what frightened people call a thing in the lowlands; documents may know the old name.
- **Forms.** Every document in its own form and hand: doctrine, chronicle, ledger, inscription with gaps and gloss, trial record, letter, field notes, catechism, inventory, confession, treaty, a thing's own account. The gatherer's one-line note is the only frame.
- **Never:** verse or song; village daily life (customs, recipes, feasts, games); conversation; game progression; class characters as people.
- **Words never used:** rot; cell, virus, DNA, organism, biology; cooldown, dps, proc, aggro, loot, buff, nerf, stun, lightning, mana. Never "Weeping Maiden". No "house" or "brotherhood" for a faith. Miasma is breath gone wrong.
- **The feel.** Things older than any god, indifferent. Knowledge costs. Counts do not close; halls are deeper inside than outside. The witness is changed by witnessing. No explanation is ever confirmed.
