# The Codex Expansion: Bible

Proposed canon for the new chapters of the Codex of the Hide. Nothing here is adopted until Derek marks it in
`docs/wiki/01-rules-and-decisions.md`. Everything in `docs/wiki/02-world-and-lore.md` and the Words section of
`01-rules-and-decisions.md` outranks this file. The existing ten chapters (the gatherer's book, `base_codex.json`)
stay as they are; the new chapters sit beside them as other documents in the same collection.

## The shape of the collection

The gatherer's prologue says he gathered pages. The expansion is the rest of what he gathered: documents by other
hands, from other ages, in other forms. The reader is meant to notice that they disagree. No document knows the whole
truth. The gatherer never comments on them except in a one-line "by" note saying where the page came from.

## Ages (the Pale Order's count; other peoples count differently and say so)

1. **The Edgeless.** Before the god. Nothing had a name. The Silence (Ur-Nihl, the Hush) is what the Edgeless is called
   once there is someone to call it something. Taboo to name in most mouths.
2. **The Held Space.** The god of three natures (Soul, Bone, Flesh) held a space open inside the Silence. Things lived
   inside it: its thoughts, its small spirits, and near the end the congregation who would become the Tithed. Length
   unknown; the Order says "a day of the god's", the Peak says "nine thousand stairs", the Brood says "one swallow".
3. **The Unsaying.** The Silence took the god's names one at a time. The god forgot how to be three in one. This is
   the age the Slayers belong to: things from outside that came in through the wounds the unsaying opened.
4. **The Last Breath.** The god died. Its corpse is the world, the Reliquary. The Pale Order counts years from here:
   "the Breath" is year 1. The congregation was inside at worship and came out onto the Cheek: the Tithed.
5. **The Cooling.** The first ages on the corpse. The flesh still warm, the breath still moving, the Myriad loud. The
   first peoples who were not Tithed rose from the god's pieces and built. Most are gone. (Breath years 1 to about 900.)
6. **The Age of Stairs.** The Pale Order is founded on ruins older than itself in the Ossa (about Breath 900); the Peak
   is climbed (about Breath 1400); the lodge of the Polished Heart, the town of the Precious Wound and the House of Eight
   Million take their shapes. The Wickbound lanterns are first lit (about Breath 1100).
7. **The Feuds.** The five houses turn on one another. Long, dull, bloody. (About Breath 2000 to 2900.)
8. **The Present.** The Order's count stands at **Breath 3,311** when the gatherer writes. The Hide is the known world.
   The ground is still cooling. Things are still coming up.

## Peoples who are gone (the "dead races"; all pieces of the god that organised, except the Slayers)

- **The Lintel-Folk.** The first builders. Pieces of the god's Bone that stood up and built with bone before any
  Tithed could. They raised the halls under the Ossa that the Pale Order later walled off and called its own. They
  left lintels, foundation stones, a counting system in notches, and no bodies: their bones are the buildings. The
  Order's founding is partly a theft and the Order hides it.
- **The First Brood.** The Flesh's first children, in what is now Shog-Mire: a brood with one mind and many bodies,
  which built the Ziggurat and the Causeway. They died when the one mind died; the bodies are still there. The
  Brood-Mother of Act III is a later, lesser echo.
- **The Sky-Climbers.** A Tithed people of An-Vhar who believed the sky was the god's open mouth and climbed to leave
  by it. They built the Stair and the monastery, rang the bells, and went up. None came down. The Chime-Abbot of Act IV
  is what the last of them became.
- **The Wick-Binders.** The Tithed guild that first learned to keep a soul in a lantern (Breath about 1100). They were
  hunted out by the Pale Order for keeping what should go down; their craft survived them in every lantern on the road.
- **The Marrow-Eaters.** A people made of the god's hunger: tall, hollow, courteous. They ate marrow and spoke well.
  They are gone from the Hide; the Root Deep may hold them still.
- **The Slayers.** Not of the god. They came through the wounds in the Unsaying. They leave no corpse. They built the
  Alien Temple in the Descent. The Thought of the Slayer is one of them, or what one of them left thinking. Their
  writing is in a form no Tithed can read whole; scholars have fragments.
- **The Porcelain Court.** The saints whose faces the Moth-Saints wear: a court of the god's Soul that sat in judgment
  inside the Held Space. Their faces survived; the rest did not.

## Names (Derek, 2026-10-04: the half-god names must be Lovecraftian, and the feel of most of the book with them)

The game's true names set the register: Yh'Anuul (Soul), Oss-Vharoth (Bone), Nol-Shogthuth (Flesh), Ur-Nihl (the
Silence). Names in the documents follow that register: guttural, apostrophed, doubled vowels, endings in -oth, -uul,
-aath, -ith, -qhan; names that cost something to say; names a people has for itself that the Tithed mispronounce; names
that are taboo and written as a gap. The plain descriptions (the Tallyman, the Gaunt Pilgrim) are what frightened
Tithed call them in the road-houses; the documents know the names.

## Half-gods, heralds and the ones from outside

Heralds (canon descriptions, kept): the Marrow Pontiff (Bone), the Wet Nurse (Flesh), the Long Exhale (Breath), a
Silent One (the Silence). Altars name them Old Upright, the Red Mother, the Last Breath, the Hush, never the true name.
The documents may know the cult names, which are never on an altar:

- **Marrow Pontiff / Old Upright:** cult name **Oss'khalath**, "the Bone That Stood", the office of the Bone made
  flesh of a sort; the heresy that it is one skeleton never replaced.
- **Wet Nurse / Red Mother:** cult name **Shog'maathe**, the first-born mouth of the Flesh; it feeds and is fed.
- **Long Exhale / Last Breath:** cult name **Hau'Nuulh**, the breath that has not finished leaving; the Myriad ride it.
- **A Silent One / the Hush:** no name. The Empty Hand write a gap where it should be, and the gap is the name:
  **[ ]**. Some write "the Finger of Ur-Nihl" and are corrected.
- **The Bleeding Maiden:** the Hemomancers' secret name **Sanguth'Ael**, "the Wound That Loves".
- **The Bearing Mother** is the Bone, Oss-Vharoth, and that name is said only on the stair.

The Visitors, who owe nothing to the god and nothing to the Silence; older than both or outside both; indifferent:

- **Ykkoth-Taal**, called the Tallyman. Counts what is not his. Appears where a count is wrong. Takes the difference.
  The count is of everything; the Hide is a column in it.
- **Nhar-Ghulim**, called the Ferryman of the Dry Ford. Carried travellers across a river that then left. Still takes
  fares. Across what, no one who paid has said.
- **Ath-Sorrauun**, called the Gaunt Pilgrim, "He Who Walks Upward". Walks the roads from the Last Vigil up to the
  Cheek. Walking since before the Breath. Where he passes, the dead lie back down, and so do some of the living.
- **Vel-Qorrath**, called the Lectern. A reading-stand found in ruins with a book open on it that is the reader's own
  life, a page ahead. The Reader, who reads from it aloud, has no name because it is a mouth and not a thing.
- **Ghom-Ulvaar**, the Hollow King (canon, parked): king of the dead herd on the Burnt Heath.

Self-names of the dead peoples (what they called themselves; the Tithed names are in the next section):
the Lintel-Folk: **the Khurr-Ossat**; the First Brood: **Yl-Nothuun**, "the One Swallow"; the Sky-Climbers:
**the An-Vhar'ith**; the Marrow-Eaters: **the Ghauul**; the Slayers, named by Tithed scholars only:
**the Vhoor-Qhan**, "Those From the Wound"; the Porcelain Court: **the Ael-Yh'ani**. The Wick-Binders were a Tithed
guild and had a plain name.

## The feel (Derek: Lovecraftian, for most of the book)

- The god was small. The Edgeless is not a void but a vastness that did not notice the god and does not notice the
  Hide. The Unsaying was not malice; it was weather.
- Things older than the god, outside the god, indifferent to the god. The Visitors do not want anything from the
  Tithed; that is what is unbearable about them.
- Knowledge costs. Scholars who read the tablets whole do not finish their glosses. Documents end mid-sentence with a
  later hand noting the author's fate. Names are written as gaps. A text may say plainly that reading it was a mistake.
- Geometry and number go wrong near the old things: counts that do not close, halls deeper inside than outside,
  stairs with a step that is not there on the way down.
- Cults, not congregations: every house has an inner order that knows the true name and a doctrine that exists to keep
  the laity from learning it.
- The witness is changed by witnessing. Reports grow stranger as they go; the last entry is not in the first hand.
- No cosmic explanation is ever confirmed. Two documents offer opposite ones and both are frightening.
- Still the game's voice: elegiac, flesh and bone and breath, never science words, never the banned words.

## Forgotten heroes (ancient; nobody alive remembers them rightly)

- **Sister Un** of the empty reliquary in the Rib Crypts (canon landmark). Proposed: the first Tithed to go down
  into the body and come back; her reliquary is empty because she asked to be forgotten and was obeyed.
- **Haddo Nine-Fingers.** A Lintel-era Tithed who tithed his own finger to the Bearing Mother and was given a hall.
- **Ketter of the Dry Ford.** Argued the river god back for one night so the camp could drink.
- **Mael the Wick-Binder.** First to bind a soul to a lantern: his own daughter's, at her asking.
- **The Abbess Who Rang Once.** Rang the Unrung Bell a single time, at the top of the Stair, and what answered is why
  it is unrung.
- **Orsabet the Twin.** One of the two who became the Miscount; the other's name is lost on purpose.
- **Saint Calcifer** (canon, Act II boss), **the Chime-Abbot** (Act IV boss), **the Ossuary Matron** (Act I boss):
  heroes once; what they are now is in the game.

## Monsters and entities (weird, independent, told by varied witnesses)

Canon ideas to flesh out: the Long Sister (shroud-weaver who wrapped herself), the Ash-Choir (three tiny singers who
never finished the note), the Splint, the Weeping Post, the Miscount (two skeletons, one set of bones), the Un-Bell,
the Root-Bride, the Sub-Deacon (four-armed censer priest), the Late Sister (three paces behind you), the Hollow King.

Proposed new: the Inventory (counts you, takes what is not on its list); the Hinge (a door that is a creature; opens
onto a room that was never built); the Apology (a thing that says sorry before it does what it does); the Second Floor
(a house deeper inside than outside); the Chorister of Teeth; the Tideless (a drowned thing the Fen will not wet);
the Unlit (a lantern with nothing in it that still follows); the Kneeling Hundred (a congregation that is one
creature); the Reader (reads you aloud from the Lectern's book, and you do what it reads).

## Eschatology (each house has one; they do not agree)

- Pale Order: the Bearing Mother sets the Hide down at last; everything that has finished stays, everything that has
  not is given to the wind.
- Gilded Peak: the god's mouth closes; those who climbed are inside it.
- Polished Heart: the winter the dead come home for good.
- Precious Wound: the Flesh fills every hollow, and then there are no hollows, and then there is no then.
- House of Eight Million: the last doll is cut; the paper is the world.
- Empty Hand: the Silence finishes its sentence.
- Slayers: a thought that remembers no one.
- The Stranger: will not say, or says a different thing every time.

## Rules for every document

- Form and voice unique to the document: scripture, chronicle, ledger, inscription with gaps and gloss, trial record,
  letter, field notes, catechism, list, confession, sermon, treaty, inventory, marginalia, a thing's own account.
- **No poems, no songs, no verse.** Prose forms only.
- Not conversational. No "I sat at a fire and he told me". The gatherer's "by" note is the only frame.
- No commoner daily life: no feasts, recipes, children's games, village customs. The camera is on myth, history,
  entities, ends.
- Banned words (game rule): rot; cell, virus, DNA, organism, biology; cooldown, dps, proc, aggro, loot, buff, nerf,
  stun, lightning, mana. Never "Weeping Maiden" (it is the Bleeding Maiden). Miasma is breath gone wrong.
- Documents may contradict each other. They may not contradict `02-world-and-lore.md`.
- Each page 500 to 1,500 words. BBCode as in `base_codex.json`: section heads

  ornament `[center][color=#8c6a37]❧[/color][/center]`, a signature `[right][i][color=#c9974a]Name[/color][/i][/right]`,
  a scholar's gloss `[indent][i][color=#c8bfae]…[/color][/i][/indent]`, gaps in a damaged text as `[ … ]`.
