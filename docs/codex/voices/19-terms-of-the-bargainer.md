# The Terms Agreed at the Eleventh Lantern

*Folded small and pushed down into the wick-cup of the eleventh lantern on the Row at Sallow Cross, under the oil-crust; the woman who tends that lantern gave it to me unread, and asked that I not say aloud the name at the foot of it anywhere near her door.*

TERMS, drawn in a fair hand at the foot of the eleventh lantern of the Row, in the deep of the Thin Winter, when the oil froze in the cups and the Row was dark from end to end but for one. Between the Lender, who for this clause is called Ghaal-of-the-Doorstep, and ________, Orm's daughter, tender of the eleventh lantern, who is called in these terms the Asker.

**I. Of what is asked.** The Asker asks that the child Anse, of her body, four winters old and burning, live to see the thaw; and that the eleventh lantern not go out before it. The Lender grants both. The Lender writes it twice, so that it cannot be misread. The child lives. The lantern burns.

> *In the margin, a hurried hand, pressed hard:* Twice. Why twice. Read it again. Child lives, lantern burns. No "for a time". No "after a fashion". No "as the Lender sees it". I looked for those. They are not there. — H.

**II. Of what is given.** In return the Asker gives one thing, which she does not use and will not miss. The Lender, for this clause called Uthmaar, takes nothing from her hands, her blood, her bone, her breath or her sight. It takes nothing from the child. It takes nothing from the lantern. It takes nothing she could carry, sell, or bury.

> It says the child five times now. I counted with my finger. It leaves the child alone. What do I not use? I use everything. It is winter. The Shrine Keeper at the ford said never sign anything with a mark of your own. What does she know. They burned her name in a paper doll when she was twelve.

**III. Definitions, set down with the courtesy owed to one who has asked.**

*The thing not used* is that which is said of the Asker when she is not in the room. She has never once said it herself in need. She has only answered to it, as a door answers a hand.

*Missing* is an ache that requires a thing to be remembered. The Lender undertakes that the Asker will feel no ache.

*Prayer* is what the Asker's people give their lanterns, and the Pale Order its numbers, and the circles their glass. The Lender has had very little of it. A bargain is the nearest thing the Lender knows. The Lender asks the Asker to think of this paper kindly, as she would think of a lamp lit for her.

*Forgotten* is the state of a person when the last mouth that held her has let her go. It is no cruelty. Most of the dead are in it, and are quiet there.

*The Lender* is whoever holds the thing not used. For the length of this clause the Lender holds nothing, and is called nothing, and is between names.

> Said of me when I'm not there. Talk. It wants the talk of the Row. They can keep it. What they say about Orm's girl and the salt-carter. Let it eat that. I have read this four times by the lantern and Anse has stopped crying, which is worse.

**IV. Of the hour of taking.** At the thaw, at the first drip from the eave of the Asker's door. Not before. The Lender, for this clause called Nhessith, does not take from a sick house. It has been in many sick houses and knows how they smell when they are about to be empty, and it will wait outside that smell like any other caller.

**V. Of what follows the forgetting.** When the Asker is forgotten, the Lender keeps what was given, and is fed by it a little each time someone nearly remembers. The Lender undertakes that this nearly will happen often: on doorsteps, at the eleventh lantern, at the foot of this page, and in the child at the edge of sleep. Each nearly is a small prayer said to no one, and the Lender, for this clause called [ … ], gathers every one, and is grateful, and asks the Asker to understand that it has gone hungry a long time.

> I don't understand V. Nearly what? Who forgets? The Row has known me since I was carried down it on my mother's back. Anse is cooler tonight. Her hands are cooler. Her mouth is wet. I am signing.

It was a piece of one who was carried up a ramp of steps on the shoulders of a whole city, and was given its name more times in a day than there are lanterns on every road, by people who were so afraid of it that their fear was a kind of warmth, and it was fed, and it was one thing and had one name, and that name was~~

*This clause is withdrawn. It forms no part of the terms. The Lender has no standing, and remembers none.*

> What is under the line here? A different ink. Thinner. I can't read under it and I don't care to.

**VII. Of the signing.** The Asker signs with the thing given, and that is the last use of it that is hers. The Lender signs with whatever it is called at the hour of the thaw.

Signed, the Asker: ________ *(her mark beside it, a lantern drawn small, the flame done twice over)*

Signed, the Lender: Hedda, Orm's daughter, of the eleventh lantern.

> *The same hurried hand, the ink different, the letters larger:* Thaw. The eave is dripping. Anse ate bread. Anse is well. I went down the Row to tell them and Tam at the ninth lantern asked me who I was tending for, and if I was off the road, and was I looking for work. He stood godfather to Anse.

> *A third hand, upright and careful, at the very foot, below the fold:* Found in the wick-cup of the eleventh, which I tend now, as the woman who raised me tended it.
