# The Catechism of the Lord in the Hill, with Its Amendments

*Eleven leaves on one iron nail, inside the door of a hill-chapel above a dry valley that no chart I carry names; the questions in a broad hand, each amendment in a smaller one, the last no thicker than a hair. A lamp stood lit beside the nail, and no one had come up the path in a long while to light it.*

THE QUESTIONS, to be had by every soul of the valley of Dhessaral at the losing of the first tooth, and answered at the gate of the hill on the dark of each month, standing, facing the hill.

**Who is your lord?**

Aum-Khereth, who sits in the hill. He was born a man in this valley. He would not consent to die, and the valley consented for him to live.

**What does your lord require?**

Fear. He does not eat the bread and he does not drink the wine; those are set at the gate so that the asking is seen. He requires that I fear him on waking and fear him before sleep, and that I say his name inside the fear, so that he hears whose fear it is.

**How is the lord to be feared?**

Rightly, with the whole body: the cold down the back, the water in the knees, the breath kept short. A fear held halfway is a theft from the hill.

**Why do the dead of the valley stand?**

Because the lord is feared here, and the dead remember it. They stand at the field-edges with their faces to the hill. They will stand as long as I am afraid.

**What becomes of the one who forgets the lord?**

The lord remembers him. He comes down in the night and sits at the forgetter's bed until he remembers, and the forgetter wakes with his hair white. The white heads of the valley are the lord's teaching.

**What are the gods?**

Beggars. They stand at every door with a bowl and live on what is put in it. The lord begs of no one. He takes what is due.

**What is the Hush?**

The lord does not speak of it. Pass to the next question.

**When will the lord die?**

There is no next question.

---

THE FIRST AMENDMENT, in the four hundredth winter of my sitting.

The answer at the gate is to be said twice. Those who come down from the high farms say it three times, for the months they did not come.

I count four thousand and some who say my name. I have them by their breath at the gate.

---

THE SECOND AMENDMENT, in the six hundredth winter and more. I have the true number on another leaf.

Bone-counters of the Pale Order came in at the valley mouth with a white stone on a sledge and set it in the road. They teach that a number outlasts a name. Along the field-edges as far as their counting reached, my dead lay down. I went out and looked at them. They lay as if they had been waiting for leave.

To the question What are the gods, add: and thieves. The Order feeds its Mother with my valley's bones.

I count three hundred and eleven.

---

THE THIRD AMENDMENT.

Strike *rightly*. A fear held halfway is accepted. A fear of the dark is accepted, if the dark is said to be the lord's. A fear of the hill is accepted, though the hill is only a hill. I have told the dark under it to answer to my name.

To the question What becomes of the one who forgets the lord, strike all. I went down to the bed of one who had forgotten me. I sat by him until the grey. He slept well. His hair is brown.

I stood the dead up along the field-edges by my own hand. When I went back up the hill they lay down again behind me, one after another, the way a row of lamps goes out when the oil runs from the same jar.

I have seen the gods on the road. The Crone's people sit in their rings saying her names until they have no voice, in case she has gone deaf. The Maiden's people bleed into a cup to the line and say the Words over it, so she knows who it was. The Shrine Keepers feed a god to every stone. Every one of them is afraid of the quiet after the prayer. They are beggars, as I wrote. I have stood at the same doors. I was the only one of us who asked for fear outright, and I thought that made me a lord.

I count forty. Then nineteen.

---

THE FOURTH AMENDMENT.

To the question What is the Hush, the answer is now given, since there is no one left to keep it from.

The Hush is what lies between my name and the next time it is said. When four thousand said it at the gate there was no room between one saying and the next for anything to stand. Then there was a day between. Then a month. Now there are seasons, and something stands in them. It does not hate me. It does not want me. It is room. I am going into it the way a word goes into the quiet after it is said, and whoever has heard that quiet once does not remember the word.

The gods fear it as I do. I have watched them in their shrines. They are thinner than they were.

I count three. One is the old woman at the well-head who calls me Old Dread and spits toward the hill. Spitting is fear. I count her. One is a girl who is afraid of the dark under the hill and has never heard my name. I count her. The third I cannot find. On the dark of the month I count three at the gate, and two come up the path, and the third breath is cold, and stands closer than the others.

---

THE LAST AMENDMENT. To whoever has this leaf.

You are not of the valley. It does not matter now. I have struck every requirement but one.

Be afraid. I ask it. I know a lord does not ask.

Be afraid of this hill, and of the chapel, and of the lamp, which I keep lit so the leaf can be read. Be afraid of the leaf in your hand: it was written by a thing that sits in the dark under you and has not died, that was a man once in a valley that has forgotten him, that hears you now. That is true. Let it be enough to be afraid of.

The old woman is dead. The well-head is dry. The girl is grown and gone down the valley, and the dark there is somebody else's.

I do not know how many remember me. I count, and the count comes out different each time I take it, and I take it all night.
