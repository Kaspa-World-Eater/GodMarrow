# Survey of the Unclosing Ground, Taken for the Wardens of Nine Stairs

*Leaves from a surveyor's cord-case, handed in at the gate of Nine Stairs by a bowl-monk of the fallen Peak who would not say where he had it. The cord in the case had all its knots and nothing between them; I have set the leaves down with their spaces as I found them.*

Survey of the ground called the Scar, taken at the commission of the Wardens of Nine Stairs by Edric Lume, lay surveyor of the Order's roads, licensed to the cord and the plumb, in the eleven hundred and ninth year of the Count.

Instruments carried: one cord of a hundred knots, each knot a Warden's span from the last, waxed. One plumb of lead on a line of gut, inked at every span. One horn of ink, soot and bone-glue. One needle in its box. Forty stakes of bone, numbered. Bearings taken on the Unfallen the Order calls the Sixth Lamp, which does not move.

**Of the approach.**

Three days east of the last white stone of the line. The ground is warm, as all the Hide is warm, and then warmer, and on the last half-day cool. In twenty-two years on the Order's roads I had never put my hand to cool ground. Ash lies thinner. Nothing stands up out of it.

There are bowls. Wooden, gilt at the rim, set mouth-up on the ash in a line going toward the Scar, empty. The monks of the fallen Peak set them there, I am told, and call the place holy. I counted them as I count stakes. Sixty-one, each one hundred and twelve paces from the last, to the pace. Their spacing is better than mine.

North of the line, the hooded people of the Maiden have poured. They hold the Scar is a wound and must be filled. The ground there is dark with what they gave to the depth of a hand, and the dark stops a span short of the lip in a straight edge, as if a rule had been laid there. None of it reached.

**Of the Scar, as seen.**

It runs, by the needle and by the Lamp, from north and east to south and west, a little under two leagues in its long bearing. It lies flush with the ground on either side. A man at the lip does not look down into it. He looks across it, and sees the far lip, and the far lip is near enough to throw a stake to.

The two lips answer each other. Every turning of the near lip is answered by a turning of the far lip, as the two halves of a split tally answer, so that if the ground were pushed together it would close without a seam. Nothing pushes it together.

Between the lips is the Scar. The commission requires me to set down what it looks like, and I have looked at it for nineteen days. It is pale, the colour of a leaf before it is written on. It has no grain. When I look across it, my eye is at the near lip and then at the far lip, and I have no memory of the crossing. The time is taken out, as the moment is taken out between counting a stair and finding the foot already on the next. When I look away I have the feeling of having come to the end of a sentence.

Ash blows across it on the east wind and arrives on the far side. None lies on it.

**Of the measure of the rim.**

The Wardens will want a figure, and I give it. The rim is forty-one knots from one stake to the next, wherever I set the next stake.

I paced a leg at sixty and laid the cord: forty-one. I paced a leg at twelve: forty-one. I drove the ninth and tenth stakes close enough to touch and laid the cord between them, and it ran forty-one knots, and lay flat on the ash, and made no loop, and I cannot set down how. I measured the rim nine times in all. It is the only figure in this survey that has never once come out different.

Each morning the cord is shorter. It has the same count of knots. The span between knot and knot is what is going.

**Of the plumb.**

At the third stake I hung the plumb over the lip and paid out the line to the fortieth inked span. I felt no bottom. The line did not go taut, and it did not go slack. A plumb on a line always trembles, a little, with the breath of the one who holds it. This one hung still, as if it were set in glass.

I drew it up. The bob came up cool and dry and of its weight. The ink marks were gone from every span of the line that had gone below the lip, and the gut under them was whiter than the rest.

I have hung the plumb since from a stake-head on open ground, a league west. It does not tremble there either.

**Of the ink.**

What I write at the rim at evening stands farther apart by morning. No letter is lost. The spaces are wider. I left them a nail wide, as I was taught. Look at the spaces on this leaf.

**Of what is written there.**

The ash at the lip lies in grains a nail apart, and none of them touches another. The stones lie in rows along the rim, and the rows are spaced. My own footprints of the first evening did not fill. By the second morning the ash on either side of each print had drawn back from it, so that every print stood alone with a margin round it, like a word.

I saw on the eleventh day that I knew which gap came next along the rim before I came to it. On the twelfth I sat at the fifth stake from the Lamp-hour to grey and read the spaces as I would read a chart.

It is a chart. I say it as a surveyor, and the Wardens may strike me from the roll. The gaps between the stones and the grains are laid out in bearings and in lengths, at a scale I have not found, and they chart ground I know. The line of white stones is there, as gaps, and it is farther out than the Order's last count of it. Nine Stairs is there: a gap with nine steps down to its floor. The road I came by is there. My forty stakes are there, at the places I drove them, and the spaces for them lay at the rim on the first morning, before I had driven one.

There is ground on it that I do not know, a great deal of it. At its scale the Hide is a small thing near one edge.

There is no gap on it for the Scar.

The monks say the place is holy. The hooded say it is a wound. The Order's article says the Hush came in here and took the god's names one at a time, and I hold to the article; I am the Order's man and I do not hold with bowls. I set down only what the cord and the leaf have shown me: that whatever came in here came in measuring, and that it has been measuring since, and that its measures close.
