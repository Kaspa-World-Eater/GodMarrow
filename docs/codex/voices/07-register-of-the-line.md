# The Register of the Line at the Red Sill

*Chained to the reading-post of the Measured chapter at the Red Sill; the keeper of the chained Rule let me copy it by one lamp on the night after the Measure, and stood at my shoulder until I had done. The margins are as I found them.*

**Here begins the register of the line at the Red Sill, set down at its head as the Rule requires.**

*Of the beginning.* Before the first cup the wound had not opened, and there was nothing to give and no one to give it. Then the wound opened, and gave. What came out of it was flesh, and the flesh grew, because nothing told it to stop, and we are of that flesh and walk on it. The Maiden is the opening. The Mother is the growing. We give back into the wound so that it knows what it made, and whom.

*Of the end.* The Mother fills. Every hollow is filled: every wound, every mouth, every grave and room, and the space between one word and the next. When nothing is hollow there is no then. This is the Mother made whole. It is to be written carefully, and read once.

*Of the Rule.* First: blood is given in measure, to the line on the cup and no higher, for a gift must be whole to be heard, and what runs over the lip is spilled and is no one's. Second: the Words are said over it, all of them, in their order, so that she knows whom it is for. Third: once in the year, on the Day of the Measure, and on no other day. Fourth:

*(Here the leaf is left bare for the width of three fingers. The fourth is in the Elder's mouth. It is written here as a space the size of it.)*

*Of the line.* The pattern cup stands on the sill of the wound by day and goes into the Elder's sleeve by night. On the eve of each Measure the Elder sets her thumbnail on the old line, and I set the punch at the top of the nail and strike, and that is the new line, and every tithe-cup of the chapter is struck to match it. Height is reckoned in thumbnails from the foot. The Rule says the line is raised. The Rule gives no reason, and I write nothing here the Rule does not give. This is the third pattern cup of the Red Sill. The first two stand on the shelf above the door, struck to the lip.

---

**The year of the cup one thousand and four.** Line at nine thumbnails.
*For:* the dead of the winter, nineteen, laid in the bank above the sill.
*Gave:* thirty-one.
*Could not:* Sister Hennet, carrying. Brother Ost, in fever; he gave in the spring, by the Elder's leave, which the third clause does not allow and the Elder allowed.

> Cups at dawn: thirty-one, empty and dry. Heard.

**The year of the cup one thousand and five.** Line at ten.
*For:* the lamps of the lower road, that they stay lit to the ford.
*Gave:* thirty.
*Could not:* Brother Ost, dead at midwinter, laid in the bank, his cup broken over his chest by my hammer as is right.

> Ost's spring cup was the only cup the wound ever left standing full. I have told no one but the Elder, and now this leaf.

**The year of the cup one thousand and six.** Line at eleven.
*For:* Villa Llaga, word of which came at midsummer with the salt carter. That the wound there not drink alone. That she remember who they were.
*Gave:* thirty.
*Could not:* none.

> The wound asks more this year. I write it as the Elder said it: *asks*. Thirty cups dry at dawn, and the lip wet a hand below where they stood, as if something had reached past them.

**The year of the cup one thousand and seven.** Line at twelve.
*For:* the chapter, and the Rule kept in it.
*Gave:* twenty-nine.
*Could not:* Brother Caddoc. The horn knife went in at the elbow, the inside, as it should, and the arm opened and gave nothing. The Elder opened him a second time and it gave nothing. He was not cut a third time. He stood for the Words with his hand laid flat on his chest, under the breastbone, as a man holds a pain.

> Caddoc's cup came back to me for striking with the rest, and I found it licked. I know a dry cup. A dry cup has a brown ring at the line and a skin in the foot. His was clean to the clay. At the lip there were marks in the glaze the width of a tongue, going round. I showed it to the Elder. She put it in her sleeve with the pattern cup and I have not seen it since.

**The year of the cup one thousand and eight.** Line at thirteen.
*For:* the procession of the Open Vein, which came along the lower road in the eighth month, forty or more, bare to the waist and scourging, and passed our sill without stopping, though we stood out and said the Words to them. That their blood be counted as a measure, though it was a flood.
*Gave:* twenty-seven.
*Could not:* Sister Imm and Brother Tallow, who walked after the procession at dusk and are struck from the line.

> The Open Vein did not come home. The road is wet where they passed, from the ford to the second milestone, and has stayed wet through the dry months. A flood is spilled, and is no one's. The Elder says so and I say so. Still, the wet goes down a little every night and is back by dusk.

> One walked at the back of them in a whole robe, unscourged, closed to the throat, with a hand flat on his chest. I write it because no one else said they saw him.

**The year of the cup one thousand and nine.** Line at fourteen.
*For:* that the wound be quiet in the night. It has been heard in the bank.
*Gave:* twenty-six.
*Could not:* Sister Hennet, weak since her lying-in. Her child, too young for the knife.

> Brother Caddoc gave to the line this year, full and clean, and I watched the knife go in. In the morning there was no mark inside his elbow. None. He runs warm. You feel it when he stands by you in the line, through two robes.

> The Elder has told the chapter that a brother who will not open his robe is not to be given the cup. She did not look at Caddoc. He opened his robe to the waist when she asked it of all of us, and there was a seam of new skin under his breastbone, pale, the length of a finger, and the Elder said nothing, and passed to the next.

**The year of the cup one thousand and ten.** Line at fifteen.
*For:* the Elder has not said.
*Gave:* twenty-four.
*Could not:* Sister Hennet, laid in the bank. Brother Caddoc, gone in the night of the Measure; his robe on its peg, his cup on the sill, empty and wet.

> Three thumbnails of the pattern cup are left below the lip. The Elder has told me to begin the fourth cup, and has had the clay carried up from the lower lip. I asked her how wide. She did not answer. The first cup on the shelf holds a third of what the second holds, and the second half of the third. I have measured them. I was not asked to.

> Twenty-four. The bank has more in it than the line.

> Tonight, after the cups were set on the sill and we had gone in, I went back for the punch, which I had left. The Elder was at the wound alone, with her back to the door, and she was saying the Words over the cups. I knew every one of them. I did not know the order. I have said the Words every year since I was given a knife, and I could not follow her, and when I tried to follow her my mouth
