# Warrant of Closing against Hollin Ford, with Its Schedule and the Return

*Found nailed face-inward to the lintel of the well-shed at Hollin Ford, where no one has lived since; the nail was bone. The back is in the same hand as the front but smaller, and one line of it is struck through.*

**Under the Count. Warrant of closing, in the eleven hundred and thirteenth year from the last breath, root six.**

Issued at the Ninth Stair of Nine Stairs by night, by the knight-captain of the low road, entered in the book as Nine Hundred and Ninety, root nine, who has no other name, to himself and the eight who go with him, and to the standing dead of the Kostyak who carry for them.

This warrant bears no Warden's mark. It is not to be shown at any Stair. The Warden of Nine Stairs will enter what is brought in, as he enters all that comes in, and will ask nothing, and that is his part.

**Of the reason for all closing.**

The Bearing Mother waits under us with her frame unfinished. Every bone of hers that walks about in the lowlands under flesh is a bone withheld from her. The Count rises at every Feast and is called her gift, and the brothers on the stairs are content with it, and wait for the dead to come in at their own pace, as rain comes into a cistern. We say plainly that this waiting is a failure of devotion. A figure left open is a debt. The Count will close at the Last Number and she will set the world down, and every frame still walking on that day is given to the wind with the flesh on it. To close a frame before its time is to save it.

The Count at the Ninth Stair stood at the last Feast at a figure whose root is four. A whole frame is two hundred and six, root eight. Thirteen whole frames brought home make two thousand six hundred and seventy-eight, root five. Four and five are nine. Nine closes a column. Therefore thirteen frames, and no fewer, and no more.

**The village.**

Hollin Ford, on the low road, two days below the white stones. Counted living by this captain on the first night from the ford-bank: eighty-one frames, root nine, which is a sign. Of these, thirteen are entered below.

**The schedule of frames.**

*The first.* The lantern-keeper at the ford, old. His frame is finished; only flesh holds it from the niche. He has knelt to the Order's tally-stone every year of his life. He is closed first, and with honour.

*The second.* The woman who gives blood to the hooded processions when they pass, in measure, into their cup. Her marrow is being spent into another's mouth. What is spent cannot be laid. Closed before it is all gone.

*The third.* The man who gave his name into the bowl of the beggar-monks from the Peak, the spring past. He answers to nothing now. A frame without a name is already halfway home. Closed.

*The fourth and fifth.* The twins of the mill. Two frames that the lowlanders count as one, and the lowland count is wrong. Closed, and entered as two.

*The sixth.* The well-digger. He says his bones ache in wet weather. His bones are asking.

*The seventh.* The widow who keeps a glass uncovered in her house and talks to it at night. Whatever is in the glass, it is not entered anywhere. Closed, and the glass broken face-down on the ash.

*The eighth.* The girl who was born the winter the Feast count came out one higher at the Fifth Wall. Closed for the coincidence of the figure, which is reason enough.

*The ninth to the thirteenth.* The household at the ford-bridge: the man, his mother, his wife, two sons. In the spring they pulled down the tally-stone at the ford and broke its notches with a hammer. The tally was the Order's and they unmade it at night. That is a debt to the Count, and a debt to the Count is paid in frames. Five frames, closed together as one payment.

**The rite.**

The Short Laying, which the lowlands call the Telling. The frame is laid on its back on bare ground. The captain kneels at the crown, lays his palm on each bone as he names it, and tells the frame aloud by number, from the crown down, as a novice tells his own frame on the cord. At two hundred and five, the last small bone of the left foot but one, he stops, and waits one breath, and says two hundred and six. The frame is then closed, and the breath goes out of it, and does not come in again.

Flesh to the wind, on the ridge above the ford, facing away from the village. Bone washed in the ford by the standing dead. Carried home on the yokes. Chrism joint by joint at the Ninth Stair, niche, number. No name is entered for any of the thirteen. A name goes out on the breath that said it. A number is laid.

No closing is to be done in sight of a lantern. No frame is to be told twice.

Given at the Ninth Stair. Nine Hundred and Ninety, root nine.

---

*(On the back.)*

**Return of the captain, written at the white stones, the fourth night after.**

Closed at Hollin Ford by the Short Laying: thirteen frames, as entered. The first to the eighth on the first night. The household at the ford-bridge on the second night, in the yard, one after another, the man last. He told along with me from the crown to the ribs and then stopped. I finished it.

The seventh, the widow: the glass broken on the ash as ordered. She asked me, before the telling, whether I would cover it. I did not answer. It was broken face-down.

The eighth was told and closed. The mother of the eighth was not on the schedule and was not closed.

Flesh to the wind on the ridge. Bone washed in the ford. The washing was done by the standing dead and took all of the third day, the water being cold, and they washed with care, and I saw nothing in it amiss.

Frames brought to the white stones on the yokes, counted by me at the stones: fourteen. Counted a second time, by the tallier of the company, with his eyes shut and his hand on each: fourteen. Each whole. Two hundred and six. Root eight.

I have gone through the thirteen by their place on the schedule and set each on the ground by its place, and there is a frame left over, and it is whole, and it is laid on its yoke as the others are, facing the same way. It does not belong to any line of this warrant. No one at Hollin Ford was closed who was not entered. I am sure of the thirteen.

Fourteen frames is two thousand eight hundred and eighty-four, root four. Four and four are eight. The figure at the Ninth Stair, when these are entered, will stand at root eight. It wanted nine. The column does not close.

I enter all fourteen. A bone brought home is not left on the road. The Warden will enter what comes in, and will ask nothing.

~~Nine of us went down to the ford, and I have counted the company at every halt since, and I come to eight each time, and each of the eight answers to his number.~~

Returned under the Count. Nine Hundred and Ninety, root nine.
