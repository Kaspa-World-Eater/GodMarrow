# The Hearth-Roll of Hathe Under

*The roll came back to the Office at Tessaly Gate bound in its own cord, carried by a carter who had found it on the chair at a window and would not say which window. The last leaf is in the counter's hand, or near it.*

Roll of the hearths of Hathe Under, in the reach of the Office at Tessaly Gate, taken in the year eleven hundred and fourteen of the common count by the Office's counter, the last roll being twelve years old and lying open beside this one. Columns as the Office sets them: names and ages, trade, beds, dreams reported. The Office asks the last column of every town in the reach, so that it may know where the sleepers are troubled and send lamps there. I take it as I am bid.

The townsfolk call the tower on the rise the Tower That Drinks, or Above. The reeve's book has an older name for it, Vhul-Oghra, written small in the margin of the first leaf. The reeve asked that I not say it in his kitchen. I have not.

**Hearth the first.** Odo Raskin, reeve, fifty-one; Mald, his wife, forty-eight; Hew, their son, nineteen. Trade: the reeve keeps the two roads in and the town's dead. Beds: three, made. Dreams reported: the stair.

**Hearth the second.** Tenna Coyle, widow, sixty; her daughter's two, girls, nine and eleven. Trade: glazier. She keeps the town's glass clear on the tower side. Beds: two, made. Dreams reported: the stair, going up.

*Remark.* Every house in Hathe Under stands with its face to the tower and its back blind. There is no glass on any other side, so the glazier has only the one side to keep. Where a lane bends, the houses bend their fronts round with it, so that a man walking any lane has doors and windows on his one hand and the tower over them. I note it because a town built so was built on purpose, and the Office will want to know by whom. Nobody here can say.

**Hearth the third.** Cobb Asher, lamp-keeper, forty; Ide his wife, thirty-eight; three sons, sixteen, twelve, six. Trade: keeps the eleven street-lanterns, all hung on the tower side of their posts. Beds: four, made. Dreams reported: the stair.

*Remark.* I have entered every bed in Hathe Under as made, because every one is, at whatever hour I knock: the cover drawn tight, the bolster smooth, cold to the hand. In every front room there are chairs drawn close to the glass, one to each soul of the hearth, and the seats are worn through in the shape of sitting. The lamp-keeper's wife told me they sleep there. The beds are kept for the roll, she said, since the Office has always asked after beds.

**Hearth the fourth.** Gone up. The last roll has Brannoc Weld, gravedigger, his wife, his mother, four children. Door open. Beds made. Seven chairs at the glass, and dust on six of them.

**Hearth the fifth.** Gone up. The last roll has the Lusk sisters, washers of the dead. Door open. Beds made. Two chairs.

**Hearth the sixth.** Piers Hale, road-mender, thirty-three, alone. Beds: one, made. Dreams reported: the stair. He said it before I had finished asking.

*Remark on the column.* I put the question as the Office words it: what have you dreamed since the last new moon? In other towns of the reach I have had drownings, teeth, the dead at table, a mother's voice in a well, a great many falls. In Hathe Under I have had the stair from every mouth, and from children too small to have the word for a stair, who point. Nobody has dreamed of bread, or water, or a face, or of the dead they buried. I asked the widow Coyle what the stair was like. She said it was the same one, and looked past me through her glass.

*Remark on the tower.* It stands a short mile off on the rise, broad as a ridge-ossuary and higher than any I have climbed. I can find no door at the foot. A stair goes round it on the outside from the ground to the crown, wide enough for two, and the middle of every step is worn into a trough, as a sill is worn by water. The households that are gone went up it; the town says so the way it says the lanterns are lit. No one says they came down. At dusk the tower shows lights in very many places, small and wet, and more of them than it has windows. Each is single. They do not gutter as a flame does. When the wind is still, they move all together, a little, toward whoever is looking. From the reeve's glass I counted them to two hundred and some, and lost the count, and did not begin it again.

**Hearth the seventh.** Gone up.

**Hearth the eighth.** Gone up.

**Hearth the ninth.** Aud Penner, salt-carrier, seventy; Rafe, his grandson, fourteen. Beds: two, made. Dreams reported: the stair. The old man added that he had dreamed it seventy years and the boy fourteen, and that it was the same length for both.

*Remark.* The wayside stone at the east road was a Shrine Keepers' stone once; the cup is still cut in it. It has turned to face the wall it stands against, and nobody here keeps it or knows what it was for. Hathe Under has no niche, no cup, no glass hung for the dead, no faith I know. When their dead are washed they are sat in the chair a last night, facing out, and buried the next day, and the reeve says the grave is always lighter than the body was. I set down that this is heathen and that I have entered it.

**Hearth the third, again.** I came back by the lamp-keeper's on the fourth morning to correct an age. Door open. Beds made. Five chairs. I have not struck the entry above. I enter beneath it: gone up, the night after the roll was taken.

*Remark, for the Office only.* At the reeve's I was given the back room, which has a bed and no window. The first night I slept in the bed and dreamed of the yard at Tessaly, and the well, and my sister, and woke glad of all of it. The second night I dreamed nothing I can set down. The third night I woke in a chair in the front room with my face to the glass. I had carried it there myself, it seems, since it is the chair from the back room. My bed was made. I had not made it.

**Hearth the twelfth.** Gone up. **Thirteenth.** Gone up. **Fourteenth.** A chair at the glass with a woman in it, who would not turn round. Names not given. Trade not given. Beds: one, made. Dreams reported: entered on her behalf as the stair, the column admitting no other answer in this town.

*Tally.* Hearths in the last roll, sixty-one. Hearths standing at this roll, thirty-eight. Souls, one hundred and forty-four. Gone up between the two rolls, by the reeve's account, nobody; by mine, ninety-six. The reeve and I have gone through it twice and we cannot both be right, and he says the Office's count has always been the higher.

**Hearth the first, again.** Door open. Beds made. Three chairs, and a fourth set at the end of the row, which is mine.

**Lodger.** Wat Fenner, counter to the Office at Tessaly Gate, forty-one, lodged at the reeve's. Trade: counting. Beds: one, made. Dreams reported: the stair, going up. The step worn to my foot exactly.
