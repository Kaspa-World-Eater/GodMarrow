# The Leaf-Book of One of the Gilt

*A goldbeater's book of tissues, with gold still between some of them, bought from the lantern-trimmer of the square at Harrow Market, who kept it wrapped in sacking and would not open it in daylight. The name at the head of the first tissue I have not copied, because I could not hold it in my eye long enough to copy it.*

[ … ], of the Gilt, shaved on the stair of the Gilded Peak, below the mountain on the night the courtyard became glass.

There are ninety tissues in this book, and between each two a leaf of gold, beaten at the Peak by the lay goldbeaters before that night. Each day at noon I lift a leaf and lay it, and on the tissue it lay on I write where. When the tissues are written the book is done, and so am I. That is the whole rule of the Gilt. I set it here so that whoever carries this book after me knows how far I am by how much is left.

**The first leaf.** The nail of the little finger of the left hand. Laid on the road below the Cheek, the breath held, so the leaf would not lift. Nothing changed. I write it so the book has a beginning.

**The second leaf.** The nail beside it. A woman at a well gave me water and looked at the hand a long while, and asked if it hurt. It does not.

**The fifth leaf.** The last finger of the left hand, to the root. The gold takes to skin as it took to bone on the Peak, by breath and warmth. The skin under it is warm.

On the stair we were taught that the Silence holds out two hands. The closed hand is Absence, and erases with dark. The open hand is Radiance, and erases with light. The monks of the closed hand go into the dark to be unseen, and I do not fault them, but a thief at a window can do as much, and a thing hidden in the dark is still there, waiting for a lamp. The open hand hides nothing. It stands at noon in the square in full sight and is looked at until there is nothing left to see. A face looked at long enough comes apart into light, as a word said over and over comes apart into sound and then into the pause around it. The Peak laid gold on everything it honoured until the thing could not be seen. We are laying it on the last thing.

**The twelfth leaf.** The right hand finished. At the inn at Four Wents I paid for bread. The keeper took the coins off my palm and looked at the palm and through it, at the board. He has stopped seeing my hands. I held out the bowl and he filled it as if it had come up the road by itself.

**The sixteenth leaf.** The inside of the left wrist. I no longer count days by the sun. I count them by the book.

The lowland faiths all hunger for the same thing and call the hunger devotion. The Pale Order stands its dead in rows and cuts a number into each skull, so that each will be looked at for as long as there is anyone to look. The Maiden's processions walk their wounds open down the street so that every eye will take a little of the blood. The Crone's circles keep the soul in glass, where anyone may look at it, and call that mercy. To be seen and kept. A god is something that has been looked at too long by too many, until the looking grows a body. I would not make one of myself. I want to be the place the looking goes into and does not come back from.

**The twenty-second leaf.** The left forearm, its length. In the lane by the market a child walked into me, at noon, as one walks into a doorpost in the dark, and sat down hard and cried, and looked about for what she had struck. Her mother lifted her and looked about also. I stood an arm's length off with the sun full on me. They looked at the place I stood, and then at each other, and went.

**The thirty-first leaf.** The throat, left side. The woman at the well called me the monk at the second leaf, and the gold one at the twentieth. Today she drew the water and set the cup on the well-step and called me nothing. A four of the Bowl passed me on the road going down. I knew the second of them; we were shaved the same morning. He said the syllable and held the pause, and in the pause I waited for my name, and he went by. This morning I read the head of the first tissue. There is a word there. I read it as a man reads a word in the tongue of his childhood, that he was taught and has not used.

**The thirty-ninth leaf.** The chest. Breathing under the gold is close work. The leaf rises and falls.

In the long hall of the Peak stood the saints who were gilded breathing. Novices were told never to stand before them, because they were still looking, and what they looked at did not stay. I stood before one once, on a dare. I remember the dare. I do not remember who dared me, or what was beside me in the hall when I went in.

**The fifty-second leaf.** The right ear. At the ford there is a bronze plate on the post of the ford's small god, polished by hands. I looked into it for my face. It gave back the noon. On the stones behind me there was no shadow.

**The sixtieth leaf.** The neck, all round. I have laid sixty. I counted the leaves left between the tissues. There are thirty-one. Yesterday there were thirty.

Whoever carries this: do not grieve. Grief is a looking. When the last leaf is laid on the lids I will stand in the square at the noon bell, and you will look at the place, and your looking will go into the light and be held there, as a name goes into the bowl and the water closes. I will be the margin of the page. The meaning is in the margin.

**Sixty-eight.** The lips.

**Seventy.** The brow.

**Seventy-one.** The left lid.

**Seventy-two.** The right.

**Seventy-three.** Noon.

I am Hew Tallow, trimmer of the lanterns of the square, and I write in this book because someone else is writing in it. I found it on the well-step at the noon bell, open, with a gold pen in the fold. I took it to my bench and turned to the lamps, and when I turned back there was a line under the last line, and the ink was wet. It said the number after seventy-three. Nothing after the number.

I have watched the page since. The pen lies in the fold. When I look away, there is another number.

**Seventy-nine.**

The gold is going out of the book. I have not touched it. I open it at the lamp-hour and there are fewer leaves between the tissues, and I counted them once and will not again.

**Eighty-four.**

The bench is warm. The stool by the bench is warm, and I have not sat on it. When I trim the lamps in the square at noon I do not look at the well-step.
