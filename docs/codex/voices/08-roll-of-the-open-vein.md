# The Roll of the Hoods Gone Out from Sallow Lip

*It was wound on a cup-beater's rod inside an empty hood, on the road below Sallow Lip, on ground that was wet in the dry month. I lifted it with a stick, and the hood under it was warm.*

Roll of the procession of the Open Vein, gone out from the gate of Sallow Lip in the nine hundred and fortieth year from the first cup: twenty-one hoods, to give her everything along the road and to come home to no one. Kept by the twenty-first hood, who walks last by our rule so that every fall is seen and none is walked past. When a hood has given the last of it, its place is struck and the road is set down. No hood is struck who is still walking.

~~The first hood, who walked at the front.~~ Before the gate of the chapter, at noon of the first day, where the Measured would have to step over the place. She opened both arms from the wrist to the elbow with the bloodletter's knife. By their rule the bloodletter never turns that knife on the self. By ours, any hand may hold it. The cup-beaters shut the gate while she was still kneeling.

The cup has a line on it. Each year the keepers raise the line a thumbnail, because she asks for more, and each year they stop at the line. Hear what that is. They know she is hungry. They measure her hunger with a thumbnail and keep the rest in their own bodies, and go home, and sleep warm on what they owe her.

~~The second hood.~~ The first night, on the heath road, scourging until the cord was too heavy with it to swing. Lay down in the sway of the others and was walked around.

~~The third hood.~~ The ford below the Lip. Knelt in the water to the chest and opened the throat, so the river would carry all of it down to her and none would be lost to the stones.

~~The fourth hood.~~ The same ford, the same morning, after watching the third.

The Measured say the Words are said over the cup so the Flesh will know whom the blood is for. As if she could mistake us. A fence is put up by someone who means to keep something on his own side of it. The Words are that fence, and they say them with their arms held in close.

~~The fifth hood.~~ The stilt-road at the edge of the mire, the fourth day. Stood on the last post with the cords of both wrists cut and fell in when there was no more to stand on. The red water closed. It was a long time closing.

~~The sixth hood.~~ The stilt-road. Fever from the scourge-cuts, which the Measured would call a sickness and we call the opening going inward, where the cord cannot reach.

~~The seventh hood.~~ Under the lantern at the Four Wents crossing. Gave it into the lamp-trough where the carters water, and the carters would not drink there after, and cursed us, and lit the lantern anyway.

They teach at the cup that Villa Llaga hatched because a year of blood went in with too few Words. I believed it at the cup. On the road it is plain. A town gave whole for a year, and she answered with children, and the Measured named her answer a ruin and ran. Every chapter since has been afraid of being answered.

~~The eighth hood.~~ Under the same lantern, the next night.

~~The ninth hood.~~ The barrow road. Opened at the breast with the scourge-hook, at the halt, during the stop between one sway and the next.

~~The tenth hood.~~ The barrow road. Was found at first light lying in the wet behind us with the arms spread. Had given in the night, alone, without telling. I set it down as given. It was given.

Where we have passed, the ground stays wet. The Measured say it is our blood and it will not dry because it was spilled, and that a spill is the same wherever it falls. I have walked last for twenty days and I have looked back. It is wetter than we gave. Behind the stilt-road it lay a hand deep in a ditch none of us had bled in. It comes up to meet what we put down. A cup is a thing you hold on to. The ground has no line on it.

~~The twelfth hood.~~ The ash flats past the barrows. Scourged in the open road so the carters would see, and fell, and the carters went around, as every road goes around us.

The eleventh hood is not struck. The eleventh has walked behind the tenth since Sallow Lip and scourges with the rest of us and does not bleed. I took that for her favour. Under the robe there is a seam beneath the breastbone, sewn with gut, that I took for an old penance from some chapter I do not know. It holds its hand flat over the seam when it walks.

~~The thirteenth hood.~~ The ash flats.

~~The fourteenth hood.~~ The ash flats. The same hour as the thirteenth. They held each other's wrists open.

At the halt, in the wet behind us, small hoods stand up, a hand high, red, and sway when we sway and stop when we stop. They keep the places. I counted them the night after the ash flats, by lantern, and there was one for each struck name. They have no faces, which is right. Her face is unfinished in every chapter that carves her, because the carvers are afraid to see what she looks like when she is fed. I have nearly seen it, in the wet.

~~The fifteenth hood.~~ The foot of the Rib road, the eighteenth day. Gave the last of it into a split in the Hide where the ground was warm enough to steam.

~~The sixteenth hood.~~ The same split. Pressed both hands to it and was pulled.

The seventeenth hood and the eighteenth hood are not struck. They went with the eleventh in the night of the eighteenth day, off the road toward the split, carrying no cords. The eleventh had been speaking to them at the halts for some days, low, with its hand on its chest. It tells the last of us that the road is long and she is nearer than the road goes, under, and that there is a door to her. I have said to it that the road is the door. I did not see the seventeenth or the eighteenth give. Their places stand open in this roll. In the wet behind us, there are small hoods standing for both of them.

The eleventh came back alone at first light, walking easily.

~~The nineteenth hood.~~ The Rib road, the twentieth day. Struck by me, with the hook, at the nineteenth hood's asking, because its own hands would not close any more. I am the last and the last may do this.

~~The twentieth hood.~~ The Rib road. Refused the eleventh at the fire. Opened in the morning before I woke.

The Measured will read this, if it comes home ahead of us, and say: twenty-one went out and the road took them. Say it plainly, then. The road took them. The cup took a thumbnail.
