# Notes from Below the Ninth

*Loose leaves found in the dust behind the hanging on the Ninth Stair at Nine Stairs, chalked on both faces and tied with a novice's cord cut clean through; I have set them in the order of the numbers at their heads, and they lay in another order.*

**First descent.** The eleven hundred and ninth year of the Count, three nights before the Feast.

Behind the board of the running Count, behind the hanging, the stair goes on. The Wardens call that place the foot of the Ninth. There is no foot. Thirty-one steps down, cut with a narrower chisel than ours and to a shorter tread, and then a door. Its lintel is one bone, longer than I am tall, smooth as a thing long held in the hand. Notches run along its face as on a tally-stick. I counted them twice. Two hundred and six, then two hundred and seven.

[Rubbing of the lintel, left end. Long notches in fours, a short stroke crossing each fourth. One notch cut deeper than the rest, with a pit at its foot like the start of a drill.]

Chalk, lamp, cord. I tied my teacher's cord to the stair-post and paid out forty knots, and came up by them.

**Second descent.** Thirty-one steps going down. Twenty-nine coming up. I chalked every tread and the chalk marks agree with neither: thirty. The cord was long enough both ways.

**Fourth descent.** I did not write the third. I went no farther than the door and stood under the lintel while my lamp burned a finger's width.

A gloss. The far ossuaries kept tallies on sticks before the books, and the Undercount reads the lower notches as those are read: the long notch for a frame laid, the short for a bone wanting, the crossing stroke for a count closed. Read so, the lintel holds a count. I took it to its root. Root seven.

The board above stood at root seven at the last Feast.

One count in nine will agree by chance. I have written that out three times on this leaf, and it is still true, and I have rubbed it out.

**Fifth descent.** A second door, sixty steps below the first. A third below that. Each lintel one bone, each notched end to end, and each, taken to root, is the count of a Feast. The second door holds the Feast of the year before I was given my number. The first holds the last. The third holds this year's, which has not been counted yet, and the Wardens in Agreement have not yet said what the gift will be.

[Rubbing of the third lintel. The notches of the left end are brown and hard, filled with old dust. Those of the right end are pale. The dust in the groove came away soft on the chalk.]

The gift is cut here before it is given.

**Seventh descent.** A sound below the third door. One stroke, a long breath, one stroke. A chisel on bone makes a dry sound, and this was dry. I counted forty strokes and went up and did not count the stair.

**Ninth descent.** I have not told the Undercount what I have. Brother Ninety-Four of our number gave his numbers to the Wardens at a Feast when I was a novice. The Ninefold came for him before the frost. He has a niche on the Sixth Wall now, laid whole, two hundred and six, root eight, closed, and his count below was not finished, and they called it devotion to close it for him. They call a count kept outside the board a second count, and a second count a heresy. If they find these leaves, let them see that I have taken no frame and carried no dead. I carry chalk.

**Twelfth descent.** The ninth door. Its lintel has letters in place of notches, the only letters I have found below, cut in a hand with no likeness to any book's.

[Rubbing: KHURR-OSSAT, or KHUR-OSSAAT; the doubling may belong to either half. A notch before it and a notch after, each crossed.]

I do not know how it is said. With the first sound rolled it is a throat being cleared. With the second half drawn long it is a name you would call down a stair. I said it once each way, aloud. The chisel below stopped both times, and started again when I was quiet.

**Thirteenth descent.** Measures. The hall behind the ninth door is forty-four paces across, wall to wall, along my cord. A gallery runs round its outside, and I walked the gallery with my hand on the outer face of the same wall, and the hall is twenty-two paces round.

The niches here face inward, away from every stair. They are empty. Our dead stand facing the stair so they may see what comes up it. These were cut for something that looked at the wall.

**Fifteenth descent.** I have added down the gift-column. No one may add it down, and the Rule does not say why, and now I can say why. I will not write the sum. I found it cut on the eleventh lintel, crossed.

**Sixteenth descent.** What I believe, set down once so that it is set down.

The Count was never the Order's. We were not first beside the Mother, if she is the Mother. Something stood in these halls before any brother and began cutting, and it has not stopped. It keeps the Count, a year ahead of us, and the Wardens read the board of the Ninth off the first lintel, and the first lintel off the second, and so on down, and we call the rise a gift because we cannot see who is giving it. Every frame we lay was a notch before it was a frame. Every Feast is a copy made in good faith.

The Ninefold close counts early to make the Count close. They are closing ours. The one below has never been closed.

**Nineteenth descent.** The chisel stops when I set the lamp down. It waits for the lamp.

**Nineteenth descent.** White dust in both sleeves, fine as sifted chalk. I did not cut anything.

**Twentieth.** Eleventh hall. A frame stands in the wall. It is the wall: the ribs go into the courses and come out as courses, and the arm is a lintel-bone with a joint at its end worn smooth from bending. It faces away from me, into the stone. Its hand is at the lintel before it. The hand holds nothing. The notch under the hand is new.

I did not count it. I could not find where it stopped.

**Twenty.** cord short. I did not cut it.

**Twenty.** Lintel of the twelfth door. Seven hundred and seventy. My number. Root five.

[Rubbing. The long notches, the short, and through the last of them the crossing stroke, pale, the dust still soft]
