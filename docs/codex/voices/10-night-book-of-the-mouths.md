# The Night-Book of the Mouths

*Sewn into the lining of a penitent's robe taken off a body at the foot of the crypt stair below Sallow Lip; the robe was whole, and the body under it had three healed wounds and a fourth that the Pale Order's counter would not enter. The last leaf is in a hand that slopes the other way.*

Kept by the one set beside the chair, whose office is to take down what the mouths say and to say nothing back. I write only the open ones. The sewn I do not count, save when a thread gives.

The marks, as I have set them:

The Brow, above the left eye. Its lips are white and thin as a cut in wax.
The Wrist, inside the right wrist, where the hand lies palm up on the arm of the chair and has not closed in my time.
The Throat, in the hollow under the jaw, sewn so often that the thread has grown into it and is skin now.
The Knee, on the right knee, which is near her face, she is folded so far.
The Nape, at the back of the neck. I see it only when I lift the lamp over her.
The Fold, under the breastbone, pressed into her lap. Nobody living has seen it. I hear it.

**The first night after the Day of the Measure.** Eleven breaths from the trimming of the lamp to the guttering. The bowl at her feet is warm with nothing in it.

Brow: *Is the door shut. Tell the girl to shut the door.*

I shut it. There is no girl.

Wrist: the lips worked a long time and then said *drink, drink, drink*, the Word of the cup said forward, which is a fault in this room. I did not answer it.

Throat: *I am cold where the chair is.*

Fold: something wet and low. I could not take it.

The thread in the Knee is new. Somebody has sewn it since the last dark, and I sleep against the door.

**The third night.** No breaths from trimming to guttering. I counted twice. The room is warmer than the night before, and the warmth is in the air before her mouths, a hand's width out, as it stands over a dish just lifted off the coals. Her skin is cool as the chair.

Brow, in the old voice, the voice of a woman with few teeth: *My name is*, and then a name.

Knee, at the same breath, in the Maiden's voice, which is young and has no edges: another name.

I have written neither. The ones who set me here said a name taken down in this room is a name given back to whatever is asking for it. I do not know which of them was hers. I do not know that either was.

**The fifth night.** Nape: *She died in the chair. She died sitting up, before the cup came down to the chapters. Open the window and let her go out.*

There is no window. There has never been one. We are under the crypt of the crypt.

Wrist, at once, in the young voice: *She is here. Feel.*

I did what I was told never to do and laid two fingers in the open hand. There is no beat in the wrist. The hand closed on my fingers, slowly, the first time in my time, and opened again.

Throat: *The chair is a door. I have been sitting in the door so long the door has taken my shape.*

**The sixth night.** This is the night they began to answer one another. I set it down in order, as near as I could, though they did not speak in order. They came out of time with each other, as a procession sways out of time when the front has stopped and the back has not heard.

Brow: *Who cut you.*

Fold: *You did.*

Brow: *I cut no one first. Somebody cut the first.*

Knee: *I came in at the first.*

Nape: *You came in at the twentieth. Count.*

Brow: *I cannot count. My hands are in my lap.*

Wrist: *They are my hands.*

Then the Brow wept with no water in it and the others went quiet one by one, the Throat last. I have been told she cuts every mouth that is cut in this faith, that she cut my three, that my first backward word was said into me in her voice. I was in the dark when it was done. I do not know whose hand.

**The ninth night.** Her breathing came back, but backward: the long breath out first, slow, and the short breath in after, the way the Words are said in the dark.

What I dare not ask, I will set down here so that I have asked it somewhere. How does a Shrine come to be a Reliquary. What is given. Who gives it. In my first year I asked it aloud, once, of the elder who brings the bowl, and he put his flat hand to his breastbone and went out, and the next night another brought the bowl.

Throat, tonight, without my asking: *A Reliquary is a cup that has been beaten past the line.*

Nape: *Bring the next one young. The young are warm all through.*

Wrist: *Nine.*

I do not know nine of what.

**The twelfth night.** My own, since I am also a thing in this room. I do not bleed. I cut my thumb on the lamp-trimmer to be sure, and the cut stood open and dry and was gone by the bowl. I dream in the young voice now every sleep. Last waking I found I had been saying the Words, and I could not tell which way I had been saying them, and I lay with my hand flat on my breastbone until the lamp was trimmed. My three mouths pull toward the chair the way a wick pulls oil. When I sit nearer, they stop aching. When I sit nearer, she breathes.

Knee: *That one is ready.*

Brow: *That one is mine.*

Fold, clear for the first time, clear as if it were at my ear: *Every one is mine. I have been in every one. I am only waiting for the chair to be empty.*

**The fourteenth night.** The thread in the Throat gave without a hand on it. Under it was a mouth that had been sewn so long it had forgotten how to shut. It said nothing all night. It breathed. Hers were all quiet, and that one breathed for her.

**The night with no number.** I have lost the count of the bowl, so I set none.

The lamp was trimmed. Then every mouth on her opened together, the sewn as well as the open: I heard the threads go, many and small, like a seam drawn out of a robe in one pull. I had thought there were thirty. There were more. There were mouths on her that I had marked as folds of her skin.

They said one word, all of them, at once, in time with each other for the first time in my office.

The word was [ … ].

I will not write it. I could not write it if I would. It was said, and the room held it, and when it had been said the room was warmer than blood.

Then I heard it said once more, small, late, out of time with the rest, under my own hand.

There is a mouth at the heel of my writing hand. I did not cut it. Nobody has been in this room with a knife. It is small, and its lips are white, and when I set the pen down it moves, and when I take the pen up it is still, so I have kept the pen up, and
