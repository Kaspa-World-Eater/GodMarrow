# Report of the Kostyak from Below the Fallen Star

*Copied from a roll in the Warden's press at Long Stair, tied with black cord and sealed with a knuckle-bone pressed in wax; the postscripts are in three hands, the last in chalk, and I have kept them where they stand.*

To the Warden of Long Stair, from the knight of the Kostyak who wears the plate cut with the names of Ossin, Haaruth and Brother Two Hundred and Sixty, in the eleven hundred and second year of the Count, root four. Let it be entered.

**First article. Of the charge.** On the night the star-watchers marked, one of the Unfallen came down east of the white stones, outside the white lands, and the Seeker was sent after it. I was given to ride with him, with a tallier of the Third Wall and nine of my standing dead. We kept the low road eleven nights. The Seeker walked ahead with his face to the ground. He said the star had gone down into the Hide, under the skin of it.

**Second article. Of the descent.** The place is a throat in the ground, hot at the lip. We left the mounts standing there. The first part of the way down is rough, as any wound in the Hide is rough. After two hundred steps the way is cut. The treads are cut to our measure. I did not miss one in the dark. We went down nine flights. I counted them and the tallier counted them, and we agree, and I enter that we agree because it was the last thing we agreed on.

**Third article. Of the floor.** The floor of the cavern is bone. No earth shows, and no stone. Frames lie on frames, unlaid, in no order, skulls among ribs among the long bones, and they go down farther than our lances. The tallier counted one square of floor a pace on each side, the skulls only, to the depth of his arm. He counted forty-one. He counted the same square again and gave me sixty. He counted a third time with my hand on his shoulder and gave me a number I will not write, because a pace of floor cannot hold it, and he wept that it was correct. I took it to its root. Root nine. He says every count he has taken since comes to nine. I enter the first two numbers and do not stand behind the third.

I do not know whose dead these are. They are older than any count. Some of the skulls have been worn smooth on one side, the side that faces inward.

**Fourth article. Of the shape of the cavern.** Warden, I knew the way. The stair comes in at the left of the long wall. Past it is the Long Hall, and the six walls go off it as ours go off ours, the Third Wall where our Third Wall is, so that the tallier went to it without being told. There is a well where our Fifth Well is, and it is dry, and the sides of it are polished. At the head of the Long Hall, where your chair stands at Long Stair, there is a seat.

The walls have niches cut in them, in our rows, at our height. They are empty, and they face the seat. Ours face the stair.

I served nineteen years at Long Stair before I was given the plate. In those years I was never told why the halls are cut as they are. I enter only that I knew the way in a place I had never been.

**Fifth article. Of the lintel.** Over the mouth of the Long Hall is a lintel of one bone, longer than nine men lying down. It is cut with notches end to end, long and short, some crossed. I cannot read them. The tallier would not look at them. The Seeker read along them with his finger and stopped at a place two thirds along and took his finger away and did not go on, and he said that was its name. I asked him for it. He said he had read it and it was gone, as a breath is gone.

**Sixth article. Of what sits.** In the seat at the head of the hall sits a frame. It is greater than the hall should hold, and the hall holds it. It sits as a Warden sits at Feast, with the knees up and the head bowed toward the floor. Its left hand lies open on its knee. Its right arm goes down from the elbow into one long bone that curves, flat and thin at the edge, the length of the hall's width, and the point of it rests in the floor of bone. The curve of that bone is grown. There is no joint in it and no binding. It is the arm.

I enter this as I saw it: the joints of the frame are anointed. The chrism stands bright in the sockets of the knees and the shoulder, and it is wet, and it is ours. I have smelled the chrism of the Fifth Well every day of my life since my naming. I do not know who anoints it. We met no one below.

It did not move while we were in the hall. I did not see it breathe. It has no breath to see.

**Seventh article. Of my standing dead.** At the mouth of the hall, under the lintel, my nine stopped. I did not give the halt. Then they knelt, all nine, together, as they never do together for any word of mine, and set their brows to the floor, toward the seat. I gave the word to rise. They did not rise. I gave the Count over them, the number that stands them, and they did not rise.

My own knee went down. I enter it. The plate is heavy and the floor is uneven, and I enter it.

They rose when the Seeker came back to the lintel, after a time I did not count. Eight rose. The ninth, the one that stood on my right hand since I took the plate, Brother Two Hundred and Six, stayed with its brow to the bone. I could not lift it. I left it. Its niche on the Third Wall is empty, and I ask that it be kept empty and not filled from the common bone.

**Eighth article. Of the star.** The Seeker went the length of the hall alone, between the knees of the thing that sits, and climbed the left hand where it lay open. He found the star in the palm. He brought it down to me in his own hands, and I have looked at it. It is one bone, the size of a finger joint, white, and hotter than marrow fresh from the Well. It has no mark on it. It weighs nothing on the scale.

He told me the palm was worn into hollows, each the size of this bone, smooth as a step, many hundreds of them, and all of them empty but the one he took it from.

**Ninth article. Of the conclusion.** The Rule of the Kostyak is that a knight reports what was counted, and what was seen, and what was done, and leaves the rest to the Wardens in Agreement. I have done so. I have been asked on the road home, by the Seeker and by myself, what the eighth article means beside the fourth. I report that I was asked. I do not answer it here.

Given under my plate, and the names on it, at Long Stair.

---

*Entered. Not to be read at Feast. The bone of the eighth article is given to the Fifth Well. The knight's plate to be cut with one more name, his own, below the others. The niche on the Third Wall to be filled. — the Warden's hand*

*The niche was not filled. The common bone was carried to it four times and was found each morning on the floor of the Long Hall, laid out in a line toward the chair. — another hand*
