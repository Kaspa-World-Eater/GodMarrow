# The Record of the Fortieth Hood, Tried at Sallow Lip

*Copied from the chapter roll at Sallow Lip, where the keeper of the chained Rule let me sit with it one night by his lamp. The last leaf is sewn into the roll with gut thread, and he would not cut the thread for me, so I read it bent.*

Set down by the clerk of the chapter at Sallow Lip, on the ninth day after the Measure, in the crypt below the image, before the Elder, the bloodletter and eleven hoods. Nothing in this record is mine except the hand.

**The charges**

That the fortieth hood of this chapter, a man of the salt road given to the Maiden in his twelfth year, who has walked with us twenty-two Measures, refused to open his robe at the cup.

That being opened by force he was found sewn under the breastbone.

That he said the Words on the crypt stair in the dark in an order that is no order, and was heard by the twenty-ninth hood.

That he has taken into himself what is given to her, which is the oldest theft.

**The examination of the body**

Done by the bloodletter by lamplight, before all. I wrote as he spoke.

Under the breastbone, a wound the length of a thumb, lying crosswise, sewn with gut thread in nine stitches. The thread is new. The wound under it is old. Its lips are smooth and pale as the inside of a cheek, and have been opened many times, and have not scarred once.

At the left flank, a second, the same. At the right flank, a third, the same. The bloodletter counted them aloud three times and I have written three.

On the backs of both hands, the marks of the scourge he wore in the spring procession. There are none. I walked behind him in that procession. I saw those marks bleed.

The bloodletter opened the inside of the left arm with his own blade, a finger's length, to take the measure as he would of any hood. There was no blood. The cut stood open and dry, dark inside as meat in a bowl. Within a count of forty it had closed. The bloodletter wiped his blade and the cloth was clean. He asked to go up the stair and the Elder let him go. He did not come down again that night.

The body was warm to the hand. The crypt is cold. At the Elder's bidding the eleven hoods each set a hand to him, and each said warm.

**The answers of the accused**

Asked whether he denied the wounds: he did not.

Asked who sewed them: he sews them himself, after. He said a sewn mouth is a shut mouth, and she is quiet behind it.

Asked what he had taken into himself, he answered as follows, and I set it down whole, because the Elder said a whole heresy burns better than half of one:

"You pour into her and call it a gift. Have you never asked why the wound stays open? A wound is held open for something to come out. She is coming out. She has been coming out since the first day, and every year you raise the line on the cup and pour her back in, and call that the Measure. She wants to be carried, and you hear *given* because cups are what you have. She has no hands down there. She has no eyes. You keep her in the dark with your praying and she grows with nothing to grow into. I gave her somewhere to go."

Asked whether he called the faithful's gift a starving: yes. He said the Open Vein stand nearer than the Measured and are wrong all the same, because they only pour faster.

Asked where his fellows meet: "Under you." The Elder ruled this no answer. I record it.

Asked who opened him: "The one in the chair." Asked what chair, and where: she has not left it. She opened him with her own hand and said the first word into the first mouth, and she is older than this chapter's Rule. Asked her name: she has outlived it. Asked whether she was a woman: he was quiet a long while, and then said that depended on the night.

I know of no chair. The Elder bade me strike this answer as raving. I have not struck it. A clerk does not choose.

Asked to say the Words forward, as a hood of this chapter, he said the first clause and the second rightly. At the third he stopped, and said one word, and every lamp in the crypt leaned toward him.

I cannot write that word. I have tried it on the waste leaf five times. Each time what my hand sets down is the first of our Words, forward, rightly spelled. I have scraped those leaves. The word he said was that word turned round, and in his mouth it sounded like swallowing. Here it is, as near as I can bring it:

[ … ]

The twenty-ninth hood was sick on the crypt floor. The accused laid his hand flat over his breastbone, as a man keeps something in, and looked at the Elder, and the Elder did not look at him again.

**The witnesses**

The twenty-ninth hood, who heard him on the stair: that he said the Words softly in the dark and they went the wrong way, and the steps were warm under her bare feet where he had stood.

The cup-beater: that four years since, the fortieth hood began to give his measure short, a thumbnail under the line and never more, and the cup-beater took it for weakness and was ashamed for him.

The keeper of the chained Rule: that at the Raising of the new image the fortieth hood asked him why the carvers never finish her face, and offered to sit for it.

The sister of the accused, a hood of the Open Vein: did not come. She has not been seen since the spring procession.

**The sentence**

Burning, on the lip of the wound below the chapter, at first light, with the Words said forward over him by every hood, loudly, so that the Mother would know whom she was not given.

The Elder asked whether he would be unsewn first. He said there was nothing left in the mouths to let out. He said he was full.

**After**

Burned at first light. The fire was kept a day and a night on the chapter's own wood and the oil of four lamps.

When the ash was raked the bones were there. They had not burned clean. They lay whole and dark, with no crack in any, and when the keeper lifted the breastbone with the tongs he said it was heavier than his own arm, and set it down quickly. Under it in the ash lay three lengths of gut thread, unburned, each still knotted.

The bones are in a lidded jar in the crypt, under the image, the lid tied down. I have been asked to note whether the man was still in them when we lit the fire. I do not know what to write. He answered to his place in the procession until the end. When I asked his name for the roll he gave one I have never heard, and I have left the space empty, because the roll keeps that space for the name of the one being burned, and I could not swear it was his.

The bloodletter asks that he not be made to open anyone at the next Measure.
