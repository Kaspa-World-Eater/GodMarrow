# Claim of Salvage from the Sunk Streets of Thessar, Entered at Low Gerrow

*Pinned in the claim-book of the factor at the stilt-quay of Low Gerrow, the leaf stained brown to the half where the diver held it wet. The factor let me copy it and would not let me take it, and by the last item I understood him.*

Claim of Ebbe Torrance, diver, of the stilt-quay at Low Gerrow, entered with the factor of the delta for weighing and the factor's fifth, in the week of the low red, the eleven hundred and twelfth year as the factor counts. Each item by street, as brought up, with what it cost in rope, oil, breath and hands. The factor's notes under each, in the factor's hand.

**The rule, as I have it.** I set it first so the next diver has it. The drowned of Thessar keep their doors and stand at their counters as they stood when the city went down, and they let you take some things and keep others. In nine years under the red I have found it to be this. Anything with a lip that pours, they give: cup, ewer, ladle, spouted lamp, a bowl with a notch in the rim. Anything that shuts and holds, they keep: a lidded box, a stoppered jar, a lock, a ring, a purse, a shut door, a closed hand. Put your fingers on a held thing and the one behind the counter lays its hand over yours. The hand is soft. It leaves the slick on you, and the slick does not wash. You let go.

*Factor:* Entered as the diver gives it. The factor buys what comes up.

**Item, Chandlers' Turn.** Brought up: four ewers of red clay, spouted, whole; one ladle of a white metal, long in the handle. The chandler at her counter stood aside for them. Cost: one rope, cut through on a lintel; half a lantern of fat.

*Factor:* Ewers weighed and sold to the cup-beaters of the stilt-chapel, as they ask for all spouted ware. The ladle does not take the red stain. Held for assay.

**Item, Weighers' Row.** The scales stand on every counter with the pans even, and every pan holds a weight, small and dark, and the drowned keep the weights. Brought up: one pouring-cup of bronze off a weigher's shelf, the lip worn thin on one side as if by a thumb. Cost: Hesk, my rope-second. He put his hand to a weight in a pan. The weigher laid its hand on his and he did not take his away. The rope came up without him, untied, the knot undone as a careful hand undoes it. On my next going down Hesk stood in the weigher's door with the slick thick at his mouth, and he stood aside for me, and I came away.

*Factor:* Cup weighed, eleven grains under its look. Hesk's share owed to his widow: nil, since nothing of his was brought up. Entered so. The widow disputes it.

**Item, the Long Stair Street, which goes down.** Brought up: one mason's basin with a pouring lip, cut stone, two men's weight. Cost: the most breath I have given in one going, and the lantern. At the stair's foot the street goes on downward and the paving stops against a pale wall, ridged across like the roof of a mouth, curving away above and below farther than the lamp reached, and wet all over. Along its foot runs a lip, longer than the street is wide. While I hung there it opened the width of a finger and the water went into it, and it drew me one step down, and where that water had passed the red was thinned to pink. I have seen it the once. The old people on the stilts say Thessar sank itself to be nearer something, and they will not say what, and I do not know. I claim nothing past that stair. The slick from the wall got on my lantern, and the flame in it leaned down toward the stair all the way up the rope, and leans so still, in the shut lantern, on the factor's shelf.

*Factor:* Basin sold to the stilt-chapel for a pouring-stone. The diver asks that his lantern be weighed. It weighs more than it did when he bought it of me, by the heft of a full cup. The flame leans toward the water whichever way the lantern is turned. Kept on the shelf, at his asking, with a cloth over it. Some on the quay call the wall the [ … ]. The factor does not.

**Item, the street with no name, of shut doors.** Every door on it shut, and one of the drowned behind each, and a shut door is a held thing. Brought up: one spouted lamp of green glass, off a step. Cost: a day's oil, and the skin of my left hand, which a door-keeper touched through the gap, and which is slick still, and warm, and bends easier than the right.

**What the drowned do.** They are soft and whole. Their eyes are open. They do not swell as the drowned of the river swell. The slick lies on them thick as fat at the mouth and the nose and keeps them as they were, and it is the same slick that is on my hand. On the low red, twice in a year, every one of them at once lifts whatever empty cup stands on its counter and tips it toward the down-stair, slow, and stops, and tips again, and sets it down. There is nothing in the cups. They pour anyway, all down every street, toward the stair.

**What the faithful do.** On the Day of the Measure the hoods of the Bleeding Maiden come down to the stilt-chapel and pour their cups into the delta to the line, with the Words over it, so that the Mother knows whom it is for. I have watched them from the quay. They lift, and tip, and stop, and tip, and set down. It is the same pour. They say the blood goes to the Mother. It goes into the water I work in, and the water goes down the Long Stair, and the drowned stand at their counters and pour toward the same place. I think the hoods learned their rite from the drowned long ago and forgot where they had it. They are fools, and spend good blood their grandmothers would have kept in them.

*Factor:* The factor sells the chapel its cups and notes the following, for the book only. Of the cups the diver brings up off the drowned counters, near a third bear the chapel's cut line. The chapel raises its line a thumbnail each year. Every chapel cup the diver has brought up is cut higher than this year's line. The highest is three fingers from the lip. The chapel has not yet cut a cup so high. The factor keeps them in a crate under the counter and has not shown the cup-beater.

**Item, the last. Not taken.** A weight, small and dark, of the kind that sits in the pans on Weighers' Row. I did not put my hand to it. I came up from the street of shut doors with my mouth shut against the water, and on the quay I spat, and it was in my mouth, under the tongue, warm. It does not come out. I have tried, and the factor's boy has tried. I claim it so that it is written that I did not take it, and that the drowned did not give it, since they give nothing that holds.

*Factor:* Declined to weigh. The diver will not open his mouth over the pan. The factor notes that Torrance has stood at the claim-counter the four days since he filed, and has not asked for his fifth, and does not go home. He lets the boy take cups and ewers from the claim-shelf without a word, and when the boy put a hand to the lidded box where the fifths are kept, Torrance laid his slick hand over the boy's, gently, and the boy let go.
