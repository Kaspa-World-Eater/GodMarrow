# The Annal of What Is Claimed, Kept at the Femur

*Copied from the roll on the lectern in the fourth window of the Citadel of the Shattered Femur, where it lay weighted open with a knuckle of marrow-stone; the reliquarist who let me copy it asked me to stop at the weight, and I did.*

Kept by order of the Lectors. Every entry is a claim. Under each I set where it came up from and how far it will bear leaning on. Where two faiths say different things I write both and choose neither, which is the office. Years are the Pale Order's count, corrected by this Citadel where the corrections hold and argued over where they do not. Before the count there are no years, and I give none.

**First claim. That there was a time before the corpse lay down.**
The Pale Order: a gap. Their tallier struck the line through while I watched and said the Rule forbids the rest. The bowl-monks of the fallen Peak: the Silence, before a mouth opened in it. A grandmother of the Barrow road: before, you could not even say Hush. *Judgement.* Three refusals of different shapes. None is a source. Entered so that the roll has a first line.

*Here the roll is left blank for the length of a forearm. The blank is the Citadel's and is deliberate. It stands for the beginning of the time when the gods walked, of which nothing is known.*

**Second claim. That the gods walked, and were loud.**
Source: a tablet from the ruin at the Fen-road fork, read in part: *the name was said in every [ ] until the stone of the [ ] was warm.* *Judgement.* The old script uses one sign for god and for lord. A tablet that says gods walked may mean men who were feared.

**Third claim. The dragons, called the Ythraal, the First Terrors.**
Theirs is the commonest of the many signs on the tablets, a coil around an open eye. The ribs that stand in the white valley north of the Barrens are said to be one of them; the Pale Order has counted those ribs and reduced the number to its root and will not publish the root. *Judgement.* That they were: as near to sure as this roll comes, which is not near. That they were the greatest: the tablets were cut by people who feared them, and the feared look greatest from underneath. Perhaps.

**Fourth claim. That one dragon would not lie down.**
Morrh-Ythraal, the Starved Crown. It is said to have eaten its worshippers when they thinned, and then its own flesh, and to sit in a citadel it broke, wearing an iron crown forged while it still had subjects to stand at the forge. Sources: a pilgrim's account bought at Four Wents; a tablet with the crown-sign cut nine times in a row and every one struck through. Side by side: the Pale Order holds that no dragon stands, since every great frame on the Hide is entered and laid. The Maiden's processions say it is flesh the Mother has not finished with. The lowlands say it waits for someone to be afraid of it. *Judgement.* Little. I was asked by a Lector not to enter it. If the Citadel's teaching is right and a power lives on what is said of it, this entry feeds it. I have written it small.

**Fifth claim. That a colossus fed on cities, and one city went up the ramp.**
The Gorrhaun, the Famines: god-corpses the height of hills that went on feeding after they died. The city was Dhum. The people are claimed to have gone up in order, the dead first, singing. Sources: the ramp, which stands, and whose foot I have walked; the paving is worn hollow in the middle by feet going one way. A Pale Order count of Dhum's dead, entered under *not received*, with no number in the column. A lowland tale that Dhum is still lit inside what took it. A carter's report of ground on the eastern waste that kept a pulse under his wheels. *Judgement.* That a city went up: the stones say so, and stones are the best witnesses we have, which is a hard thing to write. That it is lit: a tale. That one still walks: one carter.

**Sixth claim. That towers drank.**
Vhul-Oghra, the Tower That Drinks, and the city beneath it. In the fifty-first year of the corrected count this Citadel sent a reliquarist to read the city's lintels. His notes say every window faces the tower and every bed is made. He came back and slept well for the rest of his life and never once dreamed. *Judgement.* The notes are in his hand. The hand is steady. I trust the notes. Of the man I can say nothing, and I do not know why.

**Seventh claim. That citadels stood up out of their own walls.**
The Khar-Dhuum, the Walking Walls; Vesk the chief of them. Sources: a tablet showing a wall with feet. A Pale Order count from the far border, where iron frames are entered as *standing, unlaid*, the only such entry I have found. *Judgement.* I record that at each year's reading the Lectors are asked whether the Femur itself stood up out of anything. The question is entered. It has never been answered.

**Eighth claim. That lords ruled by dread.**
Kings, abbots, matrons, generals, who were feared so long that the fear went on without them, and some sat down as liches without meaning to. Sources: thrones in emptied cities, each with dust on the seat in the shape of someone sitting. *Judgement.* This one I believe, which is why I trust it least.

*Gap. Between the lords and the starving the roll holds nothing. The Order's count shows years here. The Citadel's correction strikes three hundred of them; a Lector of the last generation restored two hundred and nine; the next struck them again. The argument stands. Whatever was done in those years was done with nobody keeping count.*

**Ninth claim. That the gods starved one by one, and broke.**
Set side by side, as the office requires. The Pale Order: nothing went missing; what is missing is still to be counted. The bowl-monks: the held breath was let go. The Veiled Crone's circles, the covered ones: the threads went slack in the weave. Their uncovered sisters: there was never a weave to go slack. The Myriad: the breathing out began. The Bleeding Maiden's processions: they do not remember, and the cup came down to them from somewhere. This Citadel: the powers starved as their peoples died or stopped believing or were eaten, and a power that starves to the end breaks into demons, the way a carcass breaks along its seams. *Judgement.* Each faith calls the others ignorant. I hold the Citadel's reading. I note that I hold it because I was taught it at this window, which is no source at all.

**Tenth claim. The Tablet of the Ramp.**
Lector Hirse Ammau read the Tablet of the Ramp whole over three nights in the four hundred and twelfth year. Her gloss is kept in the press below this window. It runs eleven leaves in a good hand and stops on the twelfth: *and the thing they carried up the ramp, in order, singing, was older than the city, and its name was* . The rest of the leaf is clean. She sat at the fourth window afterward for nineteen years and did not read again, and when asked would hold up one finger, as one does to ask for quiet. *Judgement.* A Lector's word is the best source this roll has. It is not finished.

**Eleventh claim. That the last breath began to leave, and the count began.**
The Order's year one. *Judgement.* The Order's year one is the only year anyone agrees on, and the Order will not say what was counted first.

*Here the roll is blank again, for the length of the room. It stands for the ages of which no tablet, ruin, count or tale has come up. It is the longest of the gaps. I have measured.*

**Twelfth claim, entered this year.** That the reliquarists of the Femur do not finish.
Source: the roll in the fourth window, the gloss in the press, the chair. *Judgement.*
