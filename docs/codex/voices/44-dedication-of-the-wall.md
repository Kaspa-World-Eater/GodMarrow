# The Dedication of the Wall of [ … ], with the Lector's Gloss

*Copied from a rubbing in the eleventh window of the Citadel of the Shattered Femur, five sheets pasted edge to edge, with the Lector's gloss written down the margin in brown ink; the rubbing is black where the cuts are, and in one place the paper has been rubbed through.*

> *Lector's note, at the head:* The stone is the foundation stone of a wall in the country past the Barrow road, where no road now goes on. It is the lowest course but one, set at the inner foot of the wall, and it faces in, toward where the city stood. I read it lying on my side in the trench. Where the face is broken I write [ … ]. Where a word was never cut, and a space the length of a word was left smooth, I write ____. The masons left spaces. I do not know what was meant to go in them, or whether the space is the word. — Hesk, Lector.

**This is the wall of [ … ], and these are its words.** Let them be read by the inside. The outside has no need of them.

**Of the leave.** By leave of Ghesh-Amaar, who walks the hearth-street at the lamp-hour and is fed at every door; by leave of the Lord of the Ford, who was paid his ninth; under the eye of the Tower on the hill, which drinks from us at night and gave no answer, and is entered as having given none. The gods of the ground have consented. The wall is raised against what they cannot keep off, which is the ____ .

> *Gloss:* Three powers named, and all three walked. The Tower may be the one that drinks, or another; there were many towers. The city did not trust its own gods with the sky. It asked their leave and built anyway.

**Of why.** Before the wall the city stood open under the ____, and the ____ came down out of it when it was hungry, and it was always hungry. It came down low and went over, and turned, and went over again, and the fields stood after and the grain stood, and what it had eaten was the looking up. A people under an open sky looks up all its life. The ____ grew great on it. Our grandmothers' grandmothers were thin with looking up and it was fat.

Therefore we close the sky. A wall is a roof that starts at the ground.

> *Gloss:* I read the word that is not cut as dragon, since the Citadel's tablets say the Ythraal, the First Terrors, lived on the fear of a world with no walls round it. I must enter that the stone does not say so. Nothing in this country says so. Its lintels name the hearth-god, the ford, the tower, and a famine in the east. No tablet from here names a dragon, or any power of the sky, or the sky. The only record of the ____ in all this country is a space left smooth on the stone raised against it.

**Of the height.** The masons asked how high. The answer was given by the [ … ] in the twelfth year of the building. On the night of the ____ the youngest child of the city was set on this stone, where I am cut, and the city was made silent, and the child was made to look up. The masons marked where her cry went to before it was lost in the open. Forty-one courses. The wall is forty-one courses and one more, so that her cry will always be inside.

No one is to stand on the top course. The top course is the sky's. Let it have that.

> *Gloss:* Forty-two courses stand. I counted from the trench on the outer side and the inner side and had forty-two both times. The Citadel's last reading, two Lectors before me, entered forty-one. I have entered my count beside his and do not correct him.

**Of the footing.** In the footing are laid these things, and they are the wall's, and none of them is to be dug for.

First, the first fear of every hearth in [ … ]: the fear the hearth was made with, spoken down into the trench by the eldest of the hearth, on the knees, before the stone came down on it. The masons are to hear none of it. They stood with their backs turned.

Second, the names. Every name in the city is cut on the under face of the footing stones, in the order of the streets, and the faces are laid downward, so that the sky cannot read them and call them out by name. A name that cannot be read from above cannot be called up.

Third, ____, who went down willing into the footing under the gate-stone, standing, with her hands up flat, and the stone was let down onto her hands. So the wall is ________ from beneath by someone, and the wall knows it.

> *Gloss:* The double space under the third is longer than any other on the stone. I have tried every word the old script has of the right length. Held up fits. Held under fits. So does fed.

> *Gloss, below:* I did not dig. I lay with my ear to the gate-stone for the length of one lamp, because I had read the third clause and could not leave it alone. I heard nothing. I enter that the stone was warm on its under edge, where my hand was, and cold above.

**Of the one law of the wall.** The wall must never be forgotten.

A wall that is forgotten is open. It stands, and the stones lie on the stones, and the ____ goes over it as it went over before, because a wall is only as high as it is remembered. When no one inside looks at the wall and thanks it, and no one inside is afraid of the sky, the wall is a heap of cut stone in a field, and the sky comes back down to the doorsteps, and the looking up begins again.

Therefore: at every lamp-hour, the gate-ward is to walk the inner foot of the wall from the gate to the gate, with a lamp, and set the lamp in each niche in turn, and say the wall's name to each stone. Every child of [ … ] is to be brought to this stone in its seventh year and made to look up, once, and then be shown the wall, so that it knows the difference. No one is to say that the sky is empty. No one is to say the wall is old.

If the city goes, the wall is to be remembered by whoever is left. If no one is left, ____

> *Gloss:* The line runs to the edge of the stone and the next stone is set over it.

> *Gloss, the last:* I enter what is there. The city is gone. Inside the ring there is a field of grey stone-dust with the lines of streets in it, a hand deep, and no hearth anywhere that I could find. No one of that country remembers that a city stood there. The wall stands whole all round, forty-two courses, and its gate is shut, and I came over it on a ladder and went out the same way.

> The niches on the inner face are blacked with lamp-soot. I wiped one with my sleeve on the first evening. On the second evening it was black again, and the soot was warm, and I had kept a lamp in my tent both nights and had not gone out.
