# The Ordinances of the Ascent, as Read at the Femur

*Copied from the reading-leaf that lies under the tablet in the ninth window of the Citadel of the Shattered Femur, with both Lectors' glosses as they stand; the tablet is the colour of an old tooth, and warm on the face that is turned to the wall.*

> *Reading-note, in the first hand:* Grey stone, broken at the lower left. It came in letters-down among facing-stone carted up the Four Wents road, and the mason who turned it over asked to be paid twice. Where the stone is broken I write [ … ]. Where a word has been cut out with a chisel, on purpose, to the depth of a thumbnail, I write ________. It is always the same word. — Osk.

> *Under it, the second hand:* It is always the same length of word. — Ilve.

**The law of ________ for the going up.** Cut in the year the Famine turned its face to us across the plain, which is the first year of our honour and the last year of our walls. Let every stone of ________ hear it. Let it be read at every gate at the lamp-hour until the day.

**First. Of the choosing.** Nine cities stood on the plain when the Famine walked there, and it has passed eight. It passed them with its face turned away. Their walls stand and their dead lie down and nothing is asked of them, and they are [ … ]. We are chosen. Let no mouth in ________ say otherwise in the streets.

> *Osk:* "With its face turned away" is the phrase the old script uses of a lord who refuses a gift. They watched it pass eight cities and wanted it. That is devotion.

> *Ilve:* It is also the phrase for a judge who has not yet looked at you. And a law that forbids a thing in the streets is cut against what the streets are saying.

**Second. Of the dead.** The dead go first. Every tomb of ________ is to be opened, from the oldest to the newest, and the dead lifted out on their boards and carried to the foot of the ramp in the order they were laid, the oldest at the head. So the Famine will know us first by what we have already given, and know that we give it everything. The dead of this year go last of the dead and nearest the living, because they will know the faces.

Where a tomb is found empty, the [ … ] of that tomb is to go up in its place, and carry the board.

> *Osk:* The dead were the city's oldest gift, and it gave them first.

> *Ilve:* Where a tomb is found empty. They had opened enough tombs, by the time this was cut, to need the clause.

**Third. Of the streets.** After the dead, the living, by streets, in the order the streets were laid: the street of the [ … ] first, being oldest; then the street of the bowl-makers, the street of the weavers, the [ … ], the [ … ], the street of the wells. Each street goes up whole, its eldest at its head, its infants carried by those who bore them. No street goes up in pieces. No one walks with another street. The gate-street goes last, and the last of the gate-street is to shut the gate of ________ behind the city and bar it, and carry the bar up, so that the city goes whole and nothing is left open behind it.

> *Osk:* To be laid again inside it as they stood. See the sixth.

> *Ilve:* Who was the bar to keep out of an empty city? Osk answers me with the sixth.

**Fourth. Of what is said on the ramp.** On the ramp one word is said and no other: the name of ________, at every step, by every mouth, the mouths of the dead said for them by those who carry them. So that it knows what it is eating, and the name goes in with us and is kept. A mouth that says another word on the ramp [ … ] down off the ramp by the [ … ] street, and [ … ] be said again.

No one is to weep on the ramp. Weeping is a word.

> *Osk:* The name was the gift. They gave it entire, and cut it out of every stone, so it would be kept in one place only.

> *Ilve:* The chisel marks are sharp. The letters round them are worn soft as a step. The name came out long after the law went in, and a city on a ramp carries no chisels. Those who stayed took it out, so the Famine could not call them by it. Or no one went up at all. A law is cut for what has not yet been done. Nothing on this stone says it was done.

**Fifth. Of what is carried.** Each carries the lamp of their hearth, lit, and what they would be known by, and nothing that can be set down. No water: there is no thirst on the ramp. No iron but the bar of the gate. Every lamp of ________ is to be lit from the lamp of the gate on the last evening and carried up burning, so that what goes into it goes in lit.

The keepers of the tombs carry the doors of the tombs.

> *Osk:* The lamps. Dhum, the Swallowed, is said to be still lit inside what did not finish with it. This is Dhum's law.

> *Ilve:* We have Dhum from a pilgrim and the foot of a ramp. We have this from a cart of facing-stone. Neither of us knows where this city stood, and the one word that would say it has been taken out eleven times.

**Sixth. Of the honour.** The cities that are passed by end. Their walls empty in time, their dead get up with no one to keep them, and no one says their names. ________ does not end. Its streets will be laid again inside the Famine as they lie here, the oldest street first, and its lamps burn there, and its dead lie in their order, and its name be said in it at every step for as long as it walks. Where it treads, the ground will know ________ [ … ].

This is the honour. There is no [ … ] honour on the plain.

> *Osk:* Where it treads, the ground keeps a pulse: so the far carters say of the one that still walks. Read the sixth again, Ilve, and tell me they were afraid.

> *Ilve:* I have read it. It is what a people says when it has been told so at every gate, every night, until the day. Whether the one that walks is theirs, Osk does not know and I do not know, and neither of us will write it.

**Seventh. Of those who turn.** [ … ] who turns from the ramp [ … ] not of ________. His name is to be [ … ] from every [ … ], and his tomb [ … ], and no lamp. He shall stay on the plain with the eight cities and be passed by [ … ], and the Famine shall not [ … ] his face.

> *Osk:* The worst the city knew: to be left out of the honour.

> *Ilve:* His name is to be [ … ] from every [ … ]. Cut out, Osk. The same done to the one who turns and to the city's own name. Who, of the two, was left on the plain holding the chisel?
