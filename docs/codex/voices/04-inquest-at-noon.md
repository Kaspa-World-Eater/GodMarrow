# Inquest upon Odo Venn, Carter, Found Dead at the Well at the Noon Bell

*Sewn into the coroner's roll of the lowland circuit among the ordinary deaths of that year; the last leaf is in two hands. A flake of gold is folded into the margin at the place where it was entered, and the parchment round it is still warm, so I have left it where it lies.*

Inquest taken at Harrow Market in the Wool Hall, sitting the third to the fifth days after the death, before me, Aunsel Pyke, coroner for the lowland circuit by commission of the market towns, holding to no altar, upon view of the body of Odo Venn, carter, of this town, found dead at the well in the square at the noon bell. Jury of twelve, of the town, sworn on the town's iron measure, there being no god all twelve would swear by.

By my order the court rises before noon each day and sits again when the bell has stopped. The shutters of the Wool Hall are barred for that hour. I give no reason in the record.

**The view.** The body laid in the cold-room under the hall. A man of about fifty years, with a carter's hands. No wound. No mark of cord, blade or blow. The eyes open, the whites clear, and the dark of each eye gone the colour of pale straw. The body warm at the view, which was the third day.

**Deposition the first.** Hew Tallow, keeper of the well-wheel and trimmer of the square's lanterns, sworn. He was at the wheel at noon. There was someone standing between the well and the market cross, gold, very bright, as a pan is bright when it catches the sun; he notes that there was no sun, the sky being low and grey that day. He looked away because it hurt. He cannot say whether it was a man or a woman. Asked its height, he says the height of a person. Odo Venn stood an arm's length from it, facing it, and had been looking at it a long while. Then Venn sat down on the well-step and did not get up. The deponent did not see the person go. He remembers it standing. His mark.

**Deposition the second.** Mag Venn, widow of the deceased, sworn. Her husband spoke at supper nine days running of someone standing in the square at noon. The first day, a stranger by the cross in a yellow coat. The third, the coat was gold. The sixth, he could not see the coat for the shine, and it stood nearer the well. The eighth, he had stood a whole bell's length looking to make out who it was, because he knew every face in Harrow and would not be beaten by one. On the ninth morning he said he would have it that day. She deposes, unasked, that she has sat by him in the cold-room and tried to call his face to mind, and she can, and that she is afraid to try it at noon. Her mark.

**Item entered, the first.** A flake of gold leaf the breadth of a thumbnail, beaten so thin it stirs at the breath of whoever holds it near, taken by the barber-surgeon from the tongue of the deceased. It is warm. It was warm at the view. It is warm now, the fifth day, having lain in my coat. I have folded it into the roll at this place so that it stands entered. I have not looked at it closely in daylight since the second day.

**Deposition the third.** Wyn Askew, barber-surgeon, sworn. The leaf lay flat on the tongue, as if set there; the throat was clean. The eyes are as a man's would be who had looked at the sun from dawn to dusk and kept looking. The ground of Harrow is warm in every season, and the body is warmer than the ground, which he has never known in the dead. Cause of death unknown to his craft. He does not think the man suffered. The face, he says, was attentive. His hand.

**Depositions taken short, being of one matter.** Joss Crane, chandler: it was taller than the cross. Elsabet Rook, spinner: it was a child's height and stood in the well-trough. Piers Hallam, tallier of market dues: it held out a bowl at the height of its chest. Ana of Lowditch, seller of lamp-oil: its hands were open and empty and turned up. Thom Vey, sweeper of the square: there was the light, and Odo talking into it, and he saw nobody in the light. These, and nine others not entered, agree on four things only: noon; gold; very bright; standing. None can say who. None can say when it went.

**The bailiff's count.** The bailiff of the market counts heads in the square at noon from the hall steps, for the dues. On the day he counted forty-one, and cut forty-one notches on his stick. The jury and I have since named everyone present, by asking each witness who stood near them: forty names. Every witness, asked who stood between the well and the cross, says: someone. The bailiff has counted his stick three times before the court. Forty-one. He cannot name the last notch and will not cut it out.

**Deposition the fourth.** Hild Oake, of the almshouse, past eighty, sworn. She was not in the square, and came of her own will. She deposes that there were monks on the mountain, before the mountain went to glass, who laid gold on what they honoured until no one could see it; that her grandmother said some of them laid it on themselves, living, a leaf a day; and that when they were finished they walked down into the towns and no one saw them come. She says it does no harm to be looked at, and great harm to look. She says to put the leaf in the ground; then she says no, keep it, it is somebody's. She will not say whose. Her mark.

The court does not know what mountain she means. Eight of the jury say the Gilded Peak, which the Pale Order is said to have broken, and whose monks still walk the roads begging names into bowls. Four say there was never such a mountain. I enter both and take no side, having no altar to take it from.

**Findings of the coroner, the jury concurring.**

That Odo Venn, carter, of Harrow Market, died at the well in the square at the noon bell.

That he died of no wound, of no poison known to the barber-surgeon, and of no sickness.

That one person stood near him at that hour, gold and very bright, seen by every witness.

That no witness can name or describe this person; that they disagree on its height, its hands and its place, and agree that it stood.

That the deceased looked at this person on nine days, and longer than anyone.

That the leaf entered was on his tongue.

That we cannot find this person killed him. That we cannot find anything else did.
