# The Beat-Log of Cleave Stilts

*Taken from the beat-house of a stilt-town in Shog-Mire where every ladder had been drawn up. The book lay open beside a long candle burned down into its dish, and the bowl of counting-stones next to it was full to the lip.*

Copied at the head of every keeper's leaf, as the first keeper set it down:

*Hand on the pole from the lighting of the long candle to its drowning. One stone into the bowl at each clutch. Ring once for every ten the count has moved since the last ringing. When the first stilt was set the mud took hold at the third breath, and the count was 240.*

Under it, in my grandmother's hand, the last line of her office: *Measure 362. Count 318. Rang.*

**Measure 391, the first night of my office.** Count to the long candle: 371. Rang five times, for the tens my father left unrung. My own breaths to the same candle, sat still, for the check: 744.

The leech-wives taught my father to count his breaths with the stones so he would know when the tallow was poor. A poor candle gives a short night and a low count, and a keeper who rings on a poor candle frightens the town for nothing. My breaths say the candle burns true.

**Measure 391, the night of the Measure.** Count 371. The procession went down the ladders to the red water with the cups and gave in measure, with the Words said over each cup. I am the forty-first hood. After, the singing came up under the floors, as it has on every Measure-night since my grandmother was a girl. It has the sound of the Words with their shapes wrong, as a child says them. The Elder says the Mother is learning her name from us, and is glad. The leech-wives stopped their ears with wax.

**Measure 394, the salt-boats.** The tally from Pell's Wade, down the delta, nearer the Steps, sent up with the salt as every year. Their count: 412. Their keeper's breaths: 709. They have always run ahead of us.

**Measure 397.** Count 389. Breaths 731. Rang.

**Measure 398.** Two knights of the Pale Order came over the duckboards with their standing dead behind them and counted the town, the living and the laid, and wrote the beat in their book. They said it is the Bearing Mother turning in her sleep, and that a count that rises is her gift. They have never stood in the mud with their boots off. They took their number away with them, and the stilts went on shaking.

**Measure 401.** Count 433. Breaths 702. Rang.

From the bell-platform at grey light, in the long glass: the Steps in the Wet, which the one-minded built. Their bodies stand on every tread where the mind went out of them, more than I can count, and at each clutch they turn together the width of a finger and back, as a sleeper turns. My father's leaf has them facing the sea. I enter them facing the delta.

**Measure 403, the salt-boats.** Pell's Wade. Count 611. Breaths 622.

**Measure 404, the salt-boats.** No tally from Pell's Wade. The boatman says their ladders were drawn up, and their bell rang the whole while he was tied at the rails, once to every clutch, with no hand on the rope. He did not go up. He says he heard sleepers, a great many, breathing as one man breathes. I enter what is said.

**Measure 405.** Count 468. Breaths 688. Rang. I ring most moons now. Nobody comes out on the rails to ask.

**Measure 407, the night of the Measure.** Count 486. The singing under the floor had the first clause of the Words whole, every syllable in its place, and held the pause after it as long as the Elder holds it. The Elder wept and said she is near. That night the leech-wives took their wax and their jars and poled upriver without a lamp. I set it down plainly: the clause was said better than our Elder says it.

**Measure 409.** Count 517. Breaths 671. Rang.

A stilt stands in the mud past the last house, toward the Steps, with nothing on it. No one of this town set it. Its wood is ash, and there is no ash-wood in the mire. It has a notch cut in it at the height of my hand on the pole.

**Measure 411.** Count 549. Breaths 655. Rang.

My breaths are poor for the check now; I wake short of them. I counted the sleepers in the long room instead, by the stones, standing in the door. Forty of them, the children with the rest. Each takes its breath at the same time as the others and lets it go at the same time, and the long room breathes once where it should breathe forty times. I counted the room as one sleeper, since that is what it gave me. 655.

**Measure 413.** Count 580. Breaths 640. Rang.

The second clause came up whole on the Measure-night. In the long glass the bodies on the Steps face the town, all of them, on every tread. They turn the finger's width with the beat and come back to us.

**Measure 415.** Count 603. Breaths 628. Did not ring.

A keeper sets down why he did not ring. ~~The tallow is poor this year.~~ The candle burns true. I have no reason.

**Measure 416.** Count 611. Breaths 622.

I have laid these against the boatman's leaf from Measure 403, twice, by the lamp and again at grey light. I set the two side by side here and write nothing between them.

**Measure 417, the night of the Measure.** There was no procession. The Elder went down the ladder alone with the cup and the Words, and did not come up, and the line on the stilt where she went in is wet to my knee. After, the Words came up through the floor whole, all three clauses, and then a fourth. There is no fourth clause said aloud in the Rule. I did not know the sound of it until tonight, and when I heard it I knew it.

**Measure 417, after.** Count 618. Breaths 618.

I counted it over three nights. The stone goes into the bowl as I let my breath go, each time, and I can no longer tell which of the two I am counting. The long room breathes with me. I can hear it through the wall.

**Measure 417, the ninth night after.** Count 618. Breaths 618.

The count has not moved in nine nights. The office is to ring the town's warning when the beat changes, and the beat does not change. I have let the rope go.
