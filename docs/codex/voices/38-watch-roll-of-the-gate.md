# The Watch-Roll of the Last Vigil at the Gate

*Three leaves of hide sewn into a lamp-hood, taken from the gate-post at the head of the Descent by a carter who would carry oil no farther; he sold me the hood and kept the lamp, because, he said, it was still lit.*

The Rule of the Vigil, as every captain copies it at the head of the roll:

*Keep the lamps. Keep the gate. Go down only on an errand. After every word spoken below, say three names to the lamp, and count how many come. Three: go on. Two: stand. One: come up. None: come up and say nothing. More than three: put out the lamp and come up by the cord in the dark. Enter all who go down. Enter all who come up. Enter what they carry. Burn nothing they carry until the captain has seen it.*

The watch at this roll, lamp by lamp, as they hang on the post:

The first lamp, Hollin. The second, Ysmay Cord-Knotter. The third, Brannoc. The fourth, Edda of the Two Cords. The fifth, Corwen. The sixth, Tamsy, who is young. The seventh, Old Ruel, who has gone down more than any of us and carries no cord. The eighth is mine.

The gate draws. Every roll in the box begins with that. The air of the Descent goes down at all hours, the flames lean in toward the dark, and the ash on the threshold lies in drifts with their points downward. Every captain has entered the lean of the flame each morning, a finger, two fingers, and I have read them all, and none is under a finger.

**The first day of my roll.** Hollin down, to trim the far lamps at the first valve. The valve opens on the draw and shuts on the rest. Came up the second day. Carried: the stubs of nine wicks, which I burned. Said: the valve was warm to the cheek. Lean of the flame: two fingers in.

**The fourth day.** Ysmay down, with oil for the post at the third valve. Came up the sixth. Carried: her cord, every knot of it untied and tied again, the knots facing the other way along the cord. She says she tied none of them. Said: past the third valve the walls begin to fold, close and many as the inside of a head is folded, and dry, and in the grooves of them is writing she could not keep her eye on. Counted at every word: three. Lean: two fingers in.

**The ninth day.** Brannoc down, on the errand of the Vigil, which is to stand at the temple step and listen, and come up and say whether it still thinks. Came up the fourteenth day. Carried: a piece of glass the size of a palm, warm, with a hollow worn in it as for a thumb. There is no fire in the Descent to make glass. It is in the box.

Said: it still thinks.

I asked what it thinks of. He said a word, and said three names to his lamp after it, as the Rule is, and counted none, and smiled. He has said no word since that we can make out. He sits with his thumb in the glass's hollow. Lean: one finger in.

**The fifteenth day.** A party of the Pale Order at the gate, three brothers and a Warden, who would go down and count the folds. I told them what the Rule tells the Order: the deep is counted already, and by something that keeps its count backward. I gave them a lamp and no cord. Two have come up. The Warden came up holding his tally-stick by its other end and asked me which of us was the captain.

**The eighteenth day.** Two monks of the fallen Peak with their bowls, asking names at the gate. Ruel gave his. I forbade the rest. The Peak says a name in the bowl is laid in the quiet between words. The Vigil holds that the quiet in the deep is full, and what is laid in it is thought about. Ruel went down that night with no errand I gave him. Not entered as gone down. Entered here.

**The twentieth day.** Edda down after Ruel, with both her cords. Came up the twenty-sixth, by one cord. The other she tied off at the fifth valve, she says, to a hand that held it. Carried: a little dust in her sleeve, fine and grey. No bone in it, no grit. The Order's brothers say the things that came in from outside, before the breath, leave no body, only a little glass and a little dust. I burned the dust, and it went up straight and did not lean.

Said: the temple is many doors on one room. The thought is in the room. She says it is like a word you have been about to say all your life. Some of her words are gone from her mouth, and she can feel them being thought of below, one after another, as a man goes through a box. Counted at every word: three, three, three, then four.

**The twenty-seventh day.** Corwen down for Edda's tied cord. Came up the thirty-first. Carried: the cord, tied to nothing. Said nothing for a day. Then said my mother's name, which is not entered on any roll and which I have told no one at this gate, and said three names after it, and counted, and looked at me, and would not say the number.

**The thirty-third day.** Ruel up. No lamp. He came up the road walking backward, face to the deep, as a man walks out of a lord's hall. He knows us and calls us each by the name of the lamp before ours. He says the bowl-names are kept very well below. His hands are clean and he has been nine days in the folds. I have put him in the gate-hut with Brannoc. They sit facing the gate. When one speaks, the other says three names.

**The thirty-sixth day.** Tamsy asks to go down. I have refused her. A purifier of the Myriad came up the road two days since, stood at the threshold, would not breathe, and went away grey. She knew something at the gate that I did not.

**The fortieth day.** Lean of the flame: none. The flames stand straight. The ash lies flat.

**The forty-first day.** Lean of the flame: a finger, out.

Every flame on the post leans toward me, away from the dark, and the drifts on the threshold have turned their points up the road. The gate breathes out. It is warm, and dry, and it smells of lamp-oil, which is ours, carried down by the Vigil since the first roll, given back. No captain in the box has entered this.

The Citadel's readers say the grooves below tell that the world is a thought with no one thinking it, forgetting itself one thing at a time, and that the end is rest. The Citadel's readers do not finish. The Vigil holds that something is thinking it, down there, in the room with many doors, and that it has done forgetting, and has begun on the other way: it is remembering, one thing at a time, and what it remembers it takes back up out of us. Brannoc's word. Edda's words. My mother's name. The draw of the gate.

**The forty-third day.** Lean: two fingers, out. Hollin, Ysmay, Corwen and Tamsy at the post. Edda in the hut with the others now. Three facing the gate.

I am going down on the errand of the Vigil, to stand at the step and say whether it still thinks. I leave the roll on the post. After this word I will say three names to my lamp.

I have said them. I count
