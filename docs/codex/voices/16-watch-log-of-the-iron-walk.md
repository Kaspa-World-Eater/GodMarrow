# The Watch-Log of the Tower on the White Pan

*The last leaves of a log on iron-bound boards, found on the top floor of a tower on the far waste with salt drifted up its stair to the third turn. I cut the leaves and left the book, because its clasp had grown into the floor.*

The first leaf, in the first hand, as every warden copies it at the head of their watch:

*Khar-Dhuum Vesk. The Siege That Did Not End. Mark where it walks. Do not let it cross. Send word to the Seat if it crosses.*

Word has gone nine times in my watch, by the oil-carter, who has stopped coming. No word has come back. The last that ever came is pinned inside the cover in a hand no warden knows, and says: *Keep the count.* It is dated by a reckoning I cannot follow. I keep the count.

**The four thousand one hundred and eightieth day of my watch.** Bearing: the fifth merlon, a thumb's width left of the cut. Distance: the tread heard at thirty-one after the foot came down. Weather: dry; salt moving low across the pan from the east. Seen: lamps in the upper windows of the north quarter, eleven, in the same windows as the forty nights before. Whoever lights them keeps a round.

**The one hundred and eighty-first.** Bearing: the fifth merlon, a thumb and a half left. Distance: thirty-one. Weather: the same. Seen: in the long glass, the street that runs down from the north gate, which the old wardens call the Stair Street. The iron people stand in it as they stood: a woman with a bundle at her hip, two men carrying a beam between them, a child on the steps with one foot raised. They are the colour of the walls.

I checked the child against my mother, warden before me, in her second year: *Child on the Stair Street, the raised foot a hand above the step.* I make it three fingers. I enter it and say nothing more.

**The one hundred and ninetieth.** Bearing: the fifth merlon, the cut. Distance: twenty-nine. Weather: wind; the glass cleaned twice. Seen: the wall-walker on the high wall over the south gate, an iron figure with a long glass at its eye. Every warden has entered him. He looks west, away from this tower, out over the waste where there is nothing: salt, and the light going down into it.

Checked against Warden Hesk, as near as I can reckon ninety years before my count: *In to twenty-nine at the fifth merlon. Turns at the salt-cairn. The wall-walker has his glass on the far side.* It has never come nearer than twenty-nine, which is the width of the pan.

**The two hundred and third.** I have done a thing no warden has written of doing. Over forty nights I laid every bearing in this log on the floor of the top room in chalk, one mark to an entry, from the first leaf to this, each mark set out from the stair-post a finger for every ten of the count.

The marks make no ring. A ring is what I expected, because a besieger walks round what it besieges. They make one line, a little bent, running north and south across the floor from wall to wall, worn deepest at the ends where it turns. The stair-post stands a thumb's width to the east of the line, at its middle.

It walks a line. The salt-cairn is its north turning. Its south turning no warden has named. The chart of the Seat pinned by the door shows the pan white and empty, with no line on it, and the tower drawn a little too large.

**The two hundred and fourth.** Weather: still. Seen: nothing new. I enter what I found on the merlons. Each has its sighting-cut, and each cut has the old rings scratched beside it, one for each ten of the count, as the glass is marked. The rings on every merlon run to ninety. The log has never entered a distance past sixty. The cuts were made to sight on something three times farther off than the citadel has ever walked. They were made to look past it.

The south gate faces west in every entry. The wall-walker's glass is on the far side in every entry. The citadel has had its back to this tower for as long as there have been wardens. We have looked where it looks, over its shoulder, and written it down, and called it the thing we watched.

The carter's people say the ground everywhere is a body, and that it is warm, and that the citadel was a god's tooth. They are ignorant. The pan is salt and it is cold. I am on the citadel's side of its line. So was every warden before me. The Charge says do not let it cross. It does not say who else is not to cross.

**The two hundred and eleventh.** Bearing: the fifth merlon. Distance: twenty-nine. Weather: dry. Seen: the wall-walker has lowered his glass. It hangs at his side. My mother's leaves, and Hesk's, and the first hand's, have him with the glass raised. In no entry is it down.

**The two hundred and twelfth.** The lamps in the north quarter went out one at a time from the west window to the east, between the second and third hours. I counted eleven. Then a twelfth, in a window I have no mark for.

**The two hundred and fifteenth.** Bearing: the fifth merlon, then the sixth, then the fifth. Distance: twenty-nine. It has stopped at the middle of its line, across from this tower, and stood there two days. It has never stopped at the middle. It turns at the ends. The tread comes anyway, at the count, though no foot is lifted that I can see.

**The two hundred and seventeenth.** It has turned. The south gate faces east. I have the gate in the glass, open, and the street beyond it running straight to me. The wall-walker has his glass raised again. It is on this tower.

Mark where it walks. It walks the line, as it always has, with its face to the far side; and the far side is this side now. Nothing has come across the pan in my watch. Nothing has gone out from it but the carter, and he stopped coming. I do not know what is left behind me to the east for it to keep. I think it has looked, and found nothing there to keep, and turned to face what remains.

**The two hundred and twentieth.** Bearing: the fifth merlon. Distance: twenty-four. Weather: still; salt standing in the air. Seen: the child on the Stair Street has the foot two fingers above the step. I write it down because I was charged to.

The glass is heavy in the mornings. The two fingers I sight with do not open readily until noon, and they are cold, and the cold has a grain to it.

**The two hundred and twenty-third.** Bearing: the fifth merlon, the cut, square. Distance: seventeen. Weather: none. Seen: the woman with the bundle has turned her head. Every warden has her looking down at it. She is looking down the Stair Street, out of the gate, at this tower. All of them in the street are. The men have set down the beam.

I have sent no word. There is no one to carry it, and I believe the Seat had this leaf written before I was born.
