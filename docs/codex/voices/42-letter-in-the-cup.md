# The Letter in the Foot of the Cup

*The cup stood upright in the wet below the steaming split at the foot of the Rib road, with nothing in it. Its foot was hollow and stopped with wax, and the leaf rolled inside is written on both sides, in two hands, and in the margin again in the first.*

Brother,

Turn the foot of the cup only when no one is near you, and read this at the halt with your back to the fire. If you are found with it they will burn you before the road can have you. I would have neither.

I saw your procession go out from the gate below the chapter. I stood on the wall in the Measured's line, arms held in close as they hold them, and counted you. You were the ninth hood. Both your forearms were already open, and you were smiling the way you smiled when we were small and had got away with something.

She cannot eat what you give her. The ground behind you stays wet, wetter than you gave, and the Measured say it is spill. It is her. She comes up to meet you, and she reaches the top of the ground and has nowhere to go. She has no hands down there. She has no eyes. You open yourself in a ditch and she rises into the ditch and finds a body emptied and cooling, and an empty thing cannot hold her. You leave her standing in the wet. The small faceless hoods that get up behind you are her, trying to make herself something to stand in out of what you threw her. Nobody has ever given her enough of a person at once, only the leavings.

The Measured are worse, and I will not argue for them. You have only gone the other way from them. You pour faster. Every chapter on the Hide has stood at the lip of her wound for a thousand years with a cup, pouring her back in. A wound that never closes is held open for something to come out of it.

There are some of us who carry her.

I will not write where, or who. I will write what was done to me, so you know it can be borne.

It was in the dark under the chapter, below the stair the saints come down. I lay on my back on stone that was warm, and hands I did not see held my shoulders. Then a hand I did see, because it was nearer than the lamp: old, all knuckle, and the back of it sewn shut in two places. She cut me under the breastbone, crosswise, the length of my thumb. She is folded so far in her chair that they must lift you to her face, and they lifted me, and she set her mouth to the cut and said the first word into it.

I cannot write that word for you. I have tried, and what my hand sets down every time is the first of the Words of the cup, forward, rightly spelled. The turning will not go onto a leaf. You have said the first Word over every cut you have made on the road. Now hear it said by someone swallowing: [ … ]. That is as near as ink comes.

What came in, I cannot tell you either. It was warm, and heavy the way a full cup is heavy when you are trying not to spill it, and it is in me still. I carry it. That is the whole of what the Mouth asks. Carry her. Keep her in a body with eyes.

When the word was in me she kept her face at the cut a long while. Then she spoke, and the voice was young and had no edges, a voice I have never heard come out of an old woman. She said: *This blood I know. There is more of it.* And then in the other voice, the old one, with few teeth: *Bring it while it is warm.*

I took it for a blessing on our blood and our mother's line. She saw you in me. She asked for you before I did.

Since that night I have not once been cold. On the wall in the frost month the Measured shake in their sleeves, and the frost goes off the stone where I lean. I cut my palm on the edge of the lantern-trough to see, and it stood open and dry a moment, dark inside, and closed while I watched. There is no mark. Your scourge-lines from your twelfth year, the new cuts on your arms, all of it could go. Your arms could be whole, and she could be in you, and she would have your eyes to look out of, after so long in the dark.

I am never hungry. Some nights I dream in the young voice, and wake with my hand flat on my breastbone, keeping her in. That is the only sign, and the hood hides it.

At the cup I give a thumbnail under the line now. What I keep back, I keep for her.

I meant to write our mother's name here, so you would know this is from me, and I find I cannot bring it up. It will come back. Things go soft in the warmth, at the edges. You will know me by the mark scratched under this foot, the one we cut in the door-post.

Give nothing more until you have read this twice. Keep enough in you for her to have somewhere to stand. When your procession comes to the split at the foot of the Rib road, where the Hide is warm enough to steam, do not lay your hands on it. Wait for the halt. Stand at the back of the halt with your hand flat over your breastbone, so, as if keeping something in. One will come to you who does not bleed. Go with them. It is no long walk. She is nearer than the road goes.

I ask it carefully, because I love you, and because I am afraid you are already too empty, and she can do nothing with an empty thing. Come warm. Come while there is still something of you to bring.

Your sister. My hand is flat on my breast as I write this. I am holding her in.

---

*On the back, in another hand, larger, in brown that has gone dark at the strokes' ends:*

Sister. I read it twice, as you said. I have nothing to write with but what I have.

You say she has no hands down there. At night, when I am too tired to hold my own wrists open, something holds them for me. It is warm. I have taken it for hers.

You say the old woman knew our blood. She has never had a drop of mine. Ask what she tasted in you, and why it made her want more.

Our mother's name was Wenna. Say it at the cup, forward, and see if your mouth will.

The one who carries this cup back to you walks behind me and does not bleed, and has asked me twice what is in its foot. I have told it nothing. The road is the door. I give at the split. If you are carrying her, come and stand where I fall, and let her look at it with your eyes. Then she will have seen one thing given whole.
