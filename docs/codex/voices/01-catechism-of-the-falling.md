# The Questions of the Falling, as Put on the Fourth Stair

*Cut from inside the yoke-strap of a novice's carrying-frame at Nine Stairs, where it had been sewn flat under the dead teacher's knees. Two of its answers have been scraped, and one of those written over in a hand pressed so hard the leaf is cut through at the strokes.*

Questions to be had by heart before the cord. The novice stands on the Fourth Stair facing up, with the teacher on the yoke facing down, and the tallier puts the questions from the step above. A novice who misses one begins again at the first.

Set down for the Fourth Stair by the tallier entered in the book as Three Hundred and Sixty, root nine, who has no other name.

**I. What was before the Count?**

The novice lays his thumb across his own mouth and keeps it there until the tallier has counted one breath. That is the whole answer.

**II. What was the first thing?**

The Falling.

**III. Who fell?**

The Bearing Mother, Oss-Vharoth, whose old name is said once in the questions, and this is the once. She fell, and her frame came to rest, and where it came to rest is the world.

**IV. From where did she fall?**

That is a question of before the Count. The novice gives the first answer.

**V. Who stood beside her?**

The first brothers. They were there when she came to rest. They did not catch her; a brother does not catch. They stood until she was still, and then they went among her bones and laid each one where it lay, and gave each its number. The first number was one, and the root of one is one, and that was the first night.

**VI. Was the world made?**

The world was set down. A made thing has a maker, who may come back for it. A thing set down has a bearer, and she is under us still.

**VII. Why is the ground warm?**

Because she is still bearing it. The Hide lies on her as a load lies on a back. The warmth is the effort.

**VIII. What is the Count?**

Every bone of hers, told by number from the first laying. It is kept on the Ninth Stair of every ossuary, and in the heads of the talliers, who carry the open figure the way a full bowl is carried, without spilling and without drinking. Every frame laid in a niche is entered in the Count, because every frame is hers again when it is laid.

**IX. Why does the Order keep numbers and not names?**

A name is said, and goes out on the breath that said it. A number is laid. A number outlasts a name as bone outlasts flesh.

**X. What does the cord hold?**

Two hundred and six knots, one for each bone of a whole frame. The novice tells his own frame on it each night, from the crown down to the last small bone of the left foot, so that when he is laid he is already counted.

**XI. Does the Count ever go down?**

It does not. At every Feast of the Laying every wall is read, and at every Feast the Count stands higher than at the Feast before. This is the Mother's gift.

**XII. Why does the Count rise in a year when no frame was laid?**

[Scraped to the skin. One stroke is left at the head of the scraping, which may be the beginning of a one or of anything. Beneath, in the copyist's hand:] The novice says: it is the gift. He does not put the question back.

**XIII. Who cut the halls beneath the lowest stair?**

This question is not put. A novice who puts it is given the first answer, and is set to sweep the Second Stair until he has forgotten that he asked.

**XIV. What is the Last Number?**

The number at which the Count closes. Its root is nine. No brother living knows it. Each tallier carries the open figure into his niche at the end, and the next takes it up from the book, so that it is never set down between them.

**XV. What is a stopped count?**

A count left open because the counter died or tired. A stopped count is a name: it is said and it goes. A closed count is a figure, and stays.

**XVI. How is the Count to be closed?**

[Here the first answer has been scraped and the place written over, small and close. Of the first answer these words can still be read under the new: *by waiting … the dead come in at their time … the Order does not hasten the Mother … laid before its time is laid crooked.*]

By counting. Every frame on the Hide is hers, the walking with the laid. A frame that walks is a bone she lent and has not had back. The Order waits on the lowlands to die in their own time, and the lowlands take their time and spend it on the wind, in flesh and breath and names. Waiting is a stopped count with a robe on it. Let the novice learn to count at the road. Let him count the living by the frame as they come over the ford, and set the figure under the line in the book, open, and carry it. When a wall wants a frame to close it, let the frame be brought home and laid, finished or no. It was finished at the Falling. The knights of the Kostyak know it. The Wardens know it after the lamp-hour. I have counted the living of Saltbridge at the ford as they came over: three hundred and eighty-seven, which is root nine already, and wants nothing but laying.

**XVII. What is the Setting Down?**

When the Count closes at the Last Number, the Mother lays the world down at last. Everything finished stays with her: laid bone, closed counts, dust. Everything unfinished is given to the wind: flesh still growing, breath still leaving, every name. The sky, which is the Order's dust, comes down and lies over her face as a cloth lies over a face.

**XVIII. Where is the novice at the Setting Down?**

If he is laid, he is with her, counted. If he is not laid, he is unfinished, and he goes on the wind with the names.

**XIX. What of the other ends that are told?**

They are named here so that the novice may know them when he hears them, and curse them.

That the sky shuts like a mouth on the world, as the bowl-carriers of the fallen mountain say on the roads: false. The sky is our dust, and dust has no lips.

That the flesh will fill every hollow, as the hooded bleeders say over their cups: false and wicked. A niche is hollow by right. A frame is hollow by right.

That a still winter is coming, when the dead stand at their own doors asking which way, as the glass-readers say: false. The dead do not ask. They face the stair.

That the breath goes out of the world and somebody breathes it back, as the shrine-keepers say: false. Breath is the unfinished part, and goes on the wind with the rest.

That the dead walk because they are counted, as the tally-breakers of the lowlands say, unmaking our figures at night: false, and the tally-breaker is to be counted first.

**XX. What does the novice say when he is given the cord?**

I am two hundred and six. Count me.
