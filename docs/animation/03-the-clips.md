# The clips

What every clip a character has must do, from Lasseter's principles (`01-principles.md`). The Ossuarch's clips today
(`art/sprites/ossuarch_hd.json`): `idle`, `walk`, `atk`, `atk2`, `wind` (the charge's wind-up), `cast`, `dodge`, `hit`,
`death`, each in eight views. Monsters have the same set or fewer.

## Idle: the moving hold

Lasseter: "as soon as you go into a held pose, the action dies immediately ... Even the slightest movement will keep
your character alive."
- **Breath** moves the chest and shoulders, slowly, on the character's own rhythm (the Ossuarch slow and even; a
  Shrine Keeper barely, because she is holding a breath).
- **Secondary parts drift:** the plume, the cloth, the chain, the lantern's swing, each at its own rate, never in step.
- **No twins:** the weight sits on one leg; the arms hang differently.
- **The thought shows:** once in a long while, a small action that comes from the mind: the Ossuarch's head turns to
  count something; a monster's head lifts to scent the air. The head leads; the body follows a few frames later.
- **Never a loop the eye can count.** Long holds with drifting parts, and the small action rare and varied.

## Walk and run

- **The hips lead** ("in walking, the action starts with the hips"), then the chest counter-turns, then the arms swing
  opposite the legs; the head bobs on an arc, highest on the passing pose.
- **Contact, down, passing, up:** the four poses of the step, held by the sheet (the down pose longer for a heavy
  walker), never evenly spaced.
- **The feet are locked** while they bear weight: no sliding, no sinking (lock the contacts as Lasseter locked Jr.'s
  base).
- **Weight by speed and mind:** the pilgrim's walk is purposeful and a little heavy (armour, the lantern); a Hollow's
  walk has no lead and no purpose; a beast-sized thing walks slow and long in the stride.
- **The lantern swings** behind the walk and settles after the stop.
- **Stopping** overlaps into the idle: the body stops, the cloth and plume carry on and settle.

## Attack: anticipation, action, follow-through

Lasseter's three parts, on a timing sheet (`02-method.md`):
1. **The anticipation:** the weight goes back, the weapon rises, the head turns to the target first. Long enough to
   read; the bigger and heavier the blow, the longer and deeper.
2. **The action:** two or three frames at most. The fastest frame is stretched or smeared so it does not strobe. The
   blade travels on an arc ("the faster the action, the flatter the arc").
3. **The contact:** hit-stop on impact (a few frames' freeze for both), sparks or bone chips by material.
4. **The follow-through:** the weapon carries past the line; the body is pulled after it by the weapon's weight; the
   cloth and plume arrive last.
5. **The settle:** back toward guard, overlapping into the next clip.

**The second attack (`atk2`)** is a different idea, not a variant: a thrust if the first is a cut, a shield-bash if
the first is a blade.

**The charged attack (`wind`, then the release):** Derek's Secret of Mana charge is anticipation as a mechanic. The
wind-up is a held coil that deepens with the charge (moving hold: it trembles, the plume shakes, the breath shows); the
release is the biggest, fastest, most exaggerated action the character has.

## Cast

- **The thought comes first:** the head and the hands lead; the casting is a decision before it is a gesture.
- **The anticipation** gathers: the Ossuarch's bone comes to his hand from the ground; the Shrine Keeper draws a breath
  and holds it; the Hollow Mystic's threads tighten.
- **The release** is quick and clear in silhouette, aimed: the arm or fan points where the spell goes, in every view.
- **Each calling casts like itself.** Its faith is in its hands: the Ossuarch exact and counting, the Penitent with the
  body (the cost is life), the Shrine Keeper by breath and fan, the Empty Hand by stillness and then one movement.

## Dodge

- **A short, sharp anticipation** (a dip), then the body moves first with the head following, then a landing that
  squashes at the joints, and a quick recovery.
- **The cloth and plume** lag behind the body through the whole dodge.

## Hit: the external force

Lasseter: "The only time that the eyes or head would not lead the action would be when an external force is driving the
character's movements ... the force of the impact would cause the body to move first, snapping the head back and
dragging it behind."
- **The body is struck first;** the head snaps back a frame later; the arms and cloth fly last.
- **Short and sharp:** the hit is the one clip that can be fast everywhere, with a held first frame of impact.
- **By direction:** struck from the front, the body folds back; from behind, forward. A big hit is a different clip
  (knocked down) from a small one (flinch).
- **The whole body moves.** A flinch of the head alone reads as nothing at our size.

## Death: weight and finality

- **The fall is the character's weight:** a heavy armoured body goes down fast and lands hard with no bounce (Luxo's
  "thud"); a light, hollow thing crumples slowly; a bloated thing sags.
- **Overlap to the end:** the body lands, the weapon lands a beat later, the cloth settles last; the head is the last
  thing to stop.
- **The final pose holds,** and the corpse stays: it is a moving hold for a frame or two (a last settle), then still.
- **Pathos** (Frank Thomas: "When computer animation can create pathos"): a death can be sad. Not every death; the
  bosses' and the named dead's.

## Monsters

- **The eyes identify them** (Derek's rule): monsters with eyes lead every action with the eyes, a few frames before
  the head; eyeless ones lead with the head, and more, because "if the character has no eyes ... it is even more
  important to lead with the head".
- **Each kind thinks one thing** and moves by it: the Tithe-Hand wants (it reaches before it moves), the Hollow has no
  intent (no lead at all; it drifts and then lunges), the bone things are exact, the flesh things swell and sag.
- **Telegraphs are the game.** Every monster attack has a readable anticipation; elites shorten it a little;
  champions add a feint.
- **A pack never moves in step:** timing offsets per creature.

## Bosses

- **The entrance is staged** (the D2 lessons: great entrances). The room goes still, one idea at a time: the boss's
  arrival is the only thing moving.
- **Scale by timing:** big things move slowly, start late and stop late; their wind-ups are long, huge and clear.
- **The tell** is exaggerated past anything a normal monster does, and the boss's light and the room react to it.

## The Stranger

Old, amused, a little mad: he is still for a long time, and then makes one quick gesture (a card turned, a hand lifted
to the great face). His idle is a moving hold: the cards in his fingers, the breath in his shoulders, the head that
turns a few frames before anything else when something catches his interest. He does not look up when the pilgrim
arrives; his head comes up a beat after the player expects it. (His full-screen Reading animation is Derek's own, and
it stands.)
