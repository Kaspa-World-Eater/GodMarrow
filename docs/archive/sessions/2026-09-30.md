## Claude · 2026-09-30 22:46

Here's what I took from it, in order of how much it matters for Godmarrow:

1. **A game can look 2D and be 3D underneath.** Diablo II Resurrected keeps a real 3D world and flattens it through one fixed camera. The "isometric look" is a camera setting, not a limit of the art.

2. **The camera is just a few numbers.** Position, zoom, tilt and turn sit in two small tables of numbers. Change them and the same world becomes a Warcraft view or a first-person view with no new art. With a real 3D world, camera moves (a slow push-in at a boss, a tilt when a great one wakes) cost almost nothing.

3. **Diablo II's angle is 30°, not true isometric (35.26°).** 30° makes a floor tile exactly twice as wide as it is tall, which keeps pixel art clean. If we ever build 3D underneath, 30° is the angle to copy.

4. **No-perspective drawing is why his first attempt felt wrong.** D2 draws with no perspective, so far things stay the same size. That's right for the top-down view and looks like a telephoto lens from any other angle. A top-down game should keep it; perspective only belongs when the camera comes down low.

5. **Art built for one angle breaks when you look from another.** Up close, D2R's enemies looked uncanny, and things vanished as the camera tilted because the engine only loads what the normal view shows. If we go 3D underneath, the camera should stay near our angle, and our 2D paintings must never be seen side-on.

6. **Clicking has to follow the camera.** Once the camera changed, his clicks landed in the wrong place. Aiming in a 3D-underneath game has to be worked out from the camera, not screen pixels.

7. **Lighting is the real prize, and this is my inference, not the video's.** Our worst problems today are fake light and shadow: painted shadow wedges, and darkness laid over the picture like a sheet. In a 3D world the lantern would be a real light, and walls and trunks would block it properly. Night would be actual darkness with the lantern pushing it back.

The main point is that the look and the thing underneath can be separate choices. We can keep your pixel art exactly as it is on screen and still have real 3D light, shadows and camera beneath it.
